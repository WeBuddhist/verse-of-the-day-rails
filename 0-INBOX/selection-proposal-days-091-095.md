## Day 91 — Toh 205, the beggar woman's lamp · **the strongest card in the batch**

- **Canon:** Tibetan Kangyur (Mahāyāna) — *The City Beggar Woman* (Nagaropajīvinī). Fresh sūtra, never used.
- **Source:** `bo-toh205.md#^p188`
- **Speaker:** the Buddha's own verses, frame immediately before: `བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་ཀྱིས་དེའི་ཚེ་ཚིགས་སུ་བཅད་པ་འདི་དག་བཀའ་སྩལ་ཏོ།` ("Then the Blessed One spoke these verses"). Not the woman's words — her aspiration is separate prose earlier.
- **Verbatim Tibetan:**

> གྲོང་ཁྱེར་འཚོ་བའི་འབྲུ་མར་སྲན་མ་གྲེའུ་སྦྱར་སྦར་བ་ཡིས། །འཛམ་གླིང་སྣང་བས་ཁྱབ་གྱུར་དེ་ནི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་ཀྱི་མཐུས།

- **Proposed English (116 chars):**

> A beggar woman lit a lamp with a bean's worth of oil, and by the power of the awakening mind its light filled the world.

- **Why this one:** it is a story, not an instruction — which is exactly what the last two rejections were asking for. The image is a poor woman and a single lamp; the surrounding sūtra is about her one lamp outshining a king's thousand. Concrete, warm, and it says something to anyone who feels their contribution is too small to matter. Theme: **power-of-a-small-offering** (new tag). Speaks to: discouraged, hopeful, grateful.
- **Note on the quote:** the stanza's four long lines are two independent sentences — lines 1–2 are King Prasenajit's thousand lamps, lines 3–4 are the woman's one. This is the whole of the second sentence, nothing stitched. `བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས` ships as "the awakening mind" per the termbase, never "Great Vehicle mind."
- **Dedupe:** ✓ Toh 205 absent from log, log-history, previously-used, rails, day cards.

## Day 92 — Iti 86, peace in any posture

- **Canon:** Pali (Itivuttaka). Pali has had no card since Day 84.
- **Source:** `pi-itivuttaka.md#^iti86-4-1`–`^iti86-4-4`; Sujato at the same anchors (CC0, direct grounding — not a parallel).
- **Verbatim Pali:** *Caraṁ vā yadi vā tiṭṭhaṁ, nisinno uda vā sayaṁ; Ajjhattaṁ samayaṁ cittaṁ, santimevādhigacchatī.*
- **Sujato:** "Whether walking or standing, sitting or lying down, with mind collected inside, they attain only peace."
- **Proposed English (100 chars):**

> Whether walking or standing, sitting or lying down, with the mind settled within, you come to peace.

- **Why this one:** the four postures are not four claims — they are one image meaning *wherever you happen to be*. Nothing technical, nothing to look up, and it gives the reader something they can do in the next minute. Theme: **inner-peace** (last ran Day 67). Speaks to: restless, overwhelmed, equanimity.
- **Note:** this refrain also appears at Iti 110 and Iti 111, both embedded in much longer prose. Iti 86 is the clean standalone occurrence, which is why it's the anchor.
- **Dedupe:** ✓ Iti 86 never used. The vault holds Iti 22, 26, 27 only.

## Day 93 — EĀ 卷11, remembering a small kindness

- **Canon:** Chinese Āgama (Ekottarika). Fresh — the log holds 卷5, 6, 7, 23, 38.
- **Source:** `zh-ekottarika-agama.md#^pT02p0600a0602`
- **Speaker:** `爾時，世尊告諸比丘` — the Buddha addressing the monks directly.
- **Verbatim Chinese:**

> 若有眾生知反復者，此人可敬，小恩尚不忘，何況大恩！

- **Proposed English (116 chars):**

> Anyone who repays kindness is worthy of respect: if even a small kindness is not forgotten, how much more a great one.

- **Why this one:** one idea — gratitude that doesn't need the favour to be large. Warm, no jargon, and it praises rather than instructs. Theme: **gratitude** (last ran Day 41). Speaks to: grateful, lonely, hopeful.
- **Note:** the source continues into a long passage about the ungrateful (`諸有眾生不知反復者…`). The quote stops before it, deliberately — the negative mirror is what would make this card serious, and it is not included.
- **`zh` is a modern rendering.** Source is Chinese, so per the standing rule the shipped `zh` is modern Traditional, not the classical text; the verbatim goes in the `## Source` block.
- **Dedupe:** ✓ absent from log, rails, days.

## Day 94 — Toh 329, what you gave is what you kept

- **Canon:** Tibetan Kangyur — *The Devatā Sūtra*. Fresh sūtra.
- **Source:** `bo-toh329.md#^p516`
- **Speaker:** the Buddha answering a devatā's question at Jetavana; `བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་ཀྱིས་བཀའ་སྩལ་པ།` stands immediately before the couplet, grep-verified contiguous.
- **Verbatim Tibetan:**

> བདག་གིས་གང་བྱིན་ཟིན་པ་ཡིན། །ཁྱིམ་དུ་གང་བཞག་འདིར་བོར་ཡིན།

- **Proposed English (93 chars):**

> Whatever you have given is what you keep. Whatever you left at home is what you leave behind.

- **Why this one:** a single paradox, and the two halves are two sides of one image rather than two claims. It turns generosity into good news instead of a duty — the opposite of Day 89's register. Theme: **true-possession** (new tag). Speaks to: grasping, hopeful, grateful.
- **⚑ Note on the quote unit, which matters given Day 87:** the full reply-stanza answers *four* separate questions (what you possess / what you leave / your armour / your weapon). Taking the whole quatrain would be exactly the four-parallel-claims shape you rejected. **The couplet is the correct unit, not a convenience.**
- **Rendering flag:** the Tibetan is generic first person (`བདག་གིས`, "whatever I have given"). Second person is much better on a card, but that is a person shift from the source and the rail will say so.
- **Dedupe:** ✓ Toh 329 never used. Note its sibling **Toh 330** ran as Day 54 (generosity) — different text, different verse, and framing this as *true-possession* keeps them apart.

## Day 95 — Toh 293, alone under a tree

- **Canon:** Tibetan Kangyur — *The Mahāsūtra The Crest Insignia (2)*. Fresh sūtra.
- **Source:** `bo-toh293.md#^p535`
- **Speaker:** the Buddha to the monks; frame `སྟོན་པས་གཞན་ཡང་འདི་སྐད་བཀའ་སྩལ་ཏོ།` ("the Teacher spoke further thus"). Śakra does speak earlier in this sūtra, but only inside a story the Buddha is telling; this stanza is the Buddha's own verse.
- **Verbatim Tibetan:**

> དགོན་པ་དང་ནི་ཤིང་དྲུང་དང་། །ཁྱིམ་སྟོང་དག་ཏུ་འཇིག་རྟེན་གཙོ། །རྣམ་པར་འདྲེན་པ་རྫོགས་སངས་རྒྱས། །རྗེས་སུ་དྲན་གྱིས་དགེ་སློང་དག

- **Proposed English (96 chars):**

> In the wilderness, under a tree, in an empty house, remember the Buddha, the guide of the world.

- **Why this one:** three plain settings and one action — one unified thought, *wherever you find yourself alone, do this*. The surrounding sūtra is explicitly about fear subsiding, so it consoles. Theme: **remember-when-alone** (new tag). Speaks to: afraid, lonely, hopeful.
- **Two rendering decisions the rail will own rather than slip through:** the closing vocative `དགེ་སློང་དག` ("monks") is dropped to keep it lay-facing and inside the budget; and the three epithets `འཇིག་རྟེན་གཙོ` / `རྣམ་པར་འདྲེན་པ` / `རྫོགས་སངས་རྒྱས` are condensed to "the Buddha, the guide of the world."
- **Dedupe:** ✓ Toh 293 never used.

## Held back, and why — so you can overrule me

- **Toh 329 `^p517`, "giving makes friends increase, loving-kindness makes enemies grow peaceful"** (79 chars) — very warm, Buddha-spoken, uses locked terms. Held back because friends and enemies are arguably two claims rather than two halves of one image, which is the exact axis Day 87 died on. You may well disagree.
- **Toh 189, "for anyone who has faith in the Buddha, awakening is not hard to reach"** (70 chars) — warm and encouraging, passes every gate, but has **no image at all**. Purely propositional.
- **Toh 303, "just as a fine vase holds precious things, ethical conduct is the ground where good qualities grow"** (99 chars) — one clean homely simile, but it is the only salvageable line in a text that is otherwise a wall of "discipline is the best ornament / the best fragrance," and may read as moralising by association.
