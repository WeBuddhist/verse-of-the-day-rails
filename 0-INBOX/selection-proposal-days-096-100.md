## What these eight were screened for

Your three corrections, now the operative screens:

1. **Self-applying** — reader knows what it says *to them*, one read, no context. And the corollary you exposed: **a vivid image is usually worse, not better.** I stopped selecting for concreteness.
2. **One idea** — nameable in five words. Multi-part only when the parts are one thing.
3. **Warm, not serious** — consoling or encouraging, never correcting.

Plus: nothing sober (three of those rejected), no glossary terms, no named person or event, no simile whose subject isn't stated inside the quote.

**Note what the eight have in common: almost none has an image.** That is deliberate. Both cards that landed (Days 92, 93) are plain direct statements, and the three that failed were the vivid ones.

## The eight

### A · Dhp 228 — nobody is wholly praised or wholly blamed · **my top pick**
- Pali, `pi-dhammapada.md#^228`, Sujato at the same anchor (CC0, direct grounding)
- Verbatim: *Na cāhu na ca bhavissati, na cetarahi vijjati; Ekantaṁ nindito poso, ekantaṁ vā pasaṁsito.*
- **English (95):** There never was, nor will there be, nor is there today, anyone wholly praised or wholly blamed.
- One idea: *no one escapes both.* Theme `praise-and-blame`. Speaks to: stung by criticism, self-conscious about what people think.
- Zero-context takeaway: nobody in history has ever been universally liked or condemned, so today's criticism isn't a verdict on me.
- ⚑ Day 18 (Dhp 81) shipped "blame and praise do not shake the wise" — different claim (that one is about the wise being unmoved; this is that nobody is exempt), 78 cards back.

### B · EĀ 卷20/21 — bitter first, sweet after · **my top Chinese pick**
- Chinese Āgama, `zh-ekottarika-agama.md#^pT02p0653b1201`
- Verbatim: 修習梵行先苦而後樂；誦習經文先苦而後樂；坐禪念定先苦而後樂；數出入息先苦而後樂
- **English (113):** Living the practice is bitter at first and sweet after; so is study, so is meditation, so is watching the breath.
- One idea: *hard at first, sweet later.* Theme `effort`. Speaks to: discouraged, restless, aspiration.
- Zero-context takeaway: the thing I keep quitting because it feels hard is *supposed* to feel hard first.
- On the one-idea screen: four clauses, but all four assert the **same** proposition — the Day 86 / Day 88 "parts are one thing" pattern, not the Day 87 failure. Spoken to the monks after King Prasenajit has already left, so no frame is inherited.

### C · Iti 76 — you become the company you keep
- Pali, `pi-itivuttaka.md#^iti76-5-1`–`^iti76-5-4`, Sujato aligned
- Verbatim: *Yādisaṁ kurute mittaṁ, yādisaṁ cūpasevati; Sa ve tādisako hoti, sahavāso hi tādiso.*
- **English (101):** You become like the friends you make and the company you keep, for that is what living together does.
- One idea: *you become your companions.* Theme `good-company`. Speaks to: drifting with a crowd, grateful for a good friend.
- ⚑ Third company-adjacent card (Days 80 spiritual-friendship, 85 joy-of-good-company) — but a distinct claim: you are *changed* by them, not cheered by them. The surrounding poem has a "the wicked lead you to hell" line that is **not** in the quote and must never be.

### D · DĀ 6 — be your own light
- Chinese Āgama, `zh-digha-agama.md#^pT01p0039a2315`, the sutta's opening words
- Verbatim: 當自熾燃，熾燃於法，勿他熾燃；當自歸依，歸依於法，勿他歸依 (vocative 汝等 dropped)
- **English (115):** Be a light to yourself, a light of the teaching, not of another; be a refuge to yourself, a refuge in the teaching.
- One idea: *be your own light and refuge.* Theme `self-reliance`. Speaks to: doubtful, discouraged, aspiration.
- ⚑ Two flags: content relative of Day 49 (self-refuge, 47 cards back, different image); and 勿他熾燃 must not be rendered so hard that it reads "don't rely on teachers or friends."

### E · Dhp 76 — the friend who tells you your faults
- Pali, `pi-dhammapada.md#^76`, opening portion, stopping at the clause boundary
- Verbatim: *Nidhīnaṁva pavattāraṁ, yaṁ passe vajjadassinaṁ; Niggayhavādiṁ medhāviṁ, tādisaṁ paṇḍitaṁ bhaje.*
- **English (105):** Keep company with one wise enough to point out your faults, as you would keep a guide to hidden treasure.
- One idea: *correction is buried treasure.* Theme `welcoming-correction` (fresh). Speaks to: smarting from being corrected, defensive.
- Zero-context takeaway: the person who tells me what I got wrong is doing me a favour.
- The one simile in the eight — but its subject is stated inside the quote, so the point lands without decoding. Dropped final couplet is only a restatement.

### F · DĀ 5 — doing good has a good result
- Chinese Āgama, `zh-digha-agama.md#^pT01p0037a1704`
- Verbatim: 夫行善法必有善報，行清白行必有白報
- **English (99):** Whoever does good is sure to have a good result; whoever lives cleanly is sure to have a clean one.
- One idea: *good deeds bring good results.* Theme `wholesome-action`. Speaks to: discouraged, doubtful, hopeful.
- **This is a find, not a pool entry.** The pool had only the *negative* twin at a different anchor ("unwholesome conduct brings unwholesome result"); the positive form sits twelve lines later in the same address and is strictly better on warmth. Pool corrected.
- ⚑ Day 30 (T210, "evil brings its own penalty, good its own blessing") makes a close claim from the self-responsibility angle, 60+ cards back.

### G · Toh 183 — give expecting nothing back
- Tibetan Kangyur (Mahāyāna), `bo-toh183.md#^p194`
- Verbatim: ལན་ལ་མི་རེ་བ་ལ་གནས་ཏེ་སྦྱིན་པ་བྱིན་ན་རྣམ་པར་སྨིན་པ་བླ་ན་མེད་པ་ཡང་དག་པར་རྫོགས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་རབ་ཏུ་འཐོབ་པར་འགྱུར་ཏེ།
- **English (91):** Give with no hope of anything in return, and what ripens is unsurpassed, perfect awakening.
- One idea: *give without expecting return.* Theme `giving-without-return`. Speaks to: grasping, aspiration, hopeful.
- ⚑ Weaker than A–F: "unsurpassed, perfect awakening" is a lofty register, and adjacent ground to Day 45's non-attached-giving.

### H · Toh 329 — merit cannot be destroyed
- Tibetan Kangyur, `bo-toh329.md#^p518`, a complete verse
- Verbatim: རླུང་དམར་གྱིས་ཀྱང་མི་ཤིགས་ཤིང་། །མེས་ཀྱང་མི་འཚིག་བསོད་ནམས་ཡིན། །ས་རྣམས་ཁེབས་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཡི། །ཆུས་ཀྱང་མི་འདྲུལ་བསོད་ནམས་སོ།
- **English (101):** Merit is not broken by wind, not burned by fire, not rotted by the water that covers the whole earth.
- One idea: *what you've done can't be lost.* Theme `merit-cannot-be-lost`. Speaks to: after a setback, fear of loss.
- ⚑ Subject named first, deliberately, so it isn't the riddle shape you rejected in Toh 329's other verse. Still the weaker of the two Tibetan options, since "merit" is the one term a reader has to already hold.

## The Tibetan problem, stated plainly

Cards that actually ran: **Pali 32 · Chinese 31 · Tibetan 27.** Tibetan is five behind and every attempt to close it this month has failed — two Udānavarga cards rejected for shape and tone, three Kangyur sūtra cards rejected for needing context.

The reason is structural. Under the self-applying screen, what works is the **direct universal aphorism**, and that is what the Dharmapada-type collections and short Nikāya statements are made of. The Kangyur Mahāyāna sūtras are mostly **narrative** — their best lines live inside scenes, which is exactly what a standalone card can't carry. G and H above are the best Tibetan candidates I have and I've ranked them last honestly.

So one of two things has to give:

- **Relax the canon-diversity rule for the daily card** — accept that the format favours the aphorism collections, let Pali and Chinese carry most days, and use the Kangyur when a genuinely direct statement turns up.
- **Or keep the balance target** and accept that Tibetan days will need either the Udānavarga (whose usable stock is now largely exhausted) or a much deeper scan for the rare direct Kangyur statement.

`selection-criteria.md` §3 currently mandates the second and it is costing most of the rejections. Your call — I'll stop re-litigating it once you say.
