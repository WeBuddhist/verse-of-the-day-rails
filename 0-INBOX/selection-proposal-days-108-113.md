## Your genre ruling did most of the work

*"The problem before was the buddha praising himself… when they are blessings and aspirations, they are good."*

That reopened a form I had been screening out, and it changed the hunt substantially. Two findings:

**The Kangyur blessing genre is real but narrowly localised.** A corpus-wide sweep of all 229 Tibetan files for `ཤོག` / `གྱུར་ཅིག` / `བདེ་ལེགས` / `བཀྲ་ཤིས` found ~45 files with aspiration verses — but almost all are spoken by **bodhisattvas, gods, kings, or the reader**, not the Buddha. Buddha-spoken benedictions cluster in exactly two places: the *anumodanā* (rejoicing-over-a-gift) blessing, and the Vaiśālī *mahāsūtra* family. Three usable candidates came out, one of them excellent.

**And the Pali second pass found the real recall bug — it wasn't the one I'd diagnosed.** I'd said the first pass missed impersonal-voice and imperative material. Partly true, but the dominant failure was **segment fragmentation**: Sujato's verse lines are 20–30 characters each, so a two-line blessing had its warm words stranded in a sub-threshold half and never surfaced. Re-joining segments into whole sentences before filtering was worth more than any widening of the content tests. Four of the six Pali candidates below are half-verses that were invisible for that reason.

### A · AN 7.62 — don't be afraid of doing good · **my top pick**
`pi-anguttara-nikaya.md#^an7-62-1-1`–`^an7-62-1-2` · Sujato: "Mendicants, don't fear good deeds. / For 'good deeds' is a term for happiness."
*Mā, bhikkhave, puññānaṁ bhāyittha. Sukhassetaṁ, bhikkhave, adhivacanaṁ yadidaṁ puññāni.*
One idea: *goodness is another name for happiness.* Theme `goodness-is-happiness`.
Zero-context takeaway: doing good isn't a duty or a cost — it *is* the pleasant thing.
The purest answer yet to the "don't make the path sound hard" screen. ⚑ The sutta then continues into the Buddha recounting his own past lives as Brahmā and Sakka; the card stops at the opening exhortation and none of that enters it.

### B · Toh 95 — a blessing on your coming and going · **the best of the blessings**
`bo-toh95.md#^p375`, complete stanza · the Buddha to the merchants Trapuṣa and Bhallika, his first lay donors, just after his awakening
ཁྱེད་ཅག་འགྲོ་ནའང་བཀྲ་ཤིས་གྱུར། །ལྡོག་པ་ན་ཡང་བཀྲ་ཤིས་ཤོག །གཉེན་ལ་ལྟ་བ་བཀྲ་ཤིས་གྱུར། །གཉེན་གྱིས་བལྟ་བའང་བཀྲ་ཤིས་ཤོག
One idea: *a blessing on your journeys.* Theme `blessing-of-coming-and-going`.
Zero-context takeaway: someone is wishing me safety leaving, safety returning, and joy in the people I'll see.
**84000 footnote check: clean, no emendation** — worth stating, given it has silently emended the Degé three times elsewhere.

### C · AN 3.155 — do good in the morning
`pi-anguttara-nikaya.md#^an3-155-1-1` · Sujato: "Mendicants, those sentient beings who do good things in the morning by way of body, speech, and mind have a good morning."
One idea: *do good early; the day goes well.* Theme `start-the-day-well`.
**Uniquely well shaped for a daily-verse product** — it is about the morning, read in the morning. ⚑ Same sutta as F; your same-block ruling means both can run, just not adjacent.

### D · AN 2.33 — what parents did for you · **reopens a dead end**
`pi-anguttara-nikaya.md#^an2-33-1-8` · Sujato: "Parents are very helpful to their children: they raise them, nurture them, and show them the world."
One idea: *parents raised us and showed us.* Theme `gratitude-to-parents`.
**⚑ This matters beyond its own merits.** The log records `gratitude-to-parents` as a **documented dead end**: both Toh 315 and Iti 106 were rejected because *"parents are Brahmā"* is their opening predicate and Brahmā won't land for this audience. **AN 2.33 carries the same teaching with no deity frame at all.** ⚑ Do not extend the quote — the neighbouring passages have the carrying-on-your-shoulders and defecation imagery.

### E · Toh 182 — may my good free others · fills a documented gap
`bo-toh182.md#^p190`, complete stanza · the Buddha, addressing "noble son", introduced `At this point the Blessed One uttered these verses`
One idea: *may my good free others.* Theme `dedication-of-merit`.
**This is the vault's only Buddha-spoken dedication-of-merit verse**, and `theme-checklist.md` lists that theme as never represented. ⚑ Two flags: arguably two ideas (may all be freed / may I bring ease and show the way), and `ཉོན་མོངས` and `མྱ་ངན་འདས` both need careful de-jargoning — the same problem Day 107's rail solved by shipping "troubled."

### F · AN 3.155 — may you and yours be well
`pi-anguttara-nikaya.md#^an3-155-6-3`–`^an3-155-6-4` · Sujato: "May you and all your relatives / be healthy and happy!"
One idea: *may you and yours be well.* Theme `blessing-for-family`.
**The single clearest beneficiary of your ruling** — the Buddha's own closing blessing to end the Auspicious Chapter, with nothing about the Buddha in it at all. The shortest card yet at 52 chars. ⚑ Minor: `arogā` is "free of illness" — a wish *for* health, not a discourse on sickness, but adjacent to the no-sober screen.

### G · AN 4.73 — speak well of people unprompted
`pi-anguttara-nikaya.md#^an4-73-6-1` · Sujato: "Furthermore, a true person speaks well of another even when not asked, let alone when asked."
One idea: *good people praise others unprompted.* Theme `speaking-well-of-others`.
Concrete and doable today. Same "a true person who…" construction as Day 41, so the register is pre-validated. ⚑ Positive limb only — the negative mirror next door reads as admonishment.

### H · Toh 312 — may your hopes ripen today
`bo-toh312.md#^p319` · the Buddha dictating the verses to Ānanda
One idea: *may your hopes ripen today.* Theme `dedication-of-merit`.
⚑ **Two real flags, and this is the riskiest of the nine.** "The good wishes of **the gods**" risks the same objection that killed Toh 315 (culture-specific deity carrying the point); and the Buddha names *his own* merit, which is a dedication rather than self-praise but is a judgement call under your ruling. ⚑ Also a trap worth recording: **Toh 312 prints the whole benediction twice** — once as the Buddha's dictation, once as Ānanda reciting it at the city gate. Only the first is buddhavacana, and most strings in that file grep to 2.

### I · AN 8.1 — loving one being is enough
`pi-anguttara-nikaya.md#^an8-1-4-1`–`^an8-1-4-2` · Sujato: "Loving just one creature with a hateless heart / makes you a true person."
One idea: *loving one being makes you good.* Theme `small-acts-of-love`.
The strongest pass on the "don't make the path sound hard" screen of anything here — it makes the bar *smaller*. ⚑ Adjacency: Day 107 was loving-kindness, so this wants distance.

## Suggested six

**A, B, C, D, G, F** — no adjacent themes, canon Pali 5 / Tibetan 1, and a good tonal spread (permission → blessing → morning → gratitude → doing → blessing).

If you want more Tibetan, swap **E** in for **G**. If you'd rather avoid two blessings in one batch, drop **F** and take **I** with distance from Day 107.

## Two notes on the state of the sources

**AN can now be marked substantially covered.** Two passes have swept 34,600 English segments and re-joined 24,199 sentence units. What's left is good lines buried as the *n*th item of sevenfold enumerations — a judgement pass, not a grep pass.

**MN should join DN as low-yield for this format.** 16,286 units yielded one honourable mention and nothing proposable. Both are narrative and doctrinal; their best lines live in scenes. **The real bottleneck now is speaker attribution, not recall** — SN 8 (Vaṅgīsa), SN 9 (forest deities), SN 10 (yakkhas), SN 11 (Sakka) collectively hold a large share of the *warmest* verse in the Nikāyas and almost none of it is buddhavacana. A third pass should build a speaker index from the framing formulae first.

**One import lead worth having:** 84000's note on Toh 312 records that this benediction exists as a **standalone Kangyur text, Toh 816, "The Verses of Well-Wishing"** — an entire text of exactly this genre, not currently in `1-SOURCES/`.
