# Day 124 — Do Not Let Your Heart Sink (Toh 176)

Rail: [[2-RAILS/Verses/toh176-do-not-lose-heart]] · own translation from the Degé Kangyur (Public Domain); 84000 English as reference only; Mahāyāna.
Short sentence, **quoted in full**. Meaning: a hand on the shoulder, with no argument attached.

> **The scene, which the card cannot carry.** Ānanda has just heard that the wisdom of the Realized Ones is inconceivable and has said that from now on he will no longer claim to be foremost in learning. This is the Buddha's reply. **So the deflation is specifically the deflation of comparison** — which is the felt state the card meets.

> **⚑ The one thing worth a real decision.** Stripped of that scene, "don't lose heart" is something anyone might say, so the *dharma, not general life advice* screen is the live question here. The rail sets out what holds it on the dharma side; a reviewer should decide whether the bare imperative is enough.

> **27 characters, deliberately.** The whole teaching is one imperative. Padding it would mean adding content the source does not have. It also leaves generous room in the other five languages, where this kind of consolation usually needs more words than English.

> **⚑ Note on bo:** the verbatim Degé sentence below is **the source**; the shipped `bo` is **modern, plain Tibetan** like every other language. **Do not "correct" it back.**

> **Output languages: six** (en · zh · bo · hi · ne · mn). Ladakhi discontinued by Evan, 2026-08-03. **Do not add an `lbj` line back.**

## QA — pre-review (against 2-RAILS/Verses/toh176-do-not-lose-heart.md)

### en
- Back-translation: n/a (anchor rendering).
- Findings: the single imperative is carried whole ✓. **`ཞུམ་པའི་སེམས་མ་སྐྱེད` ships as "Do not let your heart sink"** per the rail flag: the literal "do not give rise to a discouraged mind" is stilted, and 84000's "do not be discouraged" is flat and slightly dismissive in English — the thing you say when you are brushing past someone's feeling. **"Let your heart sink" is the English idiom for exactly this state**, it preserves `སྐྱེད་པ` ("let arise") rather than commanding the feeling away, and it keeps `སེམས` as heart. Vocative `ཀུན་དགའ་བོ` dropped so the line addresses the reader; `ཁྱོད` kept ✓. The continuation (`དེ་ཅིའི་ཕྱིར་ཞེ་ན།` and the long comparison that follows) is excluded ✓. 27 chars. No em dash ✓. No "Buddha" in the card ✓.
- Net: clean for review, with the dharma-screen question in the banner carried to the reviewer.

### zh (Traditional)
- Back-translation: "Do not let your heart sink down."
- Findings: source is Tibetan, so this is a fresh modern rendering. 心沉下去 is the natural modern Chinese for the heart sinking and keeps the same bodily image English uses ✓ — deliberately not 灰心 ("lose heart / become despondent"), which is idiomatic but names the resulting state rather than the sinking, losing `སྐྱེད་པ`'s "let it arise". 不要讓 keeps the permissive negative rather than a flat prohibition ✓. 10 chars — by far the shortest zh line on the calendar; a reviewer should confirm it does not read clipped on the card. Taiwan/HK/SG register ✓, Traditional ✓.
- Net: clean for review, with the 沉下去 / 灰心 choice flagged.

### hi
- Back-translation: "Do not let your mind sit down."
- Findings: ⚑ **मन बैठ जाना is the standing Hindi idiom for the heart sinking** and is the closest available equivalent — it is bodily in the same way the English is ✓. Deliberately not हतोत्साह न हो ("do not be discouraged"), which is Sanskritic and flat, the same objection as 84000's English. न दो keeps the permissive negative ✓. अपने मन को keeps `སེམས` as the reader's own ✓.
- Net: clean for review.

### ne

- Confidence flags: (i) ⚑ **मन बस्नु is modelled on the Hindi idiom मन बैठ जाना and may not carry in Nepali** — this is the single thing to check on this line.
