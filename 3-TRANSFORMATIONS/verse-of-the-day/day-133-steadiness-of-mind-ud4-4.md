# Day 133 — Steadiness of Mind (Ud 4.4)

Rail: [[2-RAILS/Verses/ud-4-4]] · grounded in Sujato (CC0).
One verse (a single sentence), **quoted in full**. Meaning: a mind steady enough gives craving and anger nothing to grab, so suffering has no way in.

> **The simile names its own referent.** "A mind like a rock" says what the rock is — the steady mind — so the reader never decodes it. Clears the simile screen inside the first line.

> **The rhetorical question is the whole card — keep it.** "Where could suffering reach it?" is what makes this empowering (nothing can get at you) rather than a flat instruction. Every rendering must keep the question, not turn it into a statement.

> **⚑ The two "handles" are craving and anger — keep them concrete.** `rajanīyesu` / `kopaneyye` are what would arouse craving and what would provoke anger — the matched pair of attraction and aversion. Ship "craving" and "anger," not "desirable things / annoying things," so the reader recognises their own experience.

> **⚑ The backstory (a spirit strikes Sāriputta, who barely feels it) is excluded.** The verse stands alone; do not import the striking scene.

> **Offers a way through ✓.** The only heavy word, "suffering," appears in a question about its absence. The card shows the steadiness and its result, not the trap.

> **Output languages: six** (en · zh · bo · hi · ne · mn). Ladakhi discontinued by Evan, 2026-08-03. **Do not add an `lbj` line back.**
