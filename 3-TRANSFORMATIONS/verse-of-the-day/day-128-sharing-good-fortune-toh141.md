# Day 128 — Held in Common (Toh 141)

Rail: [[2-RAILS/Verses/toh141-share-what-comes-to-you]] · own translation from the Degé Kangyur (Public Domain); 84000 English as reference only; Mahāyāna.
One aspiration of six, **quoted in full**. Meaning: whatever goes well for you was never privately owned.

> **The "I" is the reader's, not the Buddha's.** The Buddha gives the bodhisattvas this dhāraṇī to recite three times by day and three times by night; these are words he hands the reader to say. The screen added 2026-10 bars **the Buddha** speaking as "I" — it does not bar an aspiration he gives you. Precedent: Days 107 and 111.

> **⚑ A dhāraṇī text, but in the mdo section and in scope.** Toh 141 sits in the General Sūtra range (Toh 96–202), not in the `gzungs 'dus` collection and not in the tantra division. The quoted material is plain aspiration — no mantra syllables, no visualisation, no empowerment. Noted because "dhāraṇī" in a title will look like a scope failure on every future audit.

> **⚑⚑ THE WEAK CLAUSE, AND IT IS WHY THIS CANDIDATE SAT ON THE BENCH.** `ཡོངས་སུ་ཤེས་པར་བྱེད་པ` is abstract and **states no object — understanding of what?** A cultural Buddhist reader will hear "insight," but a reader could fairly ask. This is the out-of-context screen **at its edge, not over it**. Dropping the clause would be a distillation; supplying an object would be an addition. **It ships as it stands, flagged. A reviewer may call it the other way.**

> **⚑ 84000 note [n6] records that PT77 and the Sanskrit read the second clause differently**, closer to *"may my enjoyment be in common with all beings."* The rail grounds on the Degé as it stands. **Sixth recorded case of the reference witnesses diverging from the Degé** — so the clause is textually contested as well as semantically thin.

> **⚑⚑ ADJACENCY — RULE ON THIS BEFORE IT SHIPS. Day 127 is a giving card and sits immediately before this one.** The batch ends on two consecutive days in the generosity family, the pattern `card-types.md` records as the failure that produced *"all the new quotes you supplied are similar."* **Distinct:** Day 127 is material gifts and their size, a permission for someone who thinks their offering is too small; this is your own good fortune and whether it is privately owned, an aspiration for someone things are going **well** for. Opposite emotional starting points. **Against:** both are in the giving family, back to back. **Evan assigned this to Day 128 explicitly, after Day 127 was built.** If it reads wrong, the fix is to swap Days 123 and 128, which puts five days between the two generosity cards.

- Findings: the aspiration is carried whole ✓; the other five in the set excluded ✓ (taking more than one would make it a list). **First person kept** — this is a wish the reader speaks, and converting it to "may you share…" would make it a different card and a different type ✓. **"worldly happiness" for `འཇིག་རྟེན་པའི་ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པའི་བདེ་བ`**: "worldly" is deliberately kept, because the sentence is about ordinary good luck rather than spiritual attainment, and dropping it would make the aspiration grander and vaguer than it is. **"may it bring us to understanding" is the flagged clause** (see the banner) — "us" rather than "them" preserves `ཐུན་མོང་དུ` ("in common"), which is the word the card turns on, and keeps the reader inside the wish rather than above it. 105 chars. No em dash ✓. No "Buddha" ✓.
- Net: clean for review, with the weak-clause question carried to the reviewer.

### zh (Traditional)
- Back-translation: "All the worldly blessings and happiness I enjoy, may I hold in common with all beings, and may it lead us toward realization."
- Findings: source is Tibetan, so this is a fresh modern rendering. **共有 for `ཐུན་མོང་དུ`** ✓ — it says *held in common*, not 分享 ("share out"), which would make it a distribution of goods rather than a claim about ownership; this is the same distinction the English preserves. 福樂 for `ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པའི་བདེ་བ` ✓ — it carries both the good fortune and the ease. 願 twice keeps the optative ✓. **了悟 for the flagged clause** — it is the ordinary modern word for coming to understand or realize, and deliberately not 智慧 ("wisdom"), which would supply a doctrinal object the Tibetan does not state. Taiwan/HK/SG register ✓, Traditional ✓.
- Net: clean for review, with the 了悟 choice flagged alongside the English.

### bo ⚑
- Back-translation (approx, **against the verbatim source** per rule 8): "Whatever worldly happiness and abundance I obtain, may I enjoy it in common with all beings and may it come to be fully understood." The first person, the "in common," and the understanding clause all survive; the stacked verb chain is simplified (see below).
- Findings: **our own modern Tibetan rendering**, not the verbatim Degé. Substitutions: the heavy nominal chain `འཇིག་རྟེན་པའི་ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པའི་བདེ་བ` unpacked to `འཇིག་རྟེན་པའི་བདེ་སྐྱིད་དང་འབྱོར་པ`; `མྱོང་བ་དེ` → the plain `ཐོབ་པ་དེ`; the stacked `ཡོངས་སུ་ཤེས་པར་བྱེད་པར་སྤྱོད་པར་གྱུར་ཅིག` reduced to `སྤྱོད་ཅིང་ཡོངས་སུ་ཤེས་པར་གྱུར་ཅིག`. **`གྱུར་ཅིག` retained** — the living Tibetan optative for aspiration, and the form the card depends on.
- Confidence flags: (i) ⚑ **The verb-chain simplification is the decision on this line.** The Degé stacks `བྱེད་པར་སྤྱོད་པར་གྱུར་ཅིག`; the card splits it into "enjoy in common" + "come to be fully understood." **Confirm this does not change the claim** — specifically, whether the source means *my acting brings about the understanding* or *may it be understood in common*, which is precisely where 84000's note [n6] says the witnesses disagree. (ii) Confirm `བདེ་སྐྱིད་དང་འབྱོར་པ` is the natural modern pairing for worldly good fortune. (iii) Confirm `ཐུན་མོང་དུ` still reads as "in common" in ordinary modern Tibetan rather than as a technical term. (iv) **Register: genuinely modern, or half-classical?** Answer explicitly.
