# Day 124 — What to Wear (Toh 302)

Rail: [[2-RAILS/Verses/toh302-ornament-of-compassion]] · own translation from the Degé Kangyur (Public Domain); 84000 English as reference only.
Three padas forming one sentence, **quoted in full**. Meaning: you are going to adorn yourself with something; make it this.

> **It is a substitution, not a renunciation, and every language must keep it that way.** The sentence does not say adornment is vanity and should be dropped. It says the impulse is fine and is aimed at the wrong thing. The reader ends the line **holding something**, which is what keeps it off the bleak-diagnosis screen.

> **⚑ `རྒྱན` appears twice on purpose** — worldly ornaments, then the supreme ornament. That repetition is the sentence's hinge. It is carried deliberately in all six languages; where a language could not repeat it naturally, the QA note says so.

> **⚑ The live question for the reviewer: dharma, or life advice?** "Adorn yourself with compassion" sits close to something a secular book could say — the screen Evan applied to AN 2.33 and raised again on Day 121. The rail sets out what holds it on the dharma side (it names `སྙིང་རྗེ`, a term with a precise referent, as the *replacement for the display of wealth*). **A reviewer may still call it the other way.**

> **⚑ Toh 302 has a prior rejection on file — for a different line.** `rejected.md` holds *"Disciplined conduct is most supreme. Protect it like your own eyes!"* from this sūtra. The rail answers each of its three grounds against this line. Checked, not missed.
