# Day 105 — Harmlessness (EĀ 卷4)

Rail: [[2-RAILS/Verses/ea-trouble-nothing-alive]] · own translation from CBETA Chinese source (CC BY-NC-SA). **No paired translation, no located Pali parallel** — `grounding: chinese-source`; see the rail's honest-limit note.
Short prose instruction, **quoted in full**. Meaning: the whole of care, stated as one rule — leave everything alive unbothered by you.

> **Output languages: six** (en · zh · bo · hi · ne · mn). Ladakhi (`lbj`) discontinued by Evan, 2026-08-03. The rule docs still say seven; **do not add an `lbj` line back on their strength.**

## Source (Chinese, Ekottarika Āgama — verbatim)

> 不觸嬈一切眾生，不害一切眾生，不惱一切眾生 (`zh-ekottarika-agama.md#^pT02p0564a0501`)

### zh (Traditional)
- Back-translation: "Do not disturb any living thing, do not injure any living thing, do not cause any living thing distress."
- Findings: **our modern Traditional rendering, not the verbatim CBETA text.** Back-translated **against the verbatim source** as rule 8 requires: `不觸嬈一切眾生` → `不去擾亂任何生命`; `不害一切眾生` → `不去傷害任何生命`; `不惱一切眾生` → `不去使任何生命煩惱`. Classical→modern substitutions: `觸嬈` → `擾亂` (the classical compound is not modern usage); `害` → `傷害` (modern disyllabic); bare verbal `惱` → the modern causative `使…煩惱`; and **`一切眾生` → `任何生命`, the largest substitution** — modern `眾生` carries a Buddhist-technical flavour that `任何生命` avoids, which is what keeps the card jargon-free. All three verbs stay distinct ✓. Taiwan/HK/SG register.
- Net: clean for review.

### hi
- Back-translation: "Do not provoke any living being, do not cause harm to any living being, do not cause suffering to any living being."
- Findings: three distinct verbs — छेड़ो / हानि न पहुँचाओ / कष्ट न दो — per flag (a) ✓. जीव for `眾生` is plain and avoids the technical प्राणी-मात्र or सत्त्व. The triple किसी भी जीव repetition is kept per flag (c).
- Confidence note: छेड़ो is colloquial ("tease, provoke") — a good match for `觸嬈`'s "meddle with," but a reviewer should confirm it is not too light for a dharma card.
- Net: clean for review.

### bo ⚑
- Back-translation (approx): "Do not disturb any living creature, do not harm any living creature, do not cause any living creature worry."
- Findings: source is **Chinese**, not Tibetan, so this is fresh from the rail's meaning. `སྲོག་ཆགས` for `眾生` — "living creature," deliberately plainer than `སེམས་ཅན` (which is the standard Buddhist term and would read more technical); note **Day 107 uses `སེམས་ཅན` and `སྲོག་ཆགས` side by side** because its source names both, so a reviewer seeing both cards should know the choice here was deliberate. Three distinct verbs kept ✓.
- Confidence flags: (i) **`སེམས་ཁྲལ་མ་བཟོ` ("do not cause worry") is the weakest of the three** — it renders `惱` as anxiety-causing, which may be narrower than the source's "vex/distress"; a reviewer should find a better verb. (ii) Confirm `མ་དཀྲུགས` carries "disturb, stir up" for `觸嬈`. (iii) The third clause breaks the parallel structure of the first two (which are verb-final negatives) — confirm whether the asymmetry is acceptable or should be regularised, since the rail asks for the triple form to be preserved.
- Net: escalate to native Tibetan dharma reviewer.

### mn ⚑
- Back-translation (approx): "Do not disturb any living creature, do not injure any living creature, do not distress any living creature."
- Findings: this is the most structurally faithful of the six lines — three parallel bare imperatives with the object repeated each time, exactly matching the source's form per flag (c).
- Confidence flags: (i) confirm үймүүл ("disturb, agitate") is right for `觸嬈` rather than too strong (it can mean "incite unrest"); (ii) confirm хорло is "injure" rather than specifically "poison," which is one of its senses and would be badly wrong here; (iii) confirm зовоо is clearly distinct from хорло and reads as causing distress rather than causing pain; (iv) амьтан covers animals primarily — confirm it extends to all living beings including humans, which the source requires.
