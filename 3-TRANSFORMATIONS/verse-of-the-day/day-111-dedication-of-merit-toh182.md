# Day 111 — Dedication of Merit (Toh 182)

Rail: [[2-RAILS/Verses/toh182-dedication]] · own translation from the Degé Kangyur (Public Domain); 84000 English as reference only — **and this file is known to diverge from the Degé, unfootnoted; see the rail.**
Short verse, **quoted in full**. Meaning: a wish you can make — that everyone gets free, and that you are some help in it.

> **Closes a documented gap.** `theme-checklist.md` has listed **dedication of merit** (Mahāyāna) as never represented. This is the **only Buddha-spoken dedication verse in the vault** — a corpus-wide sweep found ~45 Tibetan files with merit-dedication optatives, and in nearly all of them the speaker is a bodhisattva, god, king, or the reader.

## QA — pre-review (against 2-RAILS/Verses/toh182-dedication.md)

### en
- Findings: **`ཉོན་མོངས` de-jargoned to "the bonds that trouble them"** per rail flag (a) — `འཆིང་བ` carried literally, the *kleśa* term as ordinary trouble. **This is the same decision made on Day 107** ("troubled"), and the two cards should be reviewed together. **`མྱ་ངན་འདས` ships as "the path to peace"** per flag (b) — the `termbase.md` row gives "nibbāna, liberation" and neither can be carried cold on a card; **this is the largest single reduction here** and a reviewer may judge it too soft. **"Bring ease"** for `དབུགས་འབྱིན` per flag (c) — the literal "give breath" would read as resuscitation, a collocation failure. **"May I" made explicit** per flag (d), which is what makes this an aspiration the reader can actually make. **"Everyone" not "all beings"** per flag (e). 124 chars. No em dash ✓.
- Net: clean for review, with the rail's structural caveat carried forward — a reviewer may call this two ideas rather than one.

### zh (Traditional)
- Back-translation: "May all people be freed from the troubles that bind them. May I give a little rest to those who get no rest, and point out the road to peace."
- Findings: our modern Traditional rendering; source is Tibetan. 煩擾 for the de-jargoned `ཉོན་མོངས` — deliberately **not** 煩惱, which is the standard technical rendering of *kleśa* and would reintroduce the jargon the rail removes. 安息 for `དབུགས་འབྱིན` keeps the breath-and-rest sense that English had to drop. 安寧 for the softened `མྱ་ངན་འདས`, not 涅槃 ✓. 願我能 makes the first person explicit per flag (d). Taiwan/HK/SG register.
- Net: clean for review.

### hi
- Back-translation: "May all people be free from the bonds that give them suffering. May I give rest to those who have no rest, and show the path of peace."
- Findings: कष्ट for the de-jargoned `ཉོན་མོངས` — deliberately not क्लेश, which would be the technical term. चैन for `དབུགས་འབྱིན` carries rest/respite well and is warmer than राहत. शांति for the softened `མྱ་ངན་འདས`, not निर्वाण ✓. दूँ / दिखाऊँ are first-person subjunctives, carrying flag (d) naturally — Hindi does this more gracefully than English.
- Net: clean for review.

### bo ⚑
- Back-translation (approx): "May all people be freed from the bonds of the mind. May I give rest to those who have no rest, and show the path of peace."
- Findings: **our own modern Tibetan rendering**, not the verbatim source. Back-translated **against the verbatim source** as rule 8 requires: all four lines map across. Modernised: `། །` breaks flattened; **`ཉོན་མོངས` → `སེམས་ཀྱི`**, de-jargoning as the English does; the classical `དབུགས་མ་ཕྱིན་པ་དབུགས་འབྱིན` unpacked into `ངལ་གསོ` (rest/respite); **`མྱ་ངན་འདས་ལམ` → `ཞི་བདེའི་ལམ`**, the same softening as the English; the mixed `ཤོག`/`གྱུར་ཅིག` optatives regularised to `ཤོག`.
- Confidence flags: (i) **`ཉོན་མོངས` → `སེམས་ཀྱི` is the substantive decision, and it is a real loss** — "bonds of the mind" is vaguer than "bonds of the afflictions," and a Tibetan reader will notice the technical term is gone. Confirm whether the de-jargoning is right for `bo` specifically, or whether `ཉོན་མོངས` should be restored on the Tibetan line even though English cannot carry it. **Same question as Day 107; decide both together.** (ii) **`མྱ་ངན་འདས` → `ཞི་བདེ` is the second such loss** on one card. (iii) Confirm `ངལ་གསོ` is right for `དབུགས་འབྱིན` or whether the breath idiom should survive in Tibetan, where it is native. (iv) Confirm `ངེད་ཀྱིས` is the right first person for an aspiration rather than `བདག་གིས`.
- Net: escalate to native Tibetan dharma reviewer.
