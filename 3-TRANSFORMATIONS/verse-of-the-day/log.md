## Running balance (current state — full batch history in [`log-history.md`](log-history.md))

- **⚑ THE PALI PROSE NIKĀYAS ARE NOW OPEN, AND THEY ARE THE BEST SOURCE THIS FORMAT HAS (first scan, 2026-08-03).** `SCAN-STATUS.md` had DN/MN/SN/AN marked unscanned since the vault began — ~245,000 lines, the single biggest untapped source. The hypothesis: their register (short declarative sentences the Buddha says *to* a listener) is exactly what the self-applying / addressed-to-you screens select for. **It held. Six of eight candidates in the Days 101–105 proposal came from the Nikāyas, and four were built (Days 101, 103, 106).**
  - **Method that worked, reuse it.** Do not read these files sequentially. Work from the **Sujato English** files, grep for the *shape* (a literal second-person pronoun, 30–200 chars, minus a stop-list of technical/stern/sober keywords), then verify the Pali at the same anchor. The English and Pali share block anchors, so grounding is trivial and every candidate arrives pre-grounded.
  - **The Aṅguttara is much the richest** — short numbered suttas, plain second-person prose. SN is moderate. MN is thin. **DN yielded nothing: 14,349 English segments, 35 survived filtering, zero candidates.** Treat DN as a low-yield source for this format for the same reason as the Kangyur Mahāyāna sūtras — it is narrative and its best lines sit inside scenes.
  - **⚑ Coverage is honest but partial.** The filter required a second-person pronoun in Sujato's English, so it provably misses (a) material the Buddha addresses to a listener that Sujato renders impersonally, (b) bare imperatives with no pronoun, (c) warmth carried by words outside the filter's list. **A second pass should target impersonal-voice and imperative-voice material.** This was a first pass, not a completed scan.
  - **⚑ A finding that affects every Nikāya rail: Sujato's English is doing real work on the addressed-to-you screen.** In AN 5.161, AN 5.34 and MN 96 the **Pali is impersonal or gerundive** and Sujato renders it in the **second person**. That translation choice is what makes these cards pass the screen at all. **Every rail built on such a passage must say so explicitly** — a native reviewer will otherwise discover it and reasonably object. Day 103's rail carries the model wording.
- **⚑ SAME SOURCE BLOCK IS FINE — a constraint I invented, overruled by Evan (2026-08-03).** Proposing two cards from Toh 313's block `^p326`, I asserted that only one could run. **Evan: "totally fine to come from the same block. The quotes are different."** **Recorded as precedent: two cards may share a source block provided the quoted verses are genuinely distinct.** The dedupe rule bites on *verses*, not blocks. Days 102 and 107 are the reference case — same block, different stanzas, five days apart, different themes and different moods. Note also that they had **different textual situations**: Day 102's verse hit an 84000 emendation while Day 107's span is variant-free, so **sharing a block does not mean sharing a footnote problem** and each rail must check its own.
- **⚑ TWO CARDS IN THIS BATCH ARE NOT TEACHINGS, AND THAT NEEDS A RULING.** Every one of the first 100 days was a teaching — something to understand or do. **Day 104 (Toh 292) is a blessing the reader receives; Day 107 (Toh 313) is an aspiration the reader makes.** Both are new forms for this calendar. That may be exactly right for a daily touchpoint (the reader is *given* something rather than asked for something) or it may not be what the product is for. **There is no precedent and neither rail can settle it. If the form is ruled out of scope, both should be pulled together.** Note Day 104's addressees are lay merchants — householders — which is unusually close to this product's actual audience.
- **⚑ 84000 EMENDS THE DEGÉ, AND IT HAS NOW HAPPENED THREE TIMES. Check its footnotes before trusting its English.** Instances found: **Toh 329** (Day 97's neighbouring stanza — `ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས` "discipline" silently rendered "merit", note [n12]); **Toh 313** (Day 102 — `ཕྲད` "meet" emended to `འབྲང` "follow", note [n6]); and a clean case, **Toh 292** (Day 104 — checked, no emendation). **The rule: for any Kangyur candidate located via 84000's English, read 84000's notes on that passage before grounding.** Ground on what `bo-toh<N>.md` actually says. This is now the most reliable source of silent error in the Tibetan pipeline.
- **⚑ Rendering screen applied deliberately for the first time (Day 106).** Sujato ships *"When anger's slain you sleep at ease."* Faithful — `chetvā` is literally "having cut" — but **English does not slay anger**, and the card ships "let go of anger." This is the collocation screen Evan named on the `苦`/"bitter" correction: **check the English collocation, not just the sense; would a native speaker with no Buddhist background ever say this sentence?** Day 106's rail records both the fix and its cost (`chetvā` is decisive, "let go" is gradual).

- **⚑⚑⚑ THREE MORE SCREENS FROM THE DAYS 96–100 PROPOSAL (Evan, 2026-08-03). Three of eight candidates were declined, and each reason is a rule nothing in this vault had written down.**
  1. **NO INFERENCE STEP — the card must already be about the reader, not a truth they must apply to themselves.** Rejected: **Dhp 228**, "There never was, nor will there be, nor is there today, anyone wholly praised or wholly blamed." Evan: *"A makes people think about why and then after they understand the implication, they apply it to themselves. That's too much in this attention economy."* The verse is a third-person observation about humanity, so the reader has to do three things — grasp the claim, derive the implication, then turn it on themselves. **This refines the self-applying screen: it is not enough that a card *can* be applied to the reader; it must arrive already applied.** The practical test is grammatical — does the card address **you**, or describe **anyone who…**? Every accepted card in this batch does: *"You become like the friends you make"*, *"Keep company with one wise enough…"*, *"Whoever does good is sure to…"*, *"Give with no hope of…"*, *"Merit is not broken by wind…"* (your merit). Dhp 228 describes *nobody in history*. That is the whole difference, and it kills a large class of otherwise-excellent general aphorisms — the "such is the way of the world" shape.

## Convention

1. Build the verse's rail (`2-RAILS/Verses/<slug>.md`) and its day card (`day-NNN-...md`) first.
2. Add a row here with the date it runs as verse of the day.
3. Set the day card's `date:` and this row's date to match.
4. Don't reuse a verse within the no-repeat window; keep the canon mix moving toward balance; don't run the same theme on consecutive days.
5. Flip Status to `complete` only after rail sign-off + native review of all renderings.
