# Day 101 — Joy From a Clear Conscience (AN 10.2)

Rail: [[2-RAILS/Verses/an10-2-no-regrets]] · grounded directly in Bhikkhu Sujato's English (CC0); Pali source also CC0.
Short prose sentence, **quoted in full**. Meaning: joy is not something you have to manufacture — it follows from having nothing on your conscience.

> **Output languages: six** (en · zh · bo · hi · ne · mn). Ladakhi (`lbj`) discontinued by Evan, 2026-08-03. The rule docs still say seven; **do not add an `lbj` line back on their strength.**

### bo ⚑
- Back-translation (approx): "If you have nothing at all to regret, joy arises of its own nature."
- Findings: source is **Pali**, not Tibetan, so this is a fresh rendering from the rail's meaning. `འགྱོད་པ` for `avippaṭisāra` — the standard Tibetan for regret, and note it is the same word used on Day 86 for the first of the four powers, where regret was a *practice*; here its absence is the condition. A reviewer should confirm the two uses do not confuse each other. `རང་བཞིན་གྱིས` carries `dhammatā` per flag (a). `དགའ་སྤྲོ` for `pāmojja` — ordinary gladness, deliberately not `དགའ་བ` (the termbase's *muditā* cell) and not `བདེ་བ`.
- Confidence flags: (i) confirm `རང་བཞིན་གྱིས` reads as "of itself, unbidden" rather than "by its intrinsic nature," which would sound doctrinal. (ii) Confirm `དགའ་སྤྲོ` is the right everyday word and not too colloquial. (iii) Confirm `སྐྱེ་གི་རེད` is the right modern ending for a general truth.
