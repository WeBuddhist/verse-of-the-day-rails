# Day 123 — May You Be Well (Toh 101)

Rail: [[2-RAILS/Verses/toh101-live-long-be-well]] · own translation from the Degé Kangyur (Public Domain); 84000 English as reference only; Mahāyāna.
Two sentences, **quoted in full**. Meaning: nothing is asked of you here. You are being wished well.

> **This is the Buddha's entire utterance, not an extract from one.** The frame stands immediately before it and the narrative resumes immediately after. There is nothing to truncate and nothing to elide — the cleanest contiguity case in the batch, worth noting because the candidate this card replaced (Toh 152) failed on exactly that point.

> **⚑⚑ `གྱུར་ཅིག` is an optative and every language ships "may you," never "you will."** 84000 renders it "you shall live long," which in English reads as a prediction. **That is the one rendering error that would make this card harmful** — a promise of health, made to someone who is ill, that the tradition cannot keep.

> **⚑ Wellbeing note for the reviewer.** The card speaks directly to the unwell, and the risk is that "free from illness" lands as though health were the condition of the blessing. What protects it: it is a **wish**, not an instruction or a promise, and the first half wishes you life rather than recovery. **Day 67** (*"though the body is sick, let the mind not be sick"*) is the companion card from the opposite direction. **Confirm this reads as tenderness rather than as a well-meant sting.**

> **⚑ This is the third generic well-being benediction, after Days 104 and 109, and Evan selected it with that objection stated.** What is new is the ground: Day 104 blesses the shape of your day, Day 109 your coming and going. **Neither wishes you life or health.** Nothing on the calendar addresses a reader who is ill except Day 67, which is about the mind amid sickness. The gap is narrow and real. Note also that the whole corpus yields only ten to twelve Buddha-spoken blessings — **the type should stay rare by design.**

> **⚑ Note on bo:** the verbatim Degé sentence below is **the source**; the shipped `bo` is **modern, plain Tibetan** like every other language. **Do not "correct" it back.** `གྱུར་ཅིག` is deliberately kept — it is still the living Tibetan optative for blessing, and replacing it would flatten the register in the one place this card cannot afford to.

### zh (Traditional)
- Back-translation: "May you live long, may you be at peace and happy, and have no illness."
- Findings: source is Tibetan, so this is a fresh modern rendering. **願 twice** carries the optative ✓ — this is the whole point and 願 is unambiguous in Chinese, with none of the "you shall" risk the English has. **安樂 rather than 快樂** for `བདེ་བ`: 安樂 carries wellbeing and ease, and is the warmer, slightly more dharmic register a blessing wants; 快樂 would read as ordinary cheerfulness ✓. 長壽 is the natural modern word for long life ✓, deliberately not 長命百歲, which is idiomatic but adds a number the source does not have. 沒有疾病 keeps the negative construction ✓. Taiwan/HK/SG register ✓, Traditional ✓.
- Net: clean for review.

### hi
- Back-translation: "May you be long-lived. May you be happy and free of disease."
- Findings: **दीर्घायु** is the standard Hindi blessing word for long life ✓ and carries the benediction register directly. **निरोग** for `ནད་མེད་པ` ✓ — it is the ordinary negative-construction word ("without disease") rather than स्वस्थ ("healthy"), which would flatten it. हो / रहो carry the optative ✓ and read as wishes, not predictions.
- Net: clean for review.
