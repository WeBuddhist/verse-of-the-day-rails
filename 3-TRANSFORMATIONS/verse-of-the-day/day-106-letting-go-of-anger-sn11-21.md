# Day 106 — Letting Go of Anger (SN 11.21)

Rail: [[2-RAILS/Verses/sn11-21-letting-go-anger]] · grounded directly in Bhikkhu Sujato's English (CC0); Pali source also CC0.
Short verse, **quoted in full**. Meaning: the anger you are holding is the reason you are not resting.

- Findings: **"Let go of anger," not Sujato's "when anger's slain."** This is the rail's ⚑ collocation flag applied deliberately — `chetvā` is literally "having cut," and Sujato is faithful, but **English does not slay anger**; the phrase belongs to dragons, reads archaic, and fights a couplet whose subject is rest. Same failure mode Evan identified when a Chinese `苦` idiom was calqued as "bitter." **The cost is acknowledged:** `chetvā` is a single decisive act and "let go" is gentler and more gradual; a reviewer may prefer "cut off anger," which keeps the decisiveness and is still idiomatic. **"Sleep at ease" kept literal** per flag (b) — the physical claim about your nights is what makes the card land, so "rest easy" and "be at peace" were both rejected. **"Carry" is added** in the second clause per flag (c), not in the Pali, to parallel the first clause's physicality. **"Anger"** per the `termbase.md` *kodha* row ✓. 79 chars. ## Why this card is fresh despite crowded ground

The calendar has five prior anger cards — Days 3, 7, 14, 65 and 83 — and **all five treat anger as an ethical problem**. This one treats it as an **expense**: it says nothing about anger being wrong, only what holding it costs you in sleep. That reframing is the reason it was selected over the several other anger passages the Nikāya scan turned up, most of which were admonishing and failed the warmth screen.
