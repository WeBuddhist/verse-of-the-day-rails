# Day 90 — The Middle Way (EĀ 卷23)

Rail: [[2-RAILS/Verses/ea-wasted-body]] · own translation from CBETA Chinese source (CC BY-NC-SA), grounded on the Pali parallel MN 36 (Sujato, CC0).
Short prose sentence, **quoted in full**. Meaning: the Buddha's own turning point — a body driven into weakness cannot do the work, so eat and get your strength back.

### zh (Traditional)
- Back-translation: "With the body weakened to this degree, there is no way to set out on the most honoured road; one should eat some nourishing food and get the body back into good condition."
- Findings: **our modern Traditional rendering, not the verbatim CBETA text** (per the note above). Back-translated **against the verbatim source** as rule 8 requires: `不可以此羸劣之體，求於上尊之道` → `身體衰弱到這種程度，是沒辦法走上最尊貴的道路的`; `多少食精微之氣，長育身體` → `該吃一些滋養的食物，把身體養好`. Classical→modern substitutions: the Literary negative-potential `不可以…求於` → `沒辦法走上`; the demonstrative-plus-nominal `此羸劣之體` → the modern degree clause `身體衰弱到這種程度`; `上尊之道` → `最尊貴的道路`; `多少食` → `該吃一些`; **`精微之氣` → `滋養的食物`, the largest substitution on the card** — the classical `氣` as an ingested subtle nutriment has no modern equivalent and a modern reader would misread it as breath or vital energy, so the sense is carried by ordinary food vocabulary; `長育身體` → `把身體養好`.
- Confidence note: `最尊貴的道路` is a literal modernisation of `上尊之道` and may read slightly grand; a reviewer might prefer `最上的道` or simply `這條最高的道路`. Also confirm `養好` does not drift toward "recuperate from illness," since the source is about strength, not sickness.
- Net: clean for review.

### bo ⚑
- Back-translation (approx): "With the body made this weak, there is no way to attain that highest path. Having eaten a little nourishing food, one must build strength in the body."
- Findings: source is **Chinese**, not Tibetan, so this is a fresh rendering from the rail's meaning rather than a modernisation of a Tibetan source. `ལམ་མཆོག` uses the `termbase.md` *magga* term `ལམ` with the superlative, per flag (b). `ཟས་གཟི་བཅུད་ཅན` for `精微之氣` renders it as nourishing food rather than any `རླུང`/wind-energy term, which would badly misdirect a Tibetan reader toward a medical-system reading of `氣` — this is the same trap the Chinese line had to avoid, and it matters more here.
- Confidence flags: (i) Confirm `གཟི་བཅུད་ཅན` is the natural modern Tibetan for "nourishing" and does not read as a Tibetan-medicine technical term. (ii) `ཐོབ་ཐབས་མེད` is emphatic — confirm it matches the source's plain `不可以` rather than overstating it (this same construction is flagged on Day 89's `bo` line; the two should probably be decided together). (iii) Confirm `ཉམ་ཆུང་བྱས་ནས` reads as "worn down by what one has done" rather than "constitutionally frail," since the whole point is that the weakness was self-inflicted by practice. (iv) Confirm the closing `དགོས` is the right modality — the source is a deliberation ("let me…"), not an obligation.
