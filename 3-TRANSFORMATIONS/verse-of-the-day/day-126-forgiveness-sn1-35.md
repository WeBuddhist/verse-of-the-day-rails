# Day 126 — Where the Grudge Lands (SN 1.35)

Rail: [[2-RAILS/Verses/sn1-35-pardon]] · grounded in Bhikkhu Sujato (CC0).
Four padas, **quoted in full**. Meaning: refusing to pardon looks like something you are doing to them; it is something you are holding.

> **This closes the vault's oldest open theme gap.** `theme-checklist.md` has carried `forgiveness` as STILL OPEN since July. All four Nikāyas were grepped for *pardon* / *forgiv*: outside SN 1.35, **every hit is embedded in a specific confession scene** and cannot ship without its backstory. Nothing in Days 1–125 teaches pardon.

> **⚑⚑ These four padas are spoken twice in the sutta, and a deity says them first.** At stanza 7 a fault-finding deity uses exactly these words to rebuke the Buddha for not accepting an apology; at stanza 11 they are spoken again in reply, and **that is the occurrence the card ships**. The sutta carries **no prose speech marker** between the two, so the attribution rests on padas 5–6 (*"I do not approve of that enmity, and I accept your mistake"*), which only the Buddha can be saying. **A reasoned reading, not a marked frame** — the reviewer should be told so.

> **⚑ Same structure as Day 119** (SĀ 1222): the Buddha ratifying a formulation someone else first voiced. The pool held this card back partly for that reason. At Day 126 the gap from Day 119 is **seven days**, which dissolves the pattern by placement.

> **Padas 5–6 and stanza 10 are excluded, deliberately.** Padas 5–6 are first-person Buddha speech (the screen added 2026-10). Stanza 10 both **names the Buddha** and is **praise of him in the third person** — a gate-1 failure. Recorded so a later pass does not reach for either as "the rest of the stanza."

> **⚑ The Pali is impersonal; Sujato supplies the "you."** `yo ce na paṭigaṇhati` and `sa … paṭimuñcati` are third singular with no expressed subject. The card follows Sujato — the weaker, Day-120 form of this disclosure, but it belongs on the record.

## QA — pre-review (against 2-RAILS/Verses/sn1-35-pardon.md)

### en
- Back-translation: n/a (anchor rendering, following Sujato).
- Findings: all four padas carried whole ✓; padas 5–6 excluded ✓. **`kopantaro` ships as "the anger stays in you"** — `antara` is "inside," and Sujato's "hidden anger" makes it concealed, which is a slightly different claim; "stays in you" keeps the location, which is the verse's whole point about where the cost lands. **`vera` ships as "grudge," not "enmity" or "hatred"** — a deliberate register choice, since the card's situation is an ordinary unforgiven apology and "enmity" is too large, the same register problem the Day 122 rail flagged on `དགྲ`. The termbase row is not overridden, only rendered at the card's register; **flagged for the reviewer**. **⚑ `paṭimuñcati` is middle voice** — the person fastens the enmity onto himself — and "the grudge holds you fast" **reverses that agency**, as Sujato's "you're stuck in your enmity" does; English has no natural self-fastening idiom that is not clinical. Flagged, not hidden. "owns their mistake" for `accayaṁ desayantīnaṁ` ✓ — plain, and it makes the situation legible without a scene. 108 chars. No em dash ✓. No first person ✓. No "Buddha" ✓.
- Net: clean for review, with the `vera` register choice and the middle-voice reversal carried to the reviewer.

### zh (Traditional)
- Back-translation: "Someone has admitted their own fault, yet you are unwilling to forgive; then the anger stays in your heart, and the resentment binds you too."
- Findings: 原諒 is the ordinary modern verb for forgiving a person ✓, deliberately not 寬恕, which is elevated and slightly religious in register. 怒氣…留在你心裡 keeps the "inside" of `kopantaro` ✓. 怨恨 for `vera` matches the "grudge" register choice rather than the termbase's 仇恨 ("hatred"), which is too large here — **the same flag as the English, and a reviewer should rule on both together**. 綁住 keeps the binding image ✓. Taiwan/HK/SG register ✓, Traditional ✓.
- Net: clean for review, with the 怨恨 / 仇恨 choice flagged.

### hi
- Back-translation: "The one who admits their fault, you do not forgive; then anger remains inside you and enmity grips you."
- Findings: **क्रोध is the termbase `kodha` term** ✓. **वैर is the termbase `vera` term** ✓ — Hindi keeps the termbase word here rather than taking the "grudge" register step the English and Chinese take, because वैर is ordinary enough in Hindi not to read as feud; **flagged so the reviewer can decide whether the three languages should align**. क्षमा for pardon ✓, the standard Hindi word. जकड़ लेता है for the binding ✓ — it keeps the grudge as the actor, the same reversal disclosed in the English.
- Net: clean for review.

### ne
- Back-translation: "If you did not forgive the one who admits their fault, anger stays right inside you and enmity keeps you bound."
- Confidence flags: (i) confirm **क्षमा गर्नु** is the natural Nepali for pardoning a person who has apologised. (ii) ⚑ Confirm **वैर** is current in Nepali and not felt as a Sanskrit or Hindi import — the termbase marks the Nepali cell for `vera` as घृणा / अवैर, and घृणा ("disgust, hatred") would be **wrong** here; this line deliberately does not use it, and that departure needs ratifying. (iii) Confirm **बाँधिराख्छ** reads as ongoing holding rather than a single act. (iv) Confirm the conditional construction (…भने) carries the same weight as the English "if."
- Net: needs native review.

### bo ⚑
- Back-translation (approx): "Even though someone has admitted their own mistake, if you do not accept their apology, anger remains inside you, and hatred binds you tightly."
- Findings: source is Pali, so `bo` is a fresh rendering from the rail's meaning, not from a Tibetan text. `ནོར་འཁྲུལ་ངོས་ལེན་བྱས` for `accayaṁ desayati` ✓; `བཟོད་གསོལ་དང་ལེན` ("accept an apology") for `paṭigaṇhāti` ✓ — chosen over the honorific `དགོངས་དག་གནང` because the card addresses an ordinary reader about an ordinary person. `ཁྲོ་བ` is the termbase `kodha` term ✓; `ཞེ་སྡང` is the termbase `vera` term ✓.
- Confidence flags: (i) ⚑ **`ཞེ་སྡང` may be too large.** It is the termbase word for hatred, but the English and Chinese lines deliberately stepped down to "grudge" / 怨恨 for this card's situation. Confirm whether Tibetan has a lighter word for a held grievance, and whether the line should take that step too. (ii) Confirm **`བཟོད་གསོལ་དང་ལེན་མ་བྱས་ན`** is the natural way to say "not accept an apology" rather than a calque. (iii) Confirm **`དམ་པོར་འཆིང་`** carries "holds you fast" without sounding like physical restraint. (iv) The line is the longest in this batch — confirm it does not run past what the card can hold in Tibetan. (v) **Register: genuinely modern, or half-classical?** Answer explicitly.
- Net: escalate to native Tibetan dharma reviewer.
