## Disambiguated Meaning
Four lines, grounded in the Tibetan source (`bo-toh182.md#^p190`):

1. **`སྐྱེ་བོ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཉོན་མོངས་པའི། །འཆིང་བ་དག་ལས་འགྲོལ་བར་ཤོག`** — "may **all people** be **freed** (`འགྲོལ`) from the **bonds** (`འཆིང་བ`) of `ཉོན་མོངས`." See flag (a) on `ཉོན་མོངས`.
2. **`དབུགས་མ་ཕྱིན་པ་དབུགས་འབྱིན་ཅིང`** — "**giving breath** (`དབུགས་འབྱིན`) to those **who have no breath** (`དབུགས་མ་ཕྱིན་པ`)." The Tibetan idiom `དབུགས་འབྱིན` means to relieve, console, give respite — literally to let someone breathe.
3. **`མྱ་ངན་འདས་ལམ་སྟོན་གྱུར་ཅིག`** — "and may [I] **show the path** to `མྱ་ངན་འདས` (nirvāṇa)." See flag (b).

The stanza is a dedication: good already done is turned outward into a wish. Its shape is a wish for everyone (line 1) followed by two things the speaker hopes to *be* for them (lines 2–3) — relief, and a direction.

- **⚑ Arguably two ideas, and this is the card's main structural risk.** Line 1 wishes for everyone's freedom; lines 2–3 are the speaker's own undertaking. A reviewer could reasonably call that two claims and invoke the one-idea screen that rejected Day 87. **What holds it together:** all three clauses are one continuous aspiration in a single grammatical breath, moving from the wish to the wisher's part in it — the same "parts of one thing" pattern that carried Day 86's four powers and Day 88's two hands. Flagged rather than assumed.
- **⚑ Two glossary terms had to be de-jargoned, and both are real reductions.** (a) **`ཉོན་མོངས`** is the technical term for the mental afflictions (*kleśa*). Rendered technically it fails the no-glossary screen outright. The card ships **"the bonds that trouble them"** — carrying `འཆིང་བ` literally and `ཉོན་མོངས` as ordinary trouble. This is the **same decision made on Day 107**, where it went to "troubled," and the two cards should be reviewed together. (b) **`མྱ་ངན་འདས`** is *nirvāṇa*; the `termbase.md` row gives "nibbāna, liberation," but a card cannot carry either cold. It ships as **"the path to peace."** A reviewer may judge this too soft — it is the largest single reduction on the card.
- **Rendering choices flagged.** (c) `དབུགས་འབྱིན` ships as **"bring ease"** — the literal "give breath" is vivid but would read as resuscitation in English, a collocation failure; "relieve" is flat. (d) The first-person of lines 2–3 is implicit in the Tibetan and made **explicit as "may I"** on the card, which is what makes it an aspiration the reader can actually make rather than an abstract wish. (e) `སྐྱེ་བོ་ཐམས་ཅད` ships as **"everyone"** rather than "all beings," which would pull toward the cosmological.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **dedication-of-merit** — **this closes a documented gap.** `theme-checklist.md` has listed dedication of merit (Mahāyāna) as *"not yet represented"* since the checklist was written. **Card type: aspiration** (`card-types.md` type 8).

**This is the vault's only Buddha-spoken dedication-of-merit verse.** The corpus-wide sweep that Evan's genre ruling made worthwhile found ~45 Tibetan files carrying merit-dedication optatives — and in nearly all of them the speaker is a bodhisattva, a god, a king, or the reader. Toh 182 is the exception.

Screens: **self-applying, no inference step** ✓ — a wish the reader makes, in the first person, with nothing to derive. **One idea** — ✓ with the structural caveat flagged above. **Warm** ✓. **Public-facing** ✓ once de-jargoned. **Dharma, not general life advice** ✓ — unambiguously. **No "Buddha" in the card** ✓. Not sober ✓ · no deity frame ✓ · not self-praise ✓.

**⚑ Batch spacing (corrected rule: no repeated themes within a batch).** This batch runs **doing-good** (108), **blessing** (109), **speaking well of others** (110) and this — four distinct subjects. Held back as duplicates: AN 3.155's morning verse and blessing, and AN 8.1. Note Day 107 was also an *aspiration* by form, four days earlier — a previous batch, and the subject differs (loving-kindness vs dedication of merit), but a third aspiration soon would be too many.

⚑ **Not to be reached for elsewhere in this sūtra:** Toh 182's other ~30 dedication stanzas each open "Through this offering of X," where X runs through crest jewels, my feet, my hands, my eyes, my flesh and blood, and male and female slaves.
