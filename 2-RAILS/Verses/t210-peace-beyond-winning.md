## Disambiguated Meaning
Four phrases, grounded in the Chinese source (`zh-dharmapada.md#^lgT04p0567b1801`) and the Pali parallel (`pi-dhammapada.md#^201`; Sujato at `en-dhammapada-sujato.md#^201`):

1. **`勝則生怨`** — "winning, then, breeds resentment" (`怨` = resentment, grudge, enmity). Matches the Pali `jayaṁ veraṁ pasavati`, "victory breeds enmity."
2. **`負則自鄙`** — "losing, then, one despises oneself" (`自鄙` = to hold oneself in contempt). **Here the Chinese and Pali diverge** — see the flag below.
3. **`去勝負心`** — "remove the winning-and-losing mind." `勝負心` is a single compound: the mentality that frames things as win or lose. This is the instruction.
4. **`無爭自安`** — "with no contention, one is naturally at peace." `自安` = at ease of itself, peace that arrives on its own once contending stops.

The structure is diagnosis (both outcomes hurt) → instruction (drop the frame) → result (peace). Both losing *and* winning are shown to cost something, which is what makes the remedy land: you are not being told to win more graciously, you are being told the contest itself is the problem.

- **⚑ Divergence between the Chinese and the Pali parallel — do not flatten.** For the loser's state, the Chinese has **`自鄙`, self-contempt** — an inward, psychological injury. The Pali has **`dukkhaṁ seti`**, "sleeps badly / lies down in pain" (Sujato: "the defeated sleep badly") — a plainer statement of suffering. The card ships the **Chinese** reading, because on this card the Chinese *is* the quoted source; the Pali is cited to anchor the verse's overall sense, not to overwrite `自鄙`. Worth noting the Chinese reading is arguably the more psychologically acute of the two, and it is the one our readers get.
- **Other rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) `勝負心` should stay a **single idea** ("the win-lose mind," "the mind that keeps score") rather than being split into two separate desires; the compound is the point. (b) `自安` carries "of itself / naturally" — the peace is not manufactured, it is what remains. Don't render it as "you will become peaceful" through effort. (c) `怨` is resentment/grudge directed outward at you by others *and* felt by them — the Pali `vera` is mutual enmity; keep it interpersonal, not "regret."
