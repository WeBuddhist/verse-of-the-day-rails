> Accayaṁ desayantīnaṁ, / yo ce na paṭigaṇhati; / Kopantaro dosagaru, / sa veraṁ paṭimuñcati;

**⚑ Padas 5 and 6 are excluded and must never be added.** The stanza continues `Taṁ veraṁ nābhinandāmi, / paṭiggaṇhāmi voccayan"ti` ("I do not approve of that enmity, and I accept your mistake"). That is **first-person Buddha speech** and fails the screen added 2026-10; it is also scene-bound. The card stops at the end of pada 4, a clause boundary and the end of the general teaching.

**⚑ Stanza 10 must never be quoted either, for two independent reasons.** It reads `Tathāgatassa buddhassa, / sabbabhūtānukampino; / Tassaccayā na vijjanti…` — it **names the Buddha** (the card screen added 2026-08-03) and it is **praise of the Buddha in the third person**, a gate-1 failure. It is quoted here only so that a future pass does not reach for it as "the same stanza."

## Disambiguated Meaning
Four padas, grounded in the Pali source and Sujato's aligned English (`en-samyutta-nikaya-sujato.md#^sn1-35-11-1`…`-11-4`):

1. **`Accayaṁ desayantīnaṁ`** — "when people **confess a fault** (`accaya`, a transgression or mistake; `deseti`, to declare or confess)."
2. **`yo ce na paṭigaṇhati`** — "**whoever does not accept it** (`paṭigaṇhāti`, to receive, take, accept — the standard verb for accepting a confession)."
3. **`Kopantaro dosagaru`** — "**with anger inside** (`kopa` + `antara`) and **heavy with hate** (`dosa` + `garu`)."
4. **`sa veraṁ paṭimuñcati`** — "**he fastens enmity onto himself** (`veraṁ paṭimuñcati`)."

The teaching is not that forgiving is kind. It is a claim about **where the cost lands**. Refusing to accept an apology looks like something you are doing to the other person; the verse says it is something you are fastening onto yourself, and it names the two things you are left holding — the anger, and the enmity. That is why this reads as a reframe rather than an instruction, and why it offers a way through without stating one: the way through is visible the moment the cost is located correctly.

- **⚑⚑ THE DISCLOSURE THAT MATTERS MOST ON THIS RAIL: these four padas are spoken twice in the sutta, first by a deity.** At `^sn1-35-7-1`…`-7-4` a fault-finding deity speaks **exactly these words** to rebuke the Buddha for not immediately accepting the deities' apology. At stanza 11 they are spoken again in reply. **The card ships the stanza-11 occurrence.**
- **⚑ Who speaks stanza 11, and how that is established.** The sutta carries **no prose speech marker** between stanzas 7 and 11 — the verses run as a bare exchange, so the speaker must be established from the text rather than from a frame. Padas 5 and 6 (`nābhinandāmi` / `paṭiggaṇhāmi voccayaṁ`, "I do not approve… I accept your mistake") can only be the Buddha: he is the one whose acceptance is at issue and the one the deities have asked to pardon them. Padas 1–4 are part of that same continuous utterance and are therefore in his mouth. **This is a reasoned reading, not a marked attribution, and a reviewer should be told so.**
- **⚑ Consequently this card has the same structure as Day 119 (SĀ 1222): the Buddha ratifying a formulation someone else first voiced.** The pool held SN 1.35 back partly for that reason, since two such cards in five days would be a pattern. **At Day 126 the gap from Day 119 is seven days**, which dissolves the objection by placement. A reader never sees who first spoke the words. Recorded so the sequencing can be overruled rather than the pattern rediscovered.
- **⚑ The Pali is impersonal and Sujato supplies the second person.** `yo ce na paṭigaṇhati` and `sa … paṭimuñcati` are both third singular with no expressed subject; Sujato renders "If **you** don't give your pardon… **you're** stuck in your enmity." The card follows Sujato. This is the **weaker, Day-120 / SN-10.2 form of the disclosure** (we follow the reference translation rather than going beyond it, unlike Day 114), but it must be on the record: the shipped line is more directly addressed to the reader than the Pali grammar is.
- **⚑ Rendering choice on pada 4, flagged not fixed.** `paṭimuñcati` is middle-voice: the person **fastens** the enmity **onto himself**. The card ships "the grudge holds you fast," which **reverses the agency** — it makes the grudge the actor. Sujato's "you're stuck in your enmity" does the same thing for the same reason: English has no natural idiom for self-fastening that is not clinical ("he binds enmity upon himself"). Defensible as a description of the felt experience, and it preserves the verse's real point that the cost lands on the withholder. **A reader of the Pali will see the middle voice; the reviewer should decide whether to accept the reversal.**
- **`vera`** is the locked termbase term (hatred / enmity). The card ships **"grudge"** rather than "enmity" or "hatred" — a **deliberate register choice**, since the card's situation is an ordinary unforgiven apology rather than a feud, and "enmity" is the register problem the Day 122 rail flagged on `དགྲ`. Flagged for the reviewer; the termbase row is not being overridden, only rendered at the register the card needs.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **forgiveness**. Card type: **reframe** (Day 125 was a teaching, so no consecutive repeat).

**This closes the vault's oldest open theme gap.** `candidate-pool/theme-checklist.md` has carried `forgiveness` as **STILL OPEN since July**, asking specifically for "a standalone-intelligible verse about forgiving someone else, one that makes sense with no backstory." All four Nikāyas were grepped for *pardon* / *forgiv* in the scan for this batch: **outside SN 1.35, every hit is embedded in a specific confession scene** (AN 9.11, AN 10.96, SN 16.10). Two candidates in the whole corpus meet the gap — this and T779 realization 6 (`菩薩布施，等念冤親，不念舊惡，不憎惡人`) — and they cannot both run, since they are the same ground.

**Nearest shipped cards, and why this is not one of them.** **Day 118** (SĀ 345) is **owning your own** fault — the exact mirror, and the reason the two should not run close together; they are seven days apart here. **Day 106** (SN 11.21) is anger in general, with sleeping at ease as the payoff. **Day 103** (AN 5.161) is cultivating loving-kindness toward someone who stirs resentment — the closest felt-state neighbour, but a different act: generating a positive state, where this is releasing a held one, and against a person who has *already apologised*. **Day 8** (Dhp 50) redirects attention to your own conduct. **Nothing in Days 1–125 teaches pardon.**

Selected against the screens on file:
- **Understandable on one read** ✓ — the situation is named inside the sentence ("someone who owns their mistake"), so no backstory is imported. This is what separates it from DA 27, rejected because "out of compassion I accept your repentance" needs the confession it answers.
- **No inference step** ✓ — second person throughout (via Sujato; see the disclosure above).
- **Offers a way through** ✓ — the cost is relocated, which is itself the release; the card is not a scolding.
- **No first-person Buddha** ✓ — padas 5–6 excluded precisely for this.
- **No "Buddha" / "Dharma" / "Sangha" / "nirvana" in the card** ✓ — stanza 10 excluded precisely for this.
- **Dharma-not-life-advice** ✓ — "forgive people" is secular etiquette; "the grudge is a thing you fasten onto yourself, and it holds you" is a claim about how mind-states work.
- Warm ✓ — it sides with the reader against their own grudge rather than blaming them for holding it · not sober ✓ · not a proclamation of attainment ✓ · fresh ✓, and nothing in `previously-used.md` touches SN 1.35.
