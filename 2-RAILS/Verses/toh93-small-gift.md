**⚑ Speaker verified in the Degé.** The two sentences immediately preceding are `བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་ཀྱིས་བཀའ་སྩལ་པ།` ("the Blessed One said") and then `དྲང་སྲོང་ཆེན་པོ་ཁྱོད་སྦྱིན་པའི་རྣམ་པར་སྨིན་པ་ལ་འཇུག་པ་ཉོན་ཅིག` ("Great Seer, listen to how the ripening of gifts works"). Our sentence is the **first thing he then says**. Before that the seer Vyāsa is asking the question, in the honorific `ལགས` register that marks his speech throughout — so the speaker boundary is unambiguous.

**⚑ One complete sentence, quoted whole.** It ends at the shad. The next sentence begins `དྲང་སྲོང་ཆེན་པོ་གང་ཡང་འཇིག་རྟེན་ཕ་རོལ་གྱིས་འཇིགས་པས…` and opens a long series of cases (giving out of fear of the next world, giving to the disciplined and the undisciplined, and so on). **That series must not be pulled in** — it is where the sūtra's merit-arithmetic lives, and it would import both a length problem and a transactional frame the card is better without.

**The vocative `དྲང་སྲོང་ཆེན་པོ` ("Great Seer") is dropped**, as on Days 121 and 124, so the line addresses the reader rather than a named interlocutor. `ཁྱོད` is already established in the sentence before, which is why the second person is not an invention here.

## Disambiguated Meaning
One sentence, grounded in the Tibetan source (`bo-toh93.md#^p565`), 84000's English consulted as a meaning reference only:

- **`དད་པས`** — "**out of faith / confidence** (`དད་པ`, Skt. *śraddhā*)" — the instrumental marks the motive the gift is given from.
- **`གང་ཅུང་ཟད་ཅི་བྱིན་པ`** — "**whatever little is given**" — `ཅུང་ཟད` is "a little, a small amount," and the doubled indefinites (`གང… ཅི`) make it maximally inclusive: *whatever it is, however small*.
- **`དེ་དག་ཐམས་ཅད་ནི་སྦྱིན་པ་ཞེས་བྱའོ།`** — "**all of that is called giving** (`སྦྱིན་པ`, Skt. *dāna*)."

The sentence answers an unspoken worry rather than issuing an instruction: it removes a threshold. The reader who assumes that real giving starts somewhere above what they can manage is told that the category is not sized. What qualifies a gift is `དད་པ`, not its amount. This is why the card is an **encouragement / permission** rather than a teaching: nothing is being asked of the reader, something is being lifted off them.

- **⚑ This verse genuinely uses `སྦྱིན་པ` / *dāna*, and that is worth recording against Day 122.** The Day 122 rail (Toh 329) flagged 84000's note [n11] that `གཏོང་བ` (*tyāga*) is deliberately distinct from `སྦྱིན་པ` (*dāna*, the perfection), and the shipped `bo` there kept `གཞན་ལ་སྟེར་བ` rather than substituting. **Here no such care is needed: the Degé says `སྦྱིན་པ`**, so the `termbase.md` *dāna* row applies directly and in every language, including `bo`. The two cards therefore sit correctly on either side of that distinction rather than blurring it.
- **`དད་པ`** is the locked *saddhā* term (`termbase.md`) and ships as **faith** in English, **信心** in Chinese, **श्रद्धा** in Hindi and Nepali, **итгэл** in Mongolian. The termbase deliberately keeps "faith" and "confidence" both open for English; **"faith" is chosen here** because the sentence is about the spirit a gift is given in, not about certainty regarding a proposition.
- **⚑ Rendering choice flagged, not fixed: how much emphasis `ནི` carries.** A natural English reading is "all of it counts as **true** giving," and the intensifier does land the reassurance harder. **The card does not ship "true"**, because the Tibetan has no word for it and the vault's standing rule is that additions are the thing to avoid; `ནི` is a topic-marker, not an adjective. A reviewer who thinks the card reads thin without it can overrule, but the addition should be a decision, not a drift.
- **`ཅུང་ཟད`** ships as "small" rather than "little" or "trifling" — "trifling" editorialises the gift down, which is precisely the reader's own view that the sentence is correcting.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **giving-however-small**. Card type: **encouragement / permission** (Day 126 was a reframe, so no consecutive repeat).

**⚑ Occasion — this card is placed for Pavāraṇā and the opening of Kathina.** 2026-10-26 is the October full moon: the end of the rains retreat, and the start of the Kathina season, which `occasions.md` lists with **generosity** among its verse themes. The rail carries `occasions: [kathina]` so the scheduler can reach it. **This placement is deliberate and the card should not be silently moved off that date** without someone deciding to give up the occasion fit. Note that `occasions.md` also insists dates are lunar and resolved at runtime per tradition, so the tag is the durable part and the date is the instance.

**Nearest shipped cards, and why this is different ground.** **Day 28** (T210 / Dhp 122, *"do not dismiss small good as worthless — small goods gather into abundance"*) is the closest and is a real neighbour, but its claim is **accumulation over time**: small acts add up. This card makes no accumulation claim at all — it says the single small gift is **already** the thing, right now. **Day 122** (Toh 329) is what giving does to your relationships. **Days 13, 45, 54, 82, 100** are generosity cards about the **fruit**, the **manner**, or the **motive** of giving. **None of them is about the size of the gift**, which is the felt obstacle this card removes.

**⚑ Adjacency: Day 122 is a giving card and sits five days back.** That is the batch's tightest theme echo and it is stated rather than buried. The two make different claims (social effect vs. sufficiency) and five days is well outside the consecutive-days rule, but a reviewer should know the batch opens and closes on generosity.
