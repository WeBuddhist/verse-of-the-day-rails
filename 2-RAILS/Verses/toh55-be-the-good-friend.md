The **entire 37 kB span between that frame and this stanza contains zero occurrences of `གསོལ`, `བཀའ་སྩལ` or `བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས`** — one uninterrupted Buddha-spoken verse discourse with no speaker change. This matters because the Kangyur's most common gate-1 failure is a warm second-person line that turns out to belong to a bodhisattva, a god or a king; here the span was checked rather than inferred.

**⚑ Two padas of a four-pada stanza, stopped at a pada boundary.** The following two padas are `དམན་པའི་སེམས་ནི་ཡོངས་སྤོངས་ལ། །རྣམ་དག་ལས་ནི་རྟག་ཏུ་སྒྲུབས།` ("give up small-mindedness entirely, always accomplish pure actions") — a **separate injunction**, not the completion of this one. Carrying them would make the card a four-item list, which is the one-idea screen that killed `udv-faith-wealth`, and would push it past the character budget. This stanza sits inside a long run of parallel second-person imperatives to Candraprabha, each pair complete in itself.

## Disambiguated Meaning
Two padas, grounded in the Tibetan source (`bo-toh55.md#^p417`), 84000's English consulted as a meaning reference only:

1. **`ཁྱོད་ནི་སེམས་ཅན་རྣམས་ཀྱི་ཕྱིར།`** — "**you** (`ཁྱོད`), **for the sake of** (`ཕྱིར`) **living beings** (`སེམས་ཅན་རྣམས`)."
2. **`དགེ་བཤེས་ཡང་དག་རབ་ཏུ་བྱོས།`** — "**become** (`རབ་ཏུ་བྱོས`, imperative) **a genuine** (`ཡང་དག`) **spiritual friend** (`དགེ་བཤེས` = `དགེ་བའི་བཤེས་གཉེན`, Skt. *kalyāṇamitra*)."

The instruction inverts the ordinary direction of the *kalyāṇa-mitta* teaching. The canon almost always addresses the reader as someone who should **seek out and keep company with** a good spiritual friend — that is Day 1 (Dhp 328) and Day 80 (Toh 300). Here the reader is told to **be one**, and to be one *for the sake of beings* rather than for their own progress. That reversal is the card.

- **⚑ The address is genuinely second-person in the Tibetan.** `ཁྱོད` is explicit and `རབ་ཏུ་བྱོས` is a true imperative — **no second person is being supplied by the translator**, unlike the Sujato-supplied Nikāya cards (Days 114, 120) or the gerundive on Day 124. This is worth recording because it is rare in this corpus and it means the self-applying screen is met by the source itself.
- **⚑⚑ THE RENDERING DECISION, AND IT IS BIGGER IN TIBETAN THAN IN ENGLISH.** `དགེ་བཤེས` here is the classical contraction of `དགེ་བའི་བཤེས་གཉེན` (*kalyāṇamitra*, the spiritual friend). **In modern Tibetan, `དགེ་བཤེས` means a Geshe — the monastic degree.** Shipping the source's contraction on the `bo` line would tell a modern Tibetan reader "become a true Geshe," which is not what the sentence says and is not something a lay reader can act on. **The `bo` line therefore spells the term out as `དགེ་བའི་བཤེས་གཉེན`.** This is the clearest justification the vault has yet produced for the source-language-ships-modern rule: here, passing the classical text through would actively mislead.
- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) English: `termbase.md` locks *kalyāṇa-mitta* as "good friend, spiritual friend" and keeps both options. The card ships **"spiritual friend"**, matching Day 80's shipped line; **"good friend" was rejected** because it collapses the term into the ordinary `sahāya` sense (the Dhp 328 / Day 1 word, which the termbase exists to keep distinct) and because it walks the card straight into the dharma-not-life-advice screen. (b) `སེམས་ཅན་རྣམས` ships as "all living beings" — plain and pan-Buddhist; not "sentient beings," which reads technical on a card. (c) `ཡང་དག` ships as "true" rather than "genuine" or "perfect" for length and plainness.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **being-a-good-friend** — a new tag, and deliberately the mirror of the existing `spiritual-friendship` tag rather than a synonym for it. Card type: **teaching** (Day 124 was a reframe, so no consecutive repeat).

**Nearest shipped cards, and why nothing on the calendar says this.** **Day 80** (Toh 300, *"Live with a good spiritual friend, a good companion, a good support"*) and **Day 1** (Dhp 328) both address the reader as the one who **has** or **finds** the friend. **Day 96** (Iti 76, *"you become the company you keep"*) is about the effect company has **on you**. **Day 117** (SN 47.19) is mutual care between two people, a reciprocity claim rather than a role. **Day 85** is the joy of good company. **In every one of them the reader is the beneficiary.** This card is the only one that makes the reader the friend, and the `ཕྱིར` clause makes the beneficiary someone else.

**⚑ Adjacency worth stating: Day 122 (Toh 329) is two days back and mentions friends.** That card says *giving increases your friends* — an observation about what happens to **your** circle. This one is an instruction to **be** a particular kind of person for others. Different claim, different direction, and two days apart rather than adjacent. Flagged so a reviewer can overrule the sequencing rather than discover the echo.
