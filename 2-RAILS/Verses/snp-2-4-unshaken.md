## Disambiguated Meaning
Grounded in the Pali (`^snp2-4-12-1` … `^snp2-4-12-4`) and Sujato at the same anchors:

1. **`phuṭṭhassa lokadhammehi`** — "for one **touched by the ways of the world**." `lokadhammā` are the world's own conditions, the things that happen to everybody: gain and loss, praise and blame, fame and obscurity, pleasure and pain. `phuṭṭha` is *touched / struck* — the verse does not imagine a life where these are absent; it assumes they land.
2. **`cittaṁ yassa na kampati`** — "**whose mind does not tremble**." `kampati` is physical shaking, the same verb used of the earth quaking. Not indifference, not not-caring: not being *moved off balance*.
3. **`asokaṁ virajaṁ khemaṁ`** — three qualities of that mind: **`asoka`** without sorrow; **`viraja`** without dust, unclouded; **`khema`** safe, secure, out of danger.
4. **`etaṁ maṅgalamuttamaṁ`** — "**this is the highest blessing**" (`maṅgala` — termbase row added with this card: blessing, good fortune · 吉祥 · བཀྲ་ཤིས · मंगल · मङ्गल · өлзий ⚑). The refrain of all eleven verses of the sutta, and it makes the verse a **blessing the reader receives** rather than an instruction they must carry out.

- **⚑ THE PALI IS IMPERSONAL AND WE SHIP THE SECOND PERSON. This must be disclosed.** `cittaṁ yassa na kampati` is a relative clause — "the one whose mind does not tremble" — and Sujato renders it impersonally, "**their** mind does not tremble" (`en-sutta-nipata-sujato.md#^snp2-4-12-2`). The card ships "**your** mind." This is `log.md`'s standing finding in its sharper form: on Days 103, and in AN 5.34 and MN 96, Sujato's own English supplied the second person and that is what let those cards pass the self-applying screen; **here even Sujato is impersonal, so the second person is entirely ours.** A native reviewer will notice. The defence: the `maṅgala` refrain already addresses the enquirer, the whole sutta is an answer to "what is a blessing?", and the genre is benediction — so the addressee is structurally present even where the grammar is not. Recorded so a reviewer can object knowingly. Day 103's rail carries the model wording for this disclosure.
- **⚑ Rendering choice: `viraja` ships as "unclouded," not "stainless."** Sujato has "stainless" and it is literal (`raja` = dust). It fails the collocation test: an English speaker with no Buddhist background does not call a person or a mind "stainless" (it collocates with steel). "Unclouded" keeps the dust-and-clarity image and is something a person would actually say. This is the same screen `log.md` records from Day 106 (`chetvā` → "let go" rather than "slain") and from the `苦`/"bitter" correction.
- **⚑ Rendering choice: `lokadhammā` ships as "the ups and downs of the world."** "Worldly things" (Sujato) is vague cold; "the eight worldly conditions" would be a technical enumeration needing a gloss. "Ups and downs" names the pairs the term actually means and is immediately legible. Flagged as a widening of a technical term into plain speech.
- **⚑ Rendering choice: `khema` ships as "safe," not "secure."** Plainer, and it is what the word means for a person rather than for a fortification.
- **⚑ The three adjectives stay three.** `asokaṁ virajaṁ khemaṁ` is a run of three, and it is tempting to reduce it to two for rhythm. Do not — that would be cutting the quote. The one-idea screen is satisfied because the three are three faces of one state, not three separate claims (the distinction `card-types.md` draws between Day 86's four powers and Day 87's four unrelated statements).
- **The stanza is complete; the sutta's list is not imported.** Snp 2.4 lists many blessings across eleven verses. This is the last substantive one, quoted whole, and it stands alone: it names its own condition (being touched by the world) and its own outcome. The other verses are **not** part of this card, and it must not later be "completed" by adding them.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **unshaken-mind**, new tag. Nearest neighbours are `equanimity` (Day 18, Dhp 81) and `inner-peace` (Days 24, 67, 92) — all about stillness as such; none of them is about *what happens when life lands on you*, which is this verse's whole frame.

**⚑ This fills a gap the checklist wrongly records as closed.** `theme-checklist.md` marks *detachment from gain and honor* as **GAP CLOSED (Day 89)**. Day 89 is struck through in `log.md` and marked **skipped**, so no card has ever run this ground. The checklist line needs correcting; this card is the first real instance.

**Gate checks.** *Buddhavacana* — a deity asks the question (`^snp2-4-1-3`/`1-4`) and the Buddha answers; verses 3 through 13 are his reply, so this stanza is his own speech. Not a devotional stanza *about* him, not spoken by the deity. *In scope* — Sutta Nipāta, Khuddaka verse text. *Traceable* — `^snp2-4-12-1` … `-12-4`. *License* — SuttaCentral/Sujato **CC0**. *Real quote, kept whole* — one complete stanza. *Length* — en 118, inside the ~125 benchmark. *Fresh* — Snp 2.4 has never been used; the Maṅgalasutta's famous verses are the earlier ones about honouring parents and living well, not this one, so the pick is genuinely under-circulated.

**Screens.** *Arrives already applied* ✓ via the blessing genre — the reader receives it (with the second-person caveat flagged above). *Lands on one read* ✓ — no scene, no proper names, condition and outcome both stated inside the stanza. *One idea in five words* ✓ — "a mind nothing shakes." *Warm* ✓ — it is a benediction, it neither instructs nor corrects. *Dharma, not general life advice* ✓ — `maṅgala`, `lokadhamma` and the three qualities are all doctrinal, and the sutta's frame is a question about what genuinely counts as good fortune. *No "Buddha" in the card* ✓. *Not sober* ✓ — the world's ups and downs are named but the verse's content is the safety, not the buffeting. *Not a proclamation of attainment* ✓ — it describes a state as a blessing available to the reader, not the speaker announcing his own.

**⚑ Batch adjacency, decided by Evan before build (2026-10).** Day 112 (T389, "content is rich though poor") is arguably the same ground as this card — *your wellbeing does not hinge on circumstances*. Evan was shown the collision and chose to run both, two days apart. The angles differ: Day 112 flips a comparison the reader makes about themselves; this is a blessing conferred on them. **Type spacing:** Day 109 was the calendar's previous blessing, five days before this one, and Day 111 was an aspiration — so this is the third card of the receive-or-wish genre in eight days. Worth watching, per `card-types.md`'s supply constraint: blessings cannot carry the calendar.
