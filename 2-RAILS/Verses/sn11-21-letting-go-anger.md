## Disambiguated Meaning
Two lines, grounded in the Pali source (`pi-samyutta-nikaya.md#^sn11-21-3-1`ff) and Sujato's aligned English:

1. **`Kodhaṁ chetvā sukhaṁ seti`** — "having **cut off** (`chetvā`) **anger** (`kodha`), one **sleeps at ease** (`sukhaṁ seti`)." The image is domestic and physical: rest, actual sleep.
2. **`kodhaṁ chetvā na socati`** — "having cut off anger, one does **not grieve** (`na socati`)."

The couplet's move is to reframe anger as a cost rather than a wrong. It says nothing about anger being unjustified, unskilful, or blameworthy — it says what holding it does to your nights. That is a genuinely different angle from every anger card the calendar has run, all of which approach it ethically.

- **⚑ The "slain" problem — a worked instance of the collocation screen.** Sujato ships *"When anger's slain."* Faithful (`chetvā` is literally *having cut/severed*), but **English does not slay anger** — the collocation belongs to dragons and giants, and on a plain card it reads archaic and slightly violent, which fights the couplet's restful subject. The card ships **"let go of anger."** This is precisely the failure mode Evan identified on 2026-08-03 when a Chinese `苦`/`樂` idiom was calqued as "bitter" — *"it doesn't sound great or natural in English… it's not relatable."* **The screen: check the English collocation, not just the sense. Would a native speaker with no Buddhist background ever say this sentence?** Recorded here as the first rail to apply it deliberately.
  - The cost of the fix is honest and should be seen: `chetvā` is decisive, a single cutting act, and "let go" is gentler and more gradual. A reviewer may prefer "cut off anger" (keeps the decisiveness, still idiomatic) or "put down anger."
- **⚑ Quadruple parallel — good evidence, and a dedupe note.** This exact couplet appears at **SN 1.71**, **SN 2.3**, **SN 7.1** and **SN 11.21**. Recurrence on that scale is strong evidence of canonical prominence. It also means a future selector may find it under a different citation: **it is spent under all four**.
- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) `kodha` ships as **"anger"** — the `termbase.md` row (瞋 / `ཁྲོ་བ` / क्रोध / क्रोध / уур). (b) `sukhaṁ seti` should stay **literal sleep** ("sleep at ease") rather than being abstracted to "rest easy" or "be at peace" — the physical claim about nights is what makes the card land. (c) `na socati` is **sorrow / grief**, and the card ships "**carry** no sorrow" — the verb "carry" is added, not in the Pali, to make the second clause parallel the first's physicality. Flagged as a small addition. (d) The vocative-free couplet needs no addressee dropped; the second person is Sujato's rendering of an impersonal Pali construction, the same pattern flagged on Day 103's rail.
- **⚑ What is NOT quoted.** The full reply verse continues past this couplet. The card stops at the clause boundary after `na socati`, which is permitted, and takes the two lines that stand alone cleanly.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **letting-go-of-anger** — a new tag, deliberately distinct from the calendar's existing anger cards: **Day 3** (`non-hatred`, Dhp 5), **Day 7** (`overcoming-anger`, Dhp 223), **Day 14** (`patience`, Dhp 320), **Day 65** (`non-hatred`, Dhp 197) and **Day 83** (`peace-beyond-winning`, T210). All five treat anger as an ethical problem. **This one treats it as an expense** — what it costs you in rest. That is why it is fresh despite the crowded ground.

**Found by the first-ever scan of the Pali prose Nikāyas.**

Screens: **self-applying, no inference step** ✓ — addressed to **you**, with the payoff stated. **One idea** ✓ — *drop anger, rest easy*; the two clauses are one claim stated twice. **Warm** ✓ — it offers relief and reproaches nobody, which is what distinguishes it from the earlier anger cards. **Public-facing** ✓ — no jargon, and the benefit is immediately legible to anyone. **No image** beyond ordinary sleep. Not sober ✓ · no deity frame ✓ · not a proclamation of attainment ✓ · does not undercut a teacher ✓.

Gate checks: **buddhavacana** — a deity (SN 1.71) or Sakka (SN 11.21) asks the question verse; **this couplet is the Buddha's reply**. **In scope** — Pali Sutta Piṭaka, Saṁyutta. **Traceable** — `pi-samyutta-nikaya.md#^sn11-21-3-1`–`^sn11-21-3-2`. **License-cleared** — SuttaCentral/Sujato **CC0**. **Real quote, kept whole** — a complete couplet, nothing stitched. **Intelligible out of context** ✓. **Length** — en 79 chars. **Fresh** — none of SN 11.21 / 1.71 / 2.3 / 7.1 has been used; the vault's only prior SN card is Day 48 (SN 1.1).
