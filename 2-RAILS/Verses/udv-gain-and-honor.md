## Disambiguated Meaning
Four lines, grounded in the Tibetan source (`bo-udanavarga.md#^p442`) and the Pali parallel (`pi-dhammapada.md#^365`; Sujato at `en-dhammapada-sujato.md#^365`):

1. **`རང་གི་རྙེད་པ་བླང་བྱ་ཞིང`** — "one should **take up** (`བླང་བྱ`) one's **own** gains (`རང་གི་རྙེད་པ`, what one receives)." A positive instruction: accept your own portion.
2. **`གཞན་གྱི་ལ་ནི་འདོད་མི་སྐྱེད`** — "and do not **generate craving** (`འདོད་མི་སྐྱེད`) for **another's**." Pali `nāññesaṁ pihayaṁ care`; Sujato: "nor should they be envious."
3. **`དགེ་སློང་གཞན་གྱི་འདོད་བྱེད་པས`** — "for one who craves what belongs to another." Pali `Aññesaṁ pihayaṁ bhikkhu`; Sujato: "A mendicant who envies others."
4. **`ཏིང་འཛིན་ཐོབ་པར་མི་འགྱུར་རོ`** — "does not attain **`ཏིང་འཛིན`** (*samādhi*)." Pali `samādhiṁ nādhigacchati`; Sujato: "does not achieve immersion."

The verse's move is diagnostic and practical at once, and its force is in the causal claim of lines 3–4: comparing your portion to someone else's is not merely unattractive, it is *mechanically* what prevents the mind from settling. It does not say the other person's share is undeserved, or that you should want less; it says the wanting-what-is-theirs is the obstruction.

- **⚑ Line 1 diverges from the Pali — flagged, do not flatten.** The **Tibetan** is positive: `རང་གི་རྙེད་པ་བླང་བྱ` = *take up / accept what you have received*. The **Pali** is negative: `Salābhaṁ nātimaññeyya` = *do not scorn / disparage one's own gain*. **And Sujato reads it a third way again**, taking the scorn as directed outward: "A well-off mendicant ought not look down on others." So there are three readings on the table for line 1, and the card ships **the Tibetan**, since that is the source. Do not import "do not look down on others" or "do not scorn what you have" into any rendering — neither is what this source says. Lines 2, 3 and 4 are an exact parallel and Sujato anchors them soundly.
- **⚑ `དགེ་སློང` ("monk") dropped from line 3.** The source addresses a monastic; the card speaks to the reader, per the Day 80 precedent (where Toh 300's monastic framing was likewise dropped). This is a **widening** of the addressee, not a change of sense — the teaching is about comparison, which is not a monastic-specific condition. Flagged because the shipped line therefore has no subject noun the source supplies.
- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) **`ཏིང་འཛིན` / *samādhi* ships as "meditative stillness"** — the second option in the `termbase.md` *samādhi* row ("concentration, meditative stillness"). Note **Sujato says "immersion,"** which is his own consistent choice and is *not* the termbase's; the termbase is the contract here, so Sujato's word is cited but not shipped. A reviewer may prefer the row's first option, "concentration," which is flatter but more standard. (b) `རྙེད་པ` is **what one receives / one's portion**, not "possessions" or "property" — the word covers alms, gifts, and any allotment, which is what lets the verse read to a lay person about their own circumstances. (c) `འདོད` is rendered **"crave"** rather than "envy": the Pali/Sujato use envy language, but the Tibetan word is plain wanting directed at another's share, and "crave" keeps it closer to the source and to the vault's `freedom-from-craving` vocabulary. (d) The card renders line 1 as **"take what comes to you"** — deliberately ordinary; "accept your own gains" reads transactional and "be content with your lot" would import `santuṭṭhi`, a different term with its own termbase row (Day 20's theme), which this verse does not use.
