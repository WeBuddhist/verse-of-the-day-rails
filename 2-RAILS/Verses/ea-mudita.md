## Disambiguated Meaning
Grounded in the Chinese source (`zh-ekottarika-agama.md#^pT02p0581c1216`) and the Pali parallel (`pi-majjhima-nikaya.md#^mn62-20-1`, `#^mn62-20-2`; Sujato at the same anchors):

The Buddha instructs Rāhula to **practise / cultivate 喜心** — the "mind of joy," *muditā*, the third brahmavihāra: gladness at others' good fortune (termbase: sympathetic joy · 喜 / 隨喜 · དགའ་བ · मुदिता · मुदिता · баясал ⚑). The sentence has three beats and the middle one is a conditional, not a repetition: **`當行喜心`** "you should practise the mind of joy," **`已行喜心`** "once the mind of joy has been practised," **`所有嫉心皆當除盡`** "all envious mind (嫉心) will be entirely removed" — 皆 ("all") and 除盡 ("removed to exhaustion") are both emphatic. So the structure is *practice → and this is what it clears*, matching the whole passage's pattern: mindfulness of breathing clears sorrow, contemplation of impurity clears greed, loving-kindness clears anger, compassion clears the wish to harm, this one clears envy, and equanimity clears conceit (`zh-ekottarika-agama.md#^pT02p0581c1216`).

- **⚑ Divergence between the Chinese and the Pali parallel — do not flatten.** The Chinese names what *muditā* removes as **嫉心, envy/jealousy**. The Pali parallel names it as **`arati`**, which Sujato renders "discontent" (`en-majjhima-nikaya-sujato.md#^mn62-20-2`) — displeasure or dissatisfaction, a wider state than envy specifically. The card ships the **Chinese** reading (envy), because on this card the Chinese *is* the quoted source; the Pali is cited as the meaning anchor for *muditā* itself, not to overwrite 嫉. Flagged rather than reconciled.
- **Other rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) 行 here is "practise / put into action," not merely "do"; "cultivate" is the natural dharma register in most of the six languages. (b) 喜心 should take each language's **standard** *muditā* term (termbase row), not a general word for "happiness" — a card that renders it "be happy" loses the whole point, which is joy *at another's* good. (c) 除盡 is emphatic and complete; keep the "entirely / completely" force rather than softening to "reduces."
