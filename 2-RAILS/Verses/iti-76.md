## Disambiguated Meaning
Four lines, grounded in the Pali source (`pi-itivuttaka.md#^iti76-5-1`ff) and Sujato's aligned English (`en-itivuttaka-sujato.md#^iti76-5-1`ff):

1. **`Yādisaṁ kurute mittaṁ`** — "whatever sort of **friend** (`mitta`) one makes."
2. **`yādisaṁ cūpasevati`** — "and whoever one **associates with / frequents** (`upasevati`)." Lines 1–2 are a pair covering both deliberate friendship and ordinary proximity — the people you choose *and* the people you are simply around.
3. **`Sa ve tādisako hoti`** — "that is indeed (`ve`) what he **becomes** (`tādisako hoti`)." A claim about change, not about resemblance: the company does not reveal who you are, it makes you that.
4. **`sahavāso hi tādiso`** — "for **living together** (`sahavāsa`) is like that." The reason clause: this is simply what sharing a life does.

The verse's move is to reframe company as formative rather than incidental. It does not tell the reader to seek out good friends or avoid bad ones; it states a mechanism and leaves the reader to notice who they are around. That restraint is what makes it land without moralising.

- **⚑ Containment flag — what surrounds this verse must NOT be carried over.** The quote sits inside a longer poem that includes putrid-fish and sandalwood similes and, critically, the line `Asanto nirayaṁ nenti` — "the wicked lead you to hell." **None of that is in the quote and none of it may enter any rendering.** With the hell clause the card would fail both the warmth screen and the public-facing screen (see Theme notes). The four-anchor sentence is self-contained and stops cleanly.
- **⚑ Termbase flag: this is plain `mitta`, NOT `kalyāṇa-mitta`.** `termbase.md` keeps a deliberate distinction, and the Tibetan/Chinese cells differ sharply — `kalyāṇa-mitta` renders as `དགེ་བའི་བཤེས་གཉེན` / 善知識 (the spiritual friend, Day 80's word). **Do not use those here.** This verse uses the everyday word, so the renderings take the everyday terms: 朋友 · `གྲོགས` · मित्र · मित्र · нөхөр.
- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) Lines 1–2 are two aspects of one thing (chosen friends and mere company) and **both should ship** — dropping one narrows the claim to deliberate friendship, which is not what the verse says. (b) `hoti` is **"become,"** not "seem" or "show yourself to be" — the verse asserts real change. (c) `sahavāsa` is **living/spending time together**, ordinary and domestic, not "cohabitation" (too narrow) or "fellowship" (too churchy). (d) The card is deliberately **not** turned into advice ("so choose good friends") — the source states a mechanism and stops, and adding the imperative would make it corrective.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **good-company** — related to but distinct from Day 80's `spiritual-friendship` (Toh 300, the Buddha's instruction to seek a good friend) and Day 85's `joy-of-good-company` (Udānavarga 30, the felt gladness of it). This is the **third** company-adjacent card and the claim is genuinely different: not *seek them out*, not *how good it feels*, but **they are changing you**. Eleven days after Day 85.

Selected against all five screens now on file, which is why it survived a batch where three of eight were declined:
- **Self-applying** ✓ — no person, place, scene or event.
- **No inference step** ✓ — this is the screen that killed Dhp 228 in the same proposal, and it is the reason this verse works: it is grammatically addressed to **you** (*"whatever friend **you** make… that's how **you** become"*). The reader does not have to derive an implication and then apply it; the card arrives already applied.
- **One idea** ✓ — nameable in five words: *you become your companions.*
- **Warm, not serious** ✓ — observational rather than admonishing, and it praises nobody and blames nobody. This depends entirely on the containment flag above being respected.
- **Public-facing** ✓ — nothing here would put off a reader who is not yet Buddhist; it reads as ordinary human wisdom, which is what the newcomer-facing screen asks for.
- Not sober, no glossary term, no deity frame, not a proclamation of attainment.
