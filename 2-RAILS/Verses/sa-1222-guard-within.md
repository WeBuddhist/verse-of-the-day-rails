## Disambiguated Meaning
Grounded in the Chinese source (`zh-samyukta-agama.md#^pT02p0336b0610`) and the Pali parallel (`^sn3-5-2-5`; Sujato at `^sn3-5-1-18` / `^sn3-5-2-1`):

1. **`護其內者`** — "**the one who guards what is within**." `內` / *ajjhattika* is the interior, and the passage has already defined what it means: conduct of body, speech and mind (`行身善行、行口善行、行意善行`). It is not a psychological interior or a private feeling — it is the three doors.
2. **`名善自護`** — "**that is what is properly called guarding oneself**." `善` here is "well / properly," qualifying the naming, not a separate moral claim.
3. **`非謂防外`** — "**it is not what is called defending the outside**." A flat denial, and it is the half that does the work: the reader's existing defences are named and set aside.

The reframe: **protection is not a thing you build around your life, it is the way you conduct it.** The scene is a king with four armies around him being told his armies are not what keeps him safe, which is exactly the reader who believes they are already well defended.

- **⚑ Speaker note a reviewer will want, and it is a disclosure rather than a gate problem.** In both recensions the king says it first and the Buddha then affirms and repeats it. The Chinese opens `佛告大王：「如是，大王！如是，大王！…」` and the shipped sentence sits inside that speech; the Pali has "That's so true, great king!… And he repeated the king's statement, adding" (`^sn3-5-2-1`) before `^sn3-5-2-5`. **So the sentence is genuinely Buddha-spoken in both** — the gate is met — but the *insight* originated with Pasenadi and the Buddha ratifies it. Recorded so a reviewer knows, rather than discovering it.
- **⚑ Rendering choice: `內` ships as "what is within you," not "the inner" or "the interior."** "The interior" is architectural and cold; "the inner" needs a noun. "What is within you" is plain and puts the reader in the sentence. Flagged as a mild widening of a term the source defines narrowly as the three doors — the rail keeps the definition; the card does not carry it, and does not need to.
- **⚑ Rendering choice: `自護` ships as "keeps you safe," not "self-protection" or "guarding yourself."** The literal forms read clinical or read as self-defence training. "Keeps you safe" is the collocation an English speaker would use, and it preserves the passive position of the person being protected. This is the same collocation screen applied on Day 106 and on the `苦`/"bitter" correction.
- **⚑ Rendering choice: `防外` ships as "defending what is outside," keeping the denial explicit.** The negative half is not decoration: without it the card becomes a bland statement about inwardness, and with it the card removes a specific false comfort. Every language must keep the contrast structure.
- **⚑ Do not import the four armies, and do not import the negative half.** The elephant, horse, chariot and infantry divisions are what make the scene vivid, but they are a specific royal frame and would need context; the parallel negative passage (`若有行身惡行…實非自護`) is admonishing. **Both excluded.** The card must not later be "completed" by adding either.
- **⚑ Do not import the three doors as a list.** `行身善行、行口善行、行意善行` defines `內` and belongs in the rail, but putting it on the card would make it a three-item enumeration and fail the one-idea screen.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **inward-protection**, new tag.

**Dedupe — checked against the English line of every one of the 111 shipped cards, not just against `source_ref`.** Nearest three, and why each is a different teaching:
- **Day 49** (Udānavarga / Dhp 160, *"You are your own refuge, who else could be?"*) answers **who** can protect you — the agency question, and its force is that nobody else will do it for you. This answers **where** protection actually is, and its force is that the defences already built outward are the wrong ones. Different question, different target. ⚑ Note also that Day 49 is flagged in `log.md` under the "self-reliance that excludes others" screen; this card does not exclude anyone, it only relocates the guard.
- **Day 16** (Dhp 39, *"A mind that does not fester, a heart unhurt by hate… has nothing to fear"*) names an inner **quality** that makes someone fearless. This card makes no claim about hate and instead denies the outward guard.
- **Day 114** (Snp 2.4, unshaken mind) concerns not being *rocked* by fortune. This concerns not being *unguarded*. Adjacent but distinct, and three days earlier there would have been a collision; at five days' distance with a different verb and a different claim, it stands.

**Gate checks.** *Buddhavacana* — the block opens `佛告大王` and the shipped sentence is inside that speech; see the speaker note above for the ratification nuance. *In scope* — Saṁyukta Āgama. *Traceable* — `^pT02p0336b0610`, verified by grep (unique in the corpus). *License* — CBETA **CC BY-NC-SA** (source); Sujato **CC0** (Pali parallel). *Real quote, kept whole* — one complete sentence, contiguous. *Length* — en 88, zh 22. *Fresh* — SĀ 1222 has never been used, and no Patton pairing exists for it.

**Screens.** *Arrives already applied* ✓ — `自護` is reflexive in the Chinese and the English carries "you" twice; the reader is the one being protected. *Lands on one read* ✓ — the inside/outside contrast is complete in the sentence; no story, no unstated referent, and the four armies are not needed. *One idea in five words* ✓ — "real protection is set inside." *Warm* ✓ — it consoles: safety turns out to be something already within reach rather than something to be built, and the negative half only removes a false comfort rather than issuing a rebuke. *Dharma, not general life advice* ✓ — the passage defines `內` as conduct of body, speech and mind, so this is ethics, not psychological self-care; that definition is the rail's answer to the screen. *No "Buddha" in the card* ✓. *Not sober* ✓. *Not a proclamation of attainment* ✓.

**⚑ Batch spacing — the reason this card is Day 119 and not Day 117.** Day 117 (SN 47.19) turns on the same verb family: `rakkhati` / `rakkhā` / `自護`, guarding oneself. The two are genuinely adjacent ground — *what it actually means to look after yourself* — and Day 118 was placed between them deliberately rather than running them together. The angles differ (Day 117: self-care and other-care are one act; this card: the guard belongs inside, not outside), and both rails say so. The two cards also use **different verbs in every language on purpose** — 照顧 / `ལྟ་རྟོག` there, 守護 / `སྲུང་བ` here — and a reviewer should decide whether that distinction is right or whether the two should be harmonised.

**Shipped-zh policy — modern, not the classical source.** The verbatim CBETA sentence is **the source**; the shipped `zh` is **modern Traditional Chinese**. Substitutions: `護其內者` → `守護內在的`; `名善自護` → `才是真正保護自己`; `非謂防外` → `不是防守外面的`. The classical `者…名…非謂…` naming construction is replaced by the modern `才是…不是…` contrast, which is what carries the reframe in modern Chinese. Standing rule — **do not revert the card's zh to the classical text.**
