## Disambiguated Meaning
Grounded in the Chinese source (`zh-ekottarika-agama.md#^pT02p0671b2906`) and the Pali parallel (`pi-majjhima-nikaya.md#^mn36-33-2`; Sujato at `en-majjhima-nikaya-sujato.md#^mn36-33-2`):

1. **`不可以此羸劣之體，求於上尊之道`** — "with this **weak and wasted body** (`羸劣之體`) one cannot seek (`求`) the **most honoured path** (`上尊之道`)." Pali: `na kho taṁ sukaraṁ sukhaṁ adhigantuṁ evaṁ adhimattakasimānaṁ pattakāyena`; Sujato: "I can't achieve that pleasure with a body so severely emaciated."
2. **`多少食精微之氣，長育身體`** — "let me eat some **nourishing sustenance** (`精微之氣`) and **build up the body** (`長育身體`)." Pali: `yannūnāhaṁ oḷārikaṁ āhāraṁ āhāreyyaṁ odanakummāsan`; Sujato: "Why don't I eat some solid food, some rice and porridge?"

The context is the turning point of the Buddha's own path: after six years of extreme austerity he concludes that the austerity is not the way, eats, recovers his strength, and only then reaches the first absorption (`^mn36-34-1`). The teaching the card carries is therefore not a general permission to be comfortable — it is the Buddha's own finding that a body driven into weakness cannot do the work, spoken about himself, at the cost of losing his five companions.

- **⚑ Divergence between the Chinese and the Pali — flagged, not flattened.** The **Chinese** says the emaciated body cannot reach `上尊之道`, *the most honoured **path***. The **Pali** says it cannot reach `sukhaṁ`, *that **pleasure*** — meaning the jhāna-bliss he had just recalled from childhood two paragraphs earlier. So the Chinese generalises to the path, the Pali is specific to the meditative pleasure. **The card ships the Chinese ("the highest path")**, since that is the source; do not substitute "that bliss" or "the first absorption" from the Pali. Note the Chinese reading is the more card-friendly one, which is a reason to be careful rather than pleased: the more useful text is not automatically the right one, and the flag records that the Pali is narrower.
- **⚑ Second divergence: what he eats.** The **Pali is homely and concrete** — `odanakummāsa`, "rice and porridge." The **Chinese is abstract** — `精微之氣`, literally "subtle/refined *qi*," a nourishment word with no exact English or modern-Chinese equivalent. The card renders it as **"nourishing food,"** which is the sense, and does **not** import the Pali's rice and porridge, tempting though the concreteness is.
- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) `羸劣` is **weak / worn down**, not "sick" or "starving" — and it should not be rendered with modern body-image or diet vocabulary, which would land the card in wellness-app register. **"Worn down to weakness"** is chosen for this reason. (b) `上尊之道` renders as **"the highest path"**; *magga* is `termbase.md`-locked as "path" (道 / `ལམ` / मार्ग / मार्ग / зам), and `上尊` is the superlative, so "highest path" keeps the locked term. Avoid "supreme way" (archaic) and "the noble path" (imports *ariya*, a different term). (c) The sentence is a **first-person deliberation** in the source (`我復作是念`), and the card recasts it as an address to the reader ("**you** cannot reach… take some…"). This is the card's largest structural move and is flagged accordingly: the teaching is identical, but the shipped line asserts to the reader what the source records the Buddha concluding about himself. A reviewer may prefer to keep the first person ("I could not reach the highest path with a body worn down to weakness"), which is more faithful and arguably more moving, at the cost of needing the reader to know who is speaking.
- **Wellbeing note for the reviewer.** This card touches self-punishment and food. It is included **because** it counters harmful striving, not because it discusses the body: the Buddha's own conclusion is *eat, and get your strength back*. Any rendering that could be read as prescribing how much to eat, or as commentary on body size, is out of bounds. The `多少` ("some, a certain amount") must stay vague — do not sharpen it into a quantity.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **middle-way** — this **fills a long-standing `theme-checklist.md` gap** that was marked "only SN 1.1 so far; a clearer/different angle would help." This is the historically central instance: not an analogy for balance (the lute string, the log adrift, over-effort versus laziness — all of which the pool also holds) but **the actual turning point of the Buddha's own path**, in his own voice.

Selected against the batch's screens: **concrete** (a body, food, strength — nothing abstract); **no culture-specific deity or Vedic frame**, notable given the surrounding narrative is about competing ascetic practice; and the **opening clause is a diagnosis but an immediately actionable one**, with the remedy in the same sentence. **Offers a way through** ✓ — the remedy is half the quote, which is precisely why the quote does not stop at `上尊之道`.

Selection also rests on something the calendar has not said before: it gives a reader permission to stop punishing themselves in the name of practice. `discovery-by-feeling`'s `discouraged` state is otherwise served mostly by encouragement-to-persist cards; this is the one that says the opposite when the opposite is what is needed.

Gate checks: **buddhavacana** — the Buddha's own first-person recollection, recounted by him to the monks; same category as the Toh 95 hesitation-to-teach reflection, and the buddhavacana gate covers the Buddha's final-lifetime pre-awakening words per Evan's 2026-07-13 ruling (this episode sits in that lifetime, weeks before the awakening). **In scope** — Chinese Āgama (Ekottarika, Taishō T125). **Traceable** — `zh-ekottarika-agama.md#^pT02p0671b2906`. **License-cleared** — CBETA CC BY-NC-SA source, Sujato CC0 for the parallel, own renderings. **Real quote, kept whole** — one contiguous sentence-span, stopped at a clause boundary, nothing stitched. **Intelligible out of context** ✓ — it needs no knowledge of the six years of austerity to land, though knowing it deepens it. **Accessible with no glossary** ✓. **Not a proclamation of attainment** ✓ — the opposite: it is a record of a mistake corrected. **Length** — zh 27 chars, en 124, inside budget. **Fresh** — EĀ 卷23 has never been used (the log holds 卷5, 卷6, 卷7, 卷38); MN 36 has never been used.
