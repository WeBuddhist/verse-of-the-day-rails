**The shipped verse, verbatim from that block:**
> བདག་གིས་འཇིག་རྟེན་པའི་ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པའི་བདེ་བ་གང་ཅི་མྱོང་བ་དེ། སེམས་ཅན་ཐམས་ཅད་དང་ཐུན་མོང་དུ་ཡོངས་སུ་ཤེས་པར་བྱེད་པར་སྤྱོད་པར་གྱུར་ཅིག
> *(verbatim substring of `bo-toh141.md#^p600`. Nothing altered, nothing elided; no folio marker falls inside the sentence.)*

**⚑ Who is speaking, and whose "I" this is.** The frame in the same block is `དེའི་ཚེ་བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་ཀྱིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་རྣམས་ལ་བཀའ་སྩལ་པ། རིགས་ཀྱི་བུ་དག་ཁྱེད་ཀྱིས་…གཟུངས་ཟུང་ཤིག །དེ་ནི་འདི་ལྟ་སྟེ།` ("the Blessed One said to the bodhisattvas: sons of good family, hold the dhāraṇī called Six Gates. It is thus:"). The six aspirations follow, and the Buddha then prescribes reciting them three times by day and three times by night. **So the words are the Buddha's, and the first person is the reciter's — the reader's — not his.** The screen added 2026-10 bars the **Buddha** speaking as "I"; it does not bar an aspiration he gives the reader to say. Precedent: **Day 107** (Toh 313) and **Day 111** (Toh 182), both reader-voiced.

**⚑ In scope: this is a dhāraṇī text sitting in the mdo section, not a tantra.** Toh 141 falls in the General Sūtra range (Toh 96–202), not the `gzungs 'dus` collection and not the tantra division. The quoted material is a set of plain aspirations, with no mantra syllables, no visualisation and no empowerment content. Recorded because "dhāraṇī" in a title will otherwise look like a scope failure on every future audit.

**⚑ One aspiration of six, quoted whole, and the other five must not be pulled in.** Each of the six is a complete sentence beginning `བདག་གིས…` and ending `གྱུར་ཅིག`. Ours is the second. The first concerns suffering across rebirths, the third confession of misdeeds, the fourth the work of Māra. **Taking more than one would make the card a list** — the one-idea screen that killed `udv-faith-wealth` — and would break the budget.

## Disambiguated Meaning
One aspiration, grounded in the Tibetan source (`bo-toh141.md#^p600`), 84000's English consulted as a meaning reference only:

1. **`བདག་གིས་འཇིག་རྟེན་པའི་ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པའི་བདེ་བ་གང་ཅི་མྱོང་བ་དེ།`** — "**whatever** (`གང་ཅི`) **worldly** (`འཇིག་རྟེན་པའི`) **abundance and happiness** (`ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པའི་བདེ་བ`) **I experience** (`བདག་གིས་མྱོང་བ`)."
2. **`སེམས་ཅན་ཐམས་ཅད་དང་ཐུན་མོང་དུ་ཡོངས་སུ་ཤེས་པར་བྱེད་པར་སྤྱོད་པར་གྱུར་ཅིག`** — "**may I act** (`སྤྱོད་པར་གྱུར་ཅིག`) **so as to make it fully known / bring it to full understanding** (`ཡོངས་སུ་ཤེས་པར་བྱེད་པ`) **in common with** (`ཐུན་མོང་དུ`) **all beings** (`སེམས་ཅན་ཐམས་ཅད`)."

The move is not "give your things away." It is that **good fortune is not owned privately**. Whatever goes well for you is held in common with everyone, and the wish is to live as though that were true — and for the sharing itself to be the thing that brings people to understanding. The felt state it meets is specific and under-served: the unease of things going well for you when they are not going well for others.

- **⚑⚑ THE WEAK CLAUSE, AND IT IS THE REASON THIS CANDIDATE SAT ON THE BENCH.** `ཡོངས་སུ་ཤེས་པར་བྱེད་པ` is abstract, and no object of the understanding is stated — **understanding of what?** The card ships "may it bring us to understanding," which a cultural Buddhist reader will read as insight, but a reader could reasonably ask. This is the **out-of-context screen at its edge**, not over it. Alternatives weighed and rejected: dropping the clause (a distillation, forbidden), and supplying an object such as "understanding of the way things are" (an addition the Tibetan does not license). **The clause is shipped as it stands and flagged. A reviewer may call it the other way.**
- **⚑ 84000 note [n6]: PT77 and the Sanskrit read the second clause differently**, closer to "may my enjoyment be in common with all beings." **The rail grounds on the Degé as it stands**, per the vault's standing practice, and records the variant so a reviewer knows the clause is textually contested as well as semantically thin. This is now the sixth recorded case of the reference witnesses diverging from the Degé.
- **`བདག་གིས` ships as "I" and must stay first person.** This is an aspiration the reader makes. Converting it to the second person ("may you share…") would turn a wish the reader speaks into a wish made over them, which is a different card and a different type.
- **`འཇིག་རྟེན་པའི་ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པའི་བདེ་བ`** ships as "worldly happiness" — `ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པ` is abundance or excellence, and the compound covers both material good fortune and the ease it brings. "Worldly" is kept because the sentence is deliberately about ordinary good luck rather than spiritual attainment; dropping it would make the aspiration grander and vaguer than it is.
