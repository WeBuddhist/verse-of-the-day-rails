## Source Text
![[1-SOURCES/Text/pi-dhammapada.md#^173]]

> Yassa pāpaṁ kataṁ kammaṁ, / kusalena pidhīyati; / Somaṁ lokaṁ pabhāseti, / abbhā muttova candimā.

**Boundary ✓.** One complete Dhp stanza, a single `yassa … so …` sentence. **Buddhavacana gate ✓** (Dhammapada, corpus-level). No first-person, no "Buddha."

## Authoritative Renderings

Sujato: "Someone whose bad deed is supplanted by the good, lights up the world, like the moon freed from clouds."

## Disambiguated Meaning
A wrong already done (`pāpaṁ kataṁ kammaṁ`) can be covered over / outshone by good (`kusalena pidhīyati`); such a person lights up the world like the moon coming out from behind cloud (`abbhā muttova candimā`) (1-SOURCES/Translations/en-dhammapada-sujato.md#^173). The past deed is not erased or denied — it is *outshone* by present good. A redemption/fresh-start image, not a claim that consequences vanish.

**⚑ `pidhīyati` = "covered over / closed over," rendered "cover … with good."** Not "erased." The card keeps the honest sense: the wrong happened; the good you do now is brighter.

**⚑ The simile names its own referent** — the person who turns to good *is* the moon emerging. Keep the moon image concrete and everyday (a familiar sight, unlike an obscure vehicle — the failure mode of the rejected AN 4.32 chariot-linchpin).

## Theme & Selection Notes
**Theme:** `fresh-start`. Distinct from Day 81 (`joy-in-doing-good`, Dhp 118 — the gladness of doing good) and Day 86 (`purification-of-misdeeds`, the four powers — a structured repair method): this is the simpler, warmer *image* of redemption — good outshining a past wrong. Card type: **encouragement** (lifts the weight of regret). Casual-audience fit ✓: hopeful, homely image, instant.

**Not bleak ✓.** The word `pāpa` (a past wrong) opens it, but the arc moves immediately to good and ends on light — the affective close is the moon, not the wrong.
