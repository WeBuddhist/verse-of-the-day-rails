> །གསེར་ལ་སོགས་པ་འཇིག་རྟེན་རྒྱན། །རབ་ཏུ་སྤངས་པས་སྙིང་རྗེ་ཡི། །རྒྱན་གྱི་མཆོག་གིས་བརྒྱན་པར་བྱ།
> *(verbatim substring of `bo-toh302.md#^p253`. The speech frame `བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་ཀྱིས་བཀའ་སྩལ་པ།` ("the Blessed One said") stands immediately before it, closing the question that Upāli's retinue has just put. Nothing altered, nothing elided.)*

**⚑ Three padas, and the stop is a clause boundary, not a truncation.** The Buddha's reply opens a run of parallel substitutions, each one complete in itself and each swapping a worldly adornment for an inner one. Ours is the **first** of the run. The next unit begins `འཇིག་རྟེན་མ་ཡིན་བསླབ་པ་ཡི། །གོས་གྱོན་གོས་མཆོག་བཟང་པོ་ཡིན།` (the garment of training rather than worldly cloth), and the ones after that substitute `ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས` for perfume and `དད་པ` for cosmetic colour. They are **separate substitutions, not the completion of this sentence** — which is what makes stopping here legitimate under the contiguity rule, and what keeps the card to one idea. The full run also busts the character budget several times over. **Precedent for stopping at a clause boundary inside a verse run:** `toh329-giving-makes-friends` (Day 122).

## Disambiguated Meaning
Three padas, grounded in the Tibetan source (`bo-toh302.md#^p253`), 84000's English consulted as a meaning reference only:

1. **`གསེར་ལ་སོགས་པ་འཇིག་རྟེན་རྒྱན།`** — "**worldly ornaments** (`འཇིག་རྟེན་རྒྱན`) **such as gold** (`གསེར་ལ་སོགས་པ`)."
2. **`རབ་ཏུ་སྤངས་པས`** — "**having thoroughly given (them) up** (`རབ་ཏུ་སྤངས་པ`)."
3. **`སྙིང་རྗེ་ཡི་རྒྱན་གྱི་མཆོག་གིས་བརྒྱན་པར་བྱ།`** — "**one is to be adorned** (`བརྒྱན་པར་བྱ`) **with the supreme ornament** (`རྒྱན་གྱི་མཆོག`) **of compassion** (`སྙིང་རྗེ`)."

The move is a **substitution, not a renunciation**. The sentence does not say that adornment is vanity and should be abandoned; it says the impulse to adorn yourself is fine and is pointed at the wrong thing. You are still wearing something. What you wear is `སྙིང་རྗེ`. That is why this is a reframe rather than an ascetic instruction, and it is what keeps it off the "bleak diagnosis" screen: the reader is left with something to put on, not something taken away.

- **⚑ `བརྒྱན་པར་བྱ` is a gerundive, and rendering it as a second-person imperative is a decision, not a reading of the grammar.** The form is "is to be adorned" — impersonal, prescriptive, with no expressed subject. 84000 renders the run as imperatives addressed to the listener, and the card follows that. This is the **same class of disclosure** as the Sujato-supplies-the-second-person note on the Nikāya cards (Days 114, 120): the shipped line is more directly addressed to the reader than the source grammar is. **A native Tibetan reviewer should confirm the imperative over an impersonal "one should."** The alternative ("the finest ornament to wear is compassion") is impersonal and truer to the grammar, but it re-introduces an inference step the self-applying screen exists to prevent.
- **⚑ `རྒྱན` appears twice in three padas** — "worldly ornaments" then "supreme ornament." The repetition is the sentence's whole hinge and must be **carried deliberately in every language**, not smoothed away by using two different words. Where a language cannot repeat the noun naturally, say so in QA rather than silently varying it.
- **`སྙིང་རྗེ`** is the locked *karuṇā* term (`termbase.md`) and ships as **compassion** in English, **慈悲** in Chinese, **करुणा** in Hindi and Nepali, **нигүүлсэл** in Mongolian. It is **not** `anukampā` (the Day 120 term, which never ships as "compassion") and not `བྱམས་པ` / *mettā*.
- **`གསེར`** ships as plain "gold" — it is the concrete anchor that keeps the line from floating into abstraction, and every language should keep the metal rather than generalising to "riches."

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **true-adornment** — a new tag. Card type: **reframe** (Day 122 was teaching, so no consecutive repeat; the batch alternates reframe / teaching / reframe).

**Nearest shipped cards, and why this is different ground.** **Day 44** (Toh 194, *"whoever wishes to be beautiful should cultivate patience"*) is the closest and is the same family — an inner quality is what actually makes you lovely — but it is a **cause-and-effect** claim about a different quality (patience), where this is a **substitution** claim about compassion: not "do X and you will become beautiful" but "you are already adorning yourself; adorn yourself with this instead." **Day 112** (T389, *"content is rich though poor"*) works the inner-vs-outer-wealth ground, which is about **having**, not about **displaying**. **Days 39 and 70** are great compassion as such, with no adornment frame. Nothing shipped makes this move.

Selected against the screens on file:
- **Understandable on one read** ✓ — gold and ornaments are universally legible, and the substitution is stated inside the sentence rather than left for the reader to infer. This is the distinction `log.md` draws between *concrete* and *understandable*: the image is not a scene the reader must be standing in (the Toh 205 lamp failure), it is an object the reader already owns the idea of.
- **No inference step** ✓ — the imperative is addressed, and the thing to do is named.
- **Offers a way through** ✓ — the reader ends the sentence holding something.
- **No first-person Buddha** ✓ (the screen added 2026-10) — grep-clean in every language.
- **No "Buddha" / "Dharma" / "Sangha" / "nirvana" in the card** ✓.
- Warm ✓ · not sober ✓ · no deity frame ✓ · no glossary term ✓ · not a proclamation of attainment ✓ · fresh (never used) ✓.
- **⚑ Dharma-not-life-advice screen — the live question on this card, stated plainly for the reviewer.** "Adorn yourself with compassion" is close to something a secular book could say, and this is the screen Evan applied to AN 2.33 (parenting) and to Day 121. What holds it on the dharma side, in the rail's reading: the sentence is not praising a nice personality trait, it is naming `སྙིང་རྗེ` — a term with a precise Buddhist referent and a termbase row — as the thing that replaces the display of wealth, inside a discourse about what actually constitutes worth in a practitioner. Strip the substitution and it becomes advice; keep it and it is a teaching about value. **A reviewer may still call it the other way, and should be given the chance to.**

**⚑ Toh 302 has a prior rejection on file, and it is a different line.** `rejected.md` records *"Disciplined conduct is most supreme. Protect it like your own eyes!"* from this sūtra as a near-miss, on three grounds: the Upāli-and-the-250-precepts frame, the ethical-conduct theme, and admonishing life-at-stake surroundings. **Two of the three reach this line too and are answered here:** (a) the **frame** is monastic — the sūtra is about who counts as a real `dge slong` — but our sentence carries none of it, names no monastic, and the substitution applies to any reader who has ever chosen what to put on; (b) the **register** objection does not hold for this unit, which is warm and constructive rather than admonishing. The **theme** objection does not apply at all — that line was ethics, this one is compassion. Recorded so a reviewer sees the prior rejection was checked, not missed.
