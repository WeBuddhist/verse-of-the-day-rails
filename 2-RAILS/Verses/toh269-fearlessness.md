## Disambiguated Meaning
Grounded in the Degé Tibetan (`bo-toh269.md#^p18`), with 84000 consulted for meaning only:

1. **`རྟག་ཏུ་སེམས་ཅན་ཐམས་ཅད་ལ། །མཉམ་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་སེམས་བྱོས་ལ།`** — "**always make the mind of equality toward all beings.**" `རྟག་ཏུ` is "always," a standing disposition rather than an occasion. `མཉམ་པ་ཉིད` is equality or sameness — here the plain sense of holding everyone level with you.
2. **`སེམས་ཅན་དེ་ལ་མ་བརྙས་དང་།`** — "**and do not look down on those beings.**" `བརྙས་པ` is to disparage, hold in contempt. It is the same instruction stated negatively, which is why the two clauses are one idea and not two.
3. **`ཁྱོད་ལ་འཇིགས་པར་འབྱུང་མི་འགྱུར།`** — "**fear will not arise for you.**" `ཁྱོད` is explicit: the consequence is stated to the reader.

The causal claim is the content: **level regard is what leaves fear nowhere to enter.** Not that a good person deserves not to be afraid, and not that fear is conquered — that holding no one beneath you removes the ground fear stands on.

- **⚑ 84000 EMENDS THE DEGÉ HERE — the fourth confirmed instance, and 84000 discloses it only in a footnote.** Its note n1 reads: *"Reading Yongle, Lithang, Kangxi, Narthang, Choné, and Lhasa `འཇིགས་པ`. Degé reads `འཇིགས་པར`, which breaks from the pattern of similar passages here."* **Degé at `^p18` does read `འཇིགས་པར་འབྱུང་མི་འགྱུར`, and that is what we ground on.** The difference is the terminative particle and the sense is identical, so nothing in the rendering changes — but it joins **Toh 329**, **Toh 313** and the clean check on **Toh 292**, and it confirms the standing rule: for any Kangyur candidate located via 84000's English, read its notes on that passage before grounding.
- **⚑ Rendering choice: `མཉམ་པ་ཉིད` ships as "as your equal," not "equanimity" or "sameness."** The technical readings would import *upekkhā* (Day 18's ground, a different term entirely) or an abstraction. "As your equal" is plain, and it is what the negative clause then confirms.
- **⚑ Rendering choice: `རྟག་ཏུ` is kept as "always."** It is easy to drop for length and it is doing work: the verse asks for a standing disposition, not a response to a particular person. 93 characters with it, so there is no reason to cut it.
- **⚑ Rendering choice: `འཇིགས་པར་འབྱུང་མི་འགྱུར` ships as "fear will not find you," not "you will experience no fear."** The literal form is flat and slightly clinical; "will not find you" keeps the arising sense of `འབྱུང` and is what an English speaker would say. Collocation screen.
- **⚑ THE PAYLOAD MUST STAY ON FEARLESSNESS.** "Equal regard for all beings" is adjacent to two recently used grounds — loving-kindness (Days 103–107) and universal kinship (Day 76) — and a rendering that leads with the kindness will collapse into them. **The verse itself puts the weight on the consequence**, and the card must too: the instruction is the means, the fearlessness is the teaching.
- **⚑ The sūtra's frame is a directional travel-protection rite, and this verse stands free of it.** Toh 269 gives verses for each of the ten directions, most of which either name a specific buddha or are abstract. **This one does neither**, which is why it can ship: it is a self-standing general teaching that happens to sit in a protection text. The addressee is a Śākya youth, Shining Countenance, who asked for protection while travelling — a layman, not a monastic.
- **Two sibling verses in the same sūtra were checked and both FAIL — do not reach for them.** `^p20` couples loving-kindness with "the Victor" (an epithet of the Buddha, so it names him), and `^p21` says "remember that buddha… do not abandon the awakening mind" (names him outright). Both fall foul of the no-naming screen.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **freedom-from-fear**, and it is **not on `theme-checklist.md`'s covered list at all** — a genuinely new theme, which is rare this late in the calendar.

**Dedupe — checked against the English line of all 114 shipped cards.** Nearest are **Day 16** (Dhp 39, *"...beyond good and evil, wakeful: such a one has nothing to fear"*) and **Day 29** (Udānavarga 2 / Dhp 215, *"for one free of desire there is no grief and no fear"*). **Both describe a state already attained** — fearlessness as the property of a purified or desireless mind, which the reader does not yet have, and neither names an action the reader can take today. This card names one, and states the causal link.
