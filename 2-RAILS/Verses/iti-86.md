## Disambiguated Meaning
Four lines, grounded in the Pali source (`pi-itivuttaka.md#^iti86-4-1`ff) and Sujato's aligned English (`en-itivuttaka-sujato.md#^iti86-4-1`ff):

1. **`Caraṁ vā yadi vā tiṭṭhaṁ`** — "whether walking (`caraṁ`) or standing (`tiṭṭhaṁ`)."
2. **`nisinno uda vā sayaṁ`** — "sitting (`nisinno`) or lying down (`sayaṁ`)." Lines 1–2 together are the canonical set of **four postures**, the standard Pali way of saying *in any bodily situation whatever* — it is an idiom for "at all times," not a list of four separate cases.
3. **`Ajjhattaṁ samayaṁ cittaṁ`** — "the mind (`cittaṁ`) inwardly (`ajjhattaṁ`) calmed / settled (`samayaṁ`)." Sujato: "with mind collected inside." The key word is `ajjhattaṁ`: the settling is **internal**, not a matter of arranging one's surroundings.
4. **`santimevādhigacchatī`** — "attains peace (`santi`) itself." The emphatic `eva` in `santimeva` is doing real work: **only** peace, peace and nothing else, is what such a person arrives at. Sujato renders it "they attain only peace."

The verse's move is to detach peace from circumstance. It does not say to sit down, go somewhere quiet, or adopt a posture; it names all four postures precisely so that none of them is the condition. What matters is the one internal thing, and once that is in place the outcome is not partial.

- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) **The four postures must all four ship.** They are an idiom for "in any situation," and dropping one to save characters would turn an idiom into an incomplete list — the opposite of what it means. (b) `ajjhattaṁ` should stay explicitly **inward** ("within," "inside") — this is the word that makes the verse about the reader rather than about their environment, and losing it would let the card read as advice about posture. (c) `samayaṁ` is **settled / calmed**, not "concentrated" — the termbase's *samādhi* row is a different word and this verse does not use it; do not import "concentration" or "meditative stillness" here. (d) `santi` ships as plain **"peace,"** not "Nibbāna" and not "tranquillity"; the verse uses the ordinary word and the card should too. (e) The emphatic `eva` is **not** rendered on the card ("you come to peace," not "you come to peace alone / only peace") — flagged as a small loss taken for naturalness; Sujato keeps it as "only peace" and a reviewer may want it back.
