## Disambiguated Meaning
Grounded in the Chinese source (`zh-digha-agama.md#^pT01p0096b1608`) and the Pali parallel (`pi-digha-nikaya.md#^dn4-22-2`ff; Sujato at `en-digha-nikaya-sujato.md#^dn4-22-2`ff):

1. **`如人洗手，左右相須，左能淨右，右能淨左`** — "as a person washes their hands: left and right **depend on each other** (`相須`), the left can clean the right, the right can clean the left." Pali `hatthena vā hatthaṁ dhoveyya`; Sujato: "It's just like when you clean one hand with the other, or clean one foot with the other." (Quoted in full; note the foot clause, which the Chinese lacks — see the ⚑ divergence flag below.) The image's whole point is that **neither hand can clean itself** — the cleaning only happens between them.
2. **`此亦如是，有慧則有戒，有戒則有慧`** — "so it is here too: where there is wisdom (`慧`) there is ethics (`戒`), and where there is ethics there is wisdom." Pali `Yattha sīlaṁ tattha paññā, yattha paññā tattha sīlaṁ`; Sujato: "Ethics and wisdom always go together." This is a **co-presence** claim: you do not find one without the other.
3. **`戒能淨慧，慧能淨戒`** — "ethics can cleanse wisdom, wisdom can cleanse ethics." Pali `sīlaparidhotā paññā, paññāparidhotaṁ sīlaṁ`; Sujato: "wisdom is cleansed by ethics, and ethics are cleansed by wisdom." A **mutual-purification** claim, stronger than co-presence: each one actively cleans the other.

The teaching answers a live practical question — whether being good and seeing clearly are two separate projects that might trade off against each other. It says they are one project with two hands. Behaving well clarifies your seeing; seeing clearly cleans up your behaviour. In the sutta's frame it arrives as the Buddha's endorsement of a brahmin's own answer to "what makes a true brahmin," stripped of caste down to these two things.

- **⚑ Divergence between the Chinese and the Pali — flagged, not flattened.** The **Pali has two similes**: hand washing hand, *and* foot washing foot (`pādena vā pādaṁ dhoveyya`). The **Chinese has only the hands**, but adds a specification the Pali lacks — `左右相須`, and then each direction spelled out (`左能淨右，右能淨左`). So the Chinese is narrower in imagery and more explicit in reciprocity. **The card ships the Chinese: hands only, both directions named.** Do not import the foot from the Pali; do not drop the left/right specification to match the Pali's brevity.
- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) **`戒` / `sīla` ships as "ethics"** — this is both the `termbase.md` locked rendering (*sīla* = "ethics, moral conduct") and Sujato's own word at `^dn4-22-6`, so the two authorities agree and there is no reason to reach for "virtue," "morality," or "discipline." Noted because "virtue" is the more common English translation elsewhere and a reviewer may expect it. (b) **`淨` ships as "cleansing," not "purifying"** — following Sujato's "cleansed," and because the hand-washing image is domestic and physical; "purify" pulls it toward ritual. (c) **Claims 2 and 3 are compressed into one English clause** ("go together, each cleansing the other") to fit the budget — "go together" is Sujato's own wording for claim 2 at `^dn4-22-2`, and "each cleansing the other" carries claim 3. This is a compression of *wording*, not a cut of *content*: both claims survive. It is nonetheless the card's tightest join and the first thing QA should test. (d) `相須` is **mutual dependence**, not mere adjacency; if the compression in (c) is ever unwound, that word must come back.
