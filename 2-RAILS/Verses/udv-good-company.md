## Disambiguated Meaning
Four lines, grounded in the Tibetan source (`bo-udanavarga.md#^p484`) and the Pali parallel (`pi-dhammapada.md#^207`; Sujato at `en-dhammapada-sujato.md#^207`):

1. **`སྐྱེས་བུ་ཅང་ཤེས་དཀོན་པ་སྟེ`** — "a discerning person (`ཅང་ཤེས`, one who knows/understands) is **rare** (`དཀོན་པ`)."
2. **`དེ་དག་ཀུན་དུ་འབྱུང་མ་ཡིན`** — "they do not arise **everywhere**." A restatement that sharpens line 1: not merely uncommon, but not to be found just anywhere.
3. **`བརྟན་པ་རྣམས་དང་འགྲོགས་པ་དག`** — "keeping company with **the steadfast** (`བརྟན་པ`, firm, stable, reliable — the Pali *dhīra*)."
4. **`གཉེན་དང་ཕྲད་པ་ལྟ་བུར་བདེ`** — "is a happiness (`བདེ`) **like meeting one's kin** (`གཉེན`, relative, family)." Sujato's parallel line: "like meeting with your kin."

The verse's move is from scarcity to value: because such people are rare, finding one is not a pleasant accident but a real good — and the measure it reaches for is family. It says nothing about *becoming* such a person; it is about the gladness of company.

- **⚑ Partial overlap with the Pali parallel — note what this verse does NOT say.** Dhp 207 is a two-part verse: consorting with fools brings long grief and is "like being stuck with your enemy," *then* living with an attentive one is happy "like meeting with your kin." **This Tibetan quatrain contains only the positive half's content**, replacing the fool-clause with the rarity observation (lines 1–2). So the Pali is a sound anchor for the *simile and the claim*, but do not import the fool/enemy contrast into any rendering — it is not in this source. (This is also why the Tibetan is the better card: the Pali verse as a whole opens on the misery of bad company, which would fail the tone screen.)
- **Other rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) `ཅང་ཤེས` is "discerning / understanding" — an ordinary human quality, not a technical attainment; keep it plain, and avoid the termbase's *nipaka* row unless a reviewer prefers it, since this is a different word. (b) `བརྟན་པ` is **steadfast / reliable**, a quality of character, not "the wise" or "the learned"; the verse is about dependability, which is why the family simile fits. (c) `གཉེན` is **kin / family**, and the warmth is the point — "relatives" reads bureaucratic, "your own family" carries it. (d) Lines 1–2 are a pair and both ship; dropping line 2 as redundant would remove the emphasis that makes line 3 land.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **joy-of-good-company** — a fresh tag, and deliberately distinct from **Day 80**'s `spiritual-friendship` (Toh 300), five days earlier: that card is the Buddha's *instruction* to live with a good spiritual friend, this one is the *felt experience* of doing so. Also distinct from Day 1's `friendship` (Dhp 328, *sahāya*, a good travelling companion). Selected on the screens that failed three picks in the Days 80–84 batch: it is **concrete** (the family simile), carries **no culture-specific deity**, and its **opening clause is wistful rather than bleak** — rarity sets up a payoff instead of diagnosing a problem. Canon: **Tibetan**, per the log's standing note that Tibetan is the most under-represented canon (Pali 31 · Chinese 28 · Tibetan 25 at Day 84) and the next picks should favour it. Gate checks: buddhavacana (Udānavarga verse, Buddha-spoken); a complete quatrain, quoted whole; intelligible with no backstory and no glossary term; offers a way through (it points toward something to seek, and names the good of it); not a proclamation of attainment. In scope. Length: bo 109 chars, en 124 — inside budget. Sourcing note: this is the batch's Udānavarga pick; Days 80 and 82 drew on Kangyur sūtras (Toh 300, Toh 327) rather than the Udānavarga, keeping the "don't over-rely on the Udānavarga" guidance satisfied.
