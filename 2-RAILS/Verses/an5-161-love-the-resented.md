## Disambiguated Meaning
Grounded in the Pali source (`pi-anguttara-nikaya.md#^an5-161-1-3`) and Sujato's English at the same anchor:

1. **`Yasmiṁ puggale āghāto jāyetha`** — "in whatever **person** (`puggala`) **resentment** (`āghāta` — a grudge, settled ill will, not a flash of temper) **arises** [in you]."
2. **`mettā tasmiṁ puggale bhāvetabbā`** — "**loving-kindness** (`mettā`) is **to be developed** (`bhāvetabbā`, gerundive) **toward that** person."

The instruction's whole force is in the word *that*. It does not say cultivate kindness in general, or toward beings at large; it points at the specific person who is the source of the trouble and says: them. And it treats the resentment as a given rather than a fault — the sutta's frame is five *methods for getting rid of it*, so the reader is being handed a tool, not told off for having the feeling.

- **⚑ Only the first of five methods ships, and the choice matters.** AN 5.161 lists five: `mettā`, compassion, equanimity, **disregarding the person and paying them no attention**, and reflecting on ownership of deeds. Shipping all five would be the four-parallel-claims shape that got Day 87 rejected. And the fourth method (`asati-amanasikāra`, ignore them) would fail the warmth screen badly on its own — it reads cold. **The first method is both the warmest and the one that stands alone best.**
- **⚑ Cite AN 5.161, never AN 5.162.** The next sutta is a **near-identical list spoken by Sāriputta**, and quoting from it would fail the buddhavacana gate. The two are easy to confuse; the wording differs only slightly.
- **⚑ The second person is Sujato's, not the Pali's — and a reviewer will ask.** The Pali is a **gerundive with a locative** (`mettā tasmiṁ puggale bhāvetabbā` — "loving-kindness is to be developed toward that person"), impersonal in form. Sujato renders it "**you** should develop love," and the card follows him. **This is a translation-grounded choice, not a literal feature of the root text**, and it is what makes the card pass the addressed-to-you screen at all. Recorded here because it is exactly the kind of thing a native reviewer should be able to see rather than have to discover. (The Nikāya scan found the same pattern in two other candidates — Sujato's second person is doing real work across this source.)
- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) `mettā` ships as **"loving-kindness"** — the `termbase.md` row, and explicitly **not** "love," which the termbase forbids for this term. Note Sujato uses "love"; the termbase overrides him here. (b) `āghāta` is a **settled grudge**, stronger than irritation — but the card ships "**stirs resentment in you**," which is deliberately gentler than "whom you hate," because the card should meet a reader in ordinary annoyance as well as real enmity. Flagged as a softening. (c) `bhāvetabbā` is **develop / cultivate**; the card ships "**meet that person with**," which converts an inner cultivation into an outward stance. This is the card's largest liberty and the thing most worth a reviewer's judgement — the alternative, "develop loving-kindness for that person," is more literal but flatter and more clinical. (d) The vocative `bhikkhave` is dropped per standing precedent.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **love-for-someone-you-resent** — a new tag.

**⚑ This is the best answer the canon has yet offered to the open `forgiveness` gap** in `theme-checklist.md`. That gap has been open since **DA 27** was rejected (2026-07-13) for failing the out-of-context test — "out of compassion I accept your repentance" needs the confession scene. **Toh 249** (Day 86) only partly addressed it, being self-directed repair rather than pardoning another. This card needs **no scene at all**: it names an ordinary situation the reader is already in and says what to do. Whether it *closes* the gap is a curator's call — it is about meeting resentment with kindness rather than about pardoning a wrong — but it is the closest fit found in three batches of searching.
