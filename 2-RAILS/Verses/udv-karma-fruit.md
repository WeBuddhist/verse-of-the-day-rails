## Disambiguated Meaning
Grounded in the Tibetan source (`bo-udanavarga.md#^p436`) and the Pali parallel (`pi-anguttara-nikaya.md#^an10-216-6-9`, `#^an10-216-6-10`; Sujato at the same anchors). Four lines, one sentence:

1. **`མི་ཡིས་དགེ་བའམ་སྡིག་པ་ཡི`** — "of a person's wholesome (`དགེ་བ`, *kusala*; termbase: wholesome, skillful) **or** unwholesome (`སྡིག་པ`) …"
2. **`ལས་ནི་གང་དང་གང་བྱས་པའི`** — "… whatever deeds (`ལས`, *karma*) they have done, whichever they may be" — the doubled `གང་དང་གང` is an emphatic "any and every," not a vague "some."
3. **`ལས་རྣམས་ཆུད་ཟར་མི་འགྱུར་ཏེ`** — "those deeds do not go to waste." `ཆུད་ཟ` is "to be lost / squandered / come to nothing."
4. **`དེ་ལྟ་དེ་ལྟར་འབྲས་བུར་འགྱུར`** — "just so, in exactly that way, they become fruit" — the ripening **matches** the deed; the doubled `དེ་ལྟ་དེ་ལྟར` carries that correspondence.

The Pali parallel states the same law in the ownership idiom rather than the waste idiom: beings are the owners of and heirs to their deeds, and "they shall be the heir of whatever deeds they do, whether good or bad" (`en-anguttara-nikaya-sujato.md#^an10-216-6-10`). Both make the wholesome/unwholesome pair explicit, which is why this is a sound meaning anchor.

- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) `ཆུད་ཟར་མི་འགྱུར` is a **negative** — "is not wasted / does not come to nothing" — and should stay negative rather than being turned into a positive "every deed bears fruit"; the consolation of the verse is in the denial of waste. (b) The verse is symmetrical about `དགེ་བ` / `སྡིག་པ` and neither half may be dropped for length; a rendering that keeps only the wholesome half would be a distortion, not a trim. (c) `དེ་ལྟ་དེ་ལྟར` is "just so / in exactly that way," a correspondence between deed and fruit, not merely "eventually." (d) `མི` ("a person / a human") is generic; render it as an impersonal "whatever a person does" rather than a second-person "you," which would sharpen it into a warning the Tibetan does not carry.
