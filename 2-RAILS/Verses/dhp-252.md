## Disambiguated Meaning
Others' faults are easy to see (`sudassaṁ vajjamaññesaṁ`); one's own are hard to see (`attano pana duddasaṁ`) (1-SOURCES/Translations/en-dhammapada-sujato.md#^252). A plain observation that turns the reader's attention from others back to themselves — a gentle self-honesty prompt, not a condemnation.

**⚑ `vajja` = "fault," rendered plainly.** Keep it "faults," not "sins" (too heavy) or "flaws" (fine alternative).

## Theme & Selection Notes
**Theme:** `seeing-your-own-faults`. Related to Day 8 (`self-reflection`, Dhp 50 — don't mind others' deeds, look to your own) and Day 98 (`welcoming-correction`, Dhp 76 — value the one who points out your faults), but distinct: this is the bare, wry observation that the seeing itself is lopsided. Card type: **reframe** (turns an everyday habit — spotting others' flaws — back on the reader).

**⚑ Tone flag for the curator:** offered to Evan (2026-09) noted as *slightly pointed* — a mild "you do this too." Chosen anyway. It lands as recognition (everyone does this), not scolding; a reviewer should keep the renderings wry/light, not accusatory. Do not sharpen it into a rebuke.

**Casual-audience fit ✓:** universally relatable, instant, and it names a habit everyone recognizes in themselves.
