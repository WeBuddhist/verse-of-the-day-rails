## Disambiguated Meaning
Grounded in the Chinese source (`zh-samyukta-agama.md#^pT02p0250b1014`) and the Pali parallel (`^an3-64-4-3`/`4-4`; Sujato at the same anchors):

1. **`汝所知滿足者，我則隨喜`** — "**if what you know is complete, then I rejoice with you.**" `滿足` is complete, sufficient, wanting nothing more. **`隨喜` is the standard Chinese term for *muditā*** — gladness at another's good (termbase: sympathetic joy · 喜 / 隨喜 · དགའ་བ · मुदिता · मुदिता · баясал ⚑). The Buddha does not say "then you are correct"; he says he is *glad for you*, which is a warmer claim.
2. **`不滿足者，我當令汝滿足`** — "**if it is not complete, I will bring you to completeness.**" `當` is the volitive: an undertaking, not a possibility.

Two outcomes, and **neither is failure.** Whatever the listener has, it is met — either with gladness or with help. The teaching is not about knowledge at all; it is about what happens to you when you are found not to know enough.

- **⚑ The Chinese and the Pali order the two clauses oppositely, and the card follows the Chinese.** The Pali puts the incomplete case first ("If you've not learned it fully, I'll fill you in. But if you have…"); the Chinese puts the complete case first. The Chinese order is better for a card: it opens on gladness rather than on deficiency, so the reader is not led in through their own shortfall. Flagged so a reviewer working from Sujato does not read it as an error.
- **⚑ Rendering choice: `隨喜` ships as "I am glad for you," not "I rejoice" or "I approve."** Sujato has "I'll agree," which renders `anumodissāmi` accurately but loses that the Chinese uses the *muditā* term. "Glad for you" keeps the other-directed gladness that is the whole warmth of the line, and avoids "approve," which would make it an examination result. Flagged as a deliberate divergence from Sujato.
- **⚑ Rendering choice: `滿足` ships as "complete," not "enough" or "satisfied."** "Satisfied" would make it about the listener's feelings; "enough" is vague. "Complete" keeps the sense of a thing that can be filled in, which is what the second clause then offers to do.
- **⚑ Rendering choice: keep the first person.** "I am glad… I will help you" — the card is a direct offer, and depersonalising it ("the gap will be filled") destroys it. ⚑ **This puts the card in the same class as Day 115**, where the Buddha speaks in his own voice about what he provides: the word *Buddha* is absent but he is the one speaking. Recorded as a known open line, not a new one.
- **⚑ The scene is excluded and the card does not need it.** Sarabha was a wanderer who had left the Sakyan order and boasted that he knew its teaching and had rejected it; the Buddha put this offer to him three times and he stayed silent each time. **In the text the sentence is not sarcastic** — it is the Buddha's standing offer of fair questioning (`善諫善問`), which is why it works stripped of the scene. But a reviewer who knows the sutta should know the frame is a challenge to a boaster, and judge whether that colours it.

**Shipped-zh policy — modern, not the classical source.** The verbatim CBETA sentence is **the source**; the shipped `zh` is **modern Traditional Chinese**. Substitutions: `汝所知滿足者` → `你所知道的如果已經圓滿`; `我則隨喜` → `我就為你歡喜`; `不滿足者` → `如果還不圓滿`; `我當令汝滿足` → `我就幫你補足`. The classical `者…則…` conditional pair becomes the modern `如果…就…`. **隨喜 is deliberately unpacked to 為你歡喜** rather than kept, because bare 隨喜 in modern Chinese has drifted toward "congratulations" and toward donation contexts; a reviewer may prefer to restore it.
