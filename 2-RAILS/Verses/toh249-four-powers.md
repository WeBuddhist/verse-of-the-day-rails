## Disambiguated Meaning
Grounded in the Tibetan source (`bo-toh249.md#^p120`), with the 84000 English (`en-toh249-84000.md`) consulted as a meaning reference only:

1. **`ཆོས་བཞི་དང་ལྡན་ན་སྡིག་པ་བྱས་ཤིང་བསགས་པ་ཟིལ་གྱིས་ནོན་པར་འགྱུར་རོ`** — "if one possesses four qualities, wrongdoing (`སྡིག་པ`) that has been **done and accumulated** is overpowered (`ཟིལ་གྱིས་ནོན`)." Two things matter here. The verb is *overpowered / overwhelmed*, not "erased" — the harm is outweighed and rendered unable to bear its fruit, which is exactly how the sūtra's closing line puts it (`ཉེས་པར་སྤྱད་པ་འབྲས་བུ་འབྱིན་མི་ནུས་པར་འགྱུར་རོ`, `^p120`). And `བྱས་ཤིང་བསགས་པ` covers both the single act and the accumulated habit, so this is not addressed only to someone with one lapse to fix.
2. **The four, named in order** (`^p120`), each with the sūtra's own definition from the sentences immediately following:
   - **`རྣམ་པར་སུན་འབྱིན་པ་ཀུན་ཏུ་སྤྱོད་པ`** — practising thorough repudiation. Defined in the source as `མི་དགེ་བའི་ལས་སྤྱད་ན་དེ་ལ་འགྱོད་པ་མང་བ་ཡིན་ནོ` — "having done an unwholesome act, to have much **regret** for it."
   - **`གཉེན་པོ་ཀུན་ཏུ་སྤྱོད་པ`** — practising the antidote. Defined as `མི་དགེ་བའི་ལས་བྱས་ནས་ཀྱང་དགེ་བའི་ལས་ལ་ཤིན་ཏུ་བརྩོན་པའོ` — "having done an unwholesome act, to **strive hard at wholesome acts**."
   - **`སོར་ཆུད་པར་བྱེད་པའི་སྟོབས`** — the power of restoration. Defined as `སྡོམ་པ་ཡང་དག་པར་བླངས་པས་མི་ཕྱེད་པའི་སྡོམ་པ་ཐོབ་པའོ` — "by rightly taking on restraint, to gain **unbreakable restraint**," i.e. the settled resolve not to do it again.
   - **`རྟེན་གྱི་སྟོབས`** — the power of support. Defined as `སངས་རྒྱས་དང་། ཆོས་དང་། དགེ་འདུན་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་འགྲོ་བ་དང་། བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་མི་གཏོང་བ` — "**going for refuge** to the Buddha, the Dharma and the Sangha, and not letting go of the awakening mind."

The teaching's move is from despair to a finite procedure. It does not minimise the harm, and it does not offer absolution from outside; it says the harm is *workable*, and names four ordinary moves — feel it, act against it, resolve, and lean on something larger — that together outweigh it.

- **⚑ The central rendering decision, flagged not fixed: the card renders the four *names* with the sūtra's own *definitions*.** The four quoted terms (`རྣམ་པར་སུན་འབྱིན་པ`, `གཉེན་པོ`, `སོར་ཆུད་པར་བྱེད་པའི་སྟོབས`, `རྟེན་གྱི་སྟོབས`) are technical labels. Rendered as bare labels — "thorough repudiation, the antidote, the power of restoration, the power of support" — they would fail `selection-criteria.md` §2's no-glossary accessibility test outright; a Tier 3/4 reader would not know what any of them meant. So the card ships **regret / doing good instead / the resolve not to repeat it / taking refuge**, which are the sūtra's *own* four glosses, quoted above and cited to the same block. **This is grounding, not invention** — the definitions come from four sentences of the same source, four sentences later. But it should be seen for what it is: a substitution of each label by its in-text gloss, and a reviewer may reasonably want the labels restored. If they are, this verse fails §2 and a different card is needed.
- **⚑ Addressee universalised.** The source addresses `བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཆེན་པོ` ("bodhisattvas, great beings"). The card drops that and speaks to the reader ("the harm *you* have done"), per the ecumenical rule to frame Mahāyāna material as universal instruction (`About verse-of-the-day.md` language notes; *bodhisattva* is pan-Buddhist and would have been acceptable, but the epithet chain is not card-length). Note this is a **widening**, not a change of sense — the four powers are taught for anyone in every tradition that uses them.
- **Other rendering choices flagged.** (a) `ཟིལ་གྱིས་ནོན` — prefer **"overcome"** over "erased," "purified," or "washed away"; the sūtra's own logic is that the deed becomes unable to bear fruit, not that it never happened. (b) `རྟེན་གྱི་སྟོབས` is glossed with two parts, refuge *and* not abandoning the awakening mind (`བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས`, termbase: **"the awakening mind," never "Great Vehicle mind"**); the card ships **"taking refuge"** only, because both will not fit — this is an acknowledged partial rendering of the fourth item and is flagged for the reviewer. (c) `སྡིག་པ` as **"harm you have done"** rather than "sin" or "negativity" — "sin" imports a Christian frame, "negativity" is wellness-app vocabulary.
