## Disambiguated Meaning
Four lines, grounded in the Tibetan source (`bo-toh292.md#^p531`), with 84000's English consulted as a meaning reference only:

1. **`མཚན་མོ་བདེ་ལེགས་ཉིན་བདེ་ལེགས`** — "may the **night** be well, may the **day** be well" (`བདེ་ལེགས` = Skt. *svasti*, wellbeing / good fortune / auspiciousness).
2. **`ཉི་མའི་གུང་ལ་བདེ་ལེགས་ཤིང`** — "and may **midday** be well." Lines 1–2 together are a sweep across the whole daily cycle — night, day, the middle of the day — which is the genre's way of saying *at every hour*.
3. **`ཁྱེད་ཅག་ཀུན་དུ་བདེ་ལེགས་ལ`** — "may **you all** (`ཁྱེད་ཅག`) be well **always** (`ཀུན་དུ`)."
4. **`ཁྱེད་ལ་སྡིག་པ་མ་འབྱུང་ཤིག`** — "may `སྡིག་པ` **not arise** for you." See the ambiguity flag.

This is not a teaching. It is a **benediction** — the Buddha wishing wellbeing on a group of lay merchants after they have given a meal to the Sangha. It instructs nothing, asks nothing, and states no doctrine; it simply wishes the hearer well, hour by hour.

- **⚑ THE THING THAT NEEDS A CURATOR'S DECISION: this is a new *kind* of card.** Every one of the first 100 days has been a **teaching** — something to understand or do. This is a blessing received. That may be exactly right for a daily touchpoint (a reader is *given* something rather than asked for something), or it may not be what the product is for. **There is no precedent in `log.md` and this rail cannot settle it.** Flagged for Evan. Note Day 107 (`toh313-may-all-be-happy`) is the same genre — an aspiration rather than an instruction — so the two together are a real test of the form.
- **⚑ Rendering ambiguity, genuinely open: `སྡིག་པ་མ་འབྱུང་ཤིག`.** `སྡིག་པ` normally means *wrongdoing / misdeed*, and `མ་འབྱུང` means *may it not arise*. So the line sits between two readings: **"may no harm befall you"** (misfortune coming *to* you) and **"may you commit no wrongdoing"** (misdeeds arising *from* you). The *svasti*-benediction genre and 84000's reading both support the first, and the card ships it — a blessing wishes good on its hearer rather than warning them off sin, and the surrounding eleven stanzas are all straightforward well-wishes. **But the second reading is grammatically available and a native reviewer must confirm.** If the second is right, the card changes character: it becomes partly an exhortation, and its claim to be a pure blessing weakens.
- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) `བདེ་ལེགས` ships as **"well"** throughout, repeated as the source repeats it — the repetition *is* the stanza's form and must not be varied for English style. "Auspicious" was avoided (archaic and religious-formal), as was "may all go well" (loses the hour-by-hour structure). (b) `ཁྱེད་ཅག` is **plural** ("you all") in the source, since the Buddha addresses a group of merchants; the card ships **singular "you"**, because the card is read by one person. Flagged as a deliberate shift. (c) `ཉི་མའི་གུང` is specifically **midday**, not "noon" (clock-like) and not "afternoon"; the three-part night/day/midday sweep should survive intact.
- **Audience note worth recording.** The addressees are `ཁྱེད་ཅག` — the **lay merchants of Vaiśālī**, householders. That is unusually close to this product's actual audience: not monks, not bodhisattvas, but working laypeople who had just done something generous.
