## Disambiguated Meaning
Grounded in the Pali (`^an7-62-1-1`–`^an7-62-1-2`) and Sujato at the same anchors:

1. **`Mā puññānaṁ bhāyittha`** — "do not **be afraid of** (`bhāyittha`, prohibitive) **`puñña`**." A bare prohibition with no pronoun in the Pali.
2. **`Sukhassetaṁ adhivacanaṁ yadidaṁ puññāni`** — "**`puñña` is an `adhivacana` for `sukha`**" — a *designation*, a synonym: "good" is another name for "happy." Not *leads to*, not *is rewarded with*. The claim is identity.

The move is to dissolve a supposed trade-off. The reader may assume goodness costs something and happiness is elsewhere; the sutta says they are two words for one thing. `adhivacana` is doing the real work and must not be softened into causation.

- **⚑ Containment flag — the sutta continues into the Buddha's own past lives** as Brahmā and Sakka, recounting his own former glory. **None of that enters the card.** The quote is the opening exhortation, stopped at the end of the second sentence, which is permitted. Including any of the continuation would breach both the self-praise gate and the deity-frame screen.
- **⚑ Termbase divergence, deliberate: `puñña` ships as "doing good," not "merit."** `termbase.md` locks *puñña* = "merit, making merit," and that is right for cards where merit is a **noun the reader accumulates** (Day 97, Day 100). Here `puñña` is the **activity**, and "do not be afraid of merit" is not English — it fails the collocation screen Evan set on the `苦`/"bitter" correction. Sujato reaches the same conclusion with "good deeds." **A termbase note should be added recording that *puñña* takes "merit" as a noun and "doing good" as an activity.**
- **Rendering choices flagged.** (a) `adhivacana` ships as **"just another word for"** — the identity claim. Avoid "leads to," "brings," or "results in," all of which reinstate the trade-off the sutta is denying. This is the card's whole point and the easiest thing to lose. (b) The prohibitive `Mā … bhāyittha` ships as **"do not be afraid,"** keeping it a reassurance rather than a command; "never fear" reads archaic. (c) Vocative `bhikkhave` dropped per standing precedent.
