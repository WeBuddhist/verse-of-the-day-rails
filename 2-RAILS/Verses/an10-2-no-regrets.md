## Disambiguated Meaning
Grounded in the Pali source (`pi-anguttara-nikaya.md#^an10-2-1-6`) and Sujato's English at the same anchor:

1. **`Dhammatā esā`** — "this is **the natural way of things** (`dhammatā`)" — i.e. it simply happens, without contrivance. Sujato: "It's only natural."
2. **`avippaṭisārissa`** — "for one **without regret** (`a-vippaṭisāra`, the absence of remorse over what one has done)."
3. **`pāmojjaṁ jāyati`** — "**joy is born** (`pāmojja`, gladness — the ordinary word, not a meditative attainment)."

The teaching's move is to take happiness out of the category of things you pursue and put it in the category of things that follow. It does not instruct the reader to be joyful or to try harder; it says joy is the automatic consequence of a particular condition, and names the condition.

- **⚑ Containment flag — the chain that follows must NOT be included.** AN 10.2 is a causal sequence, and the subsequent links run into technical meditative vocabulary: joy → rapture (`pīti`) → tranquil body → bliss → **immersion** (`samādhi`) → knowledge and vision → disenchantment → **liberation**. Every one of those later links would fail the no-glossary screen. **The card is the first link only**, which stands perfectly alone as a complete sentence with its own anchor.
- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) `dhammatā` ships as **"all by itself"** rather than "it is a natural law" or "it is the nature of things" — the abstract renderings are accurate but turn a warm observation into a doctrinal statement. Sujato's "only natural" is the same instinct. (b) `pāmojja` is ordinary **joy / gladness**, not "rapture" (that is `pīti`, the next link) and not "bliss" — keeping it ordinary is what keeps the card out of meditative-attainment territory. (c) `avippaṭisāra` ships as **"nothing to regret,"** which is the plain form; "free of remorse" reads clinical and "guiltless" imports a moral-verdict frame the Pali does not have. (d) The vocative `bhikkhave` is dropped, per the standing precedent for monastic vocatives.
