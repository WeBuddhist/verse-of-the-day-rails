## Disambiguated Meaning
Four lines, grounded in the Tibetan source (`bo-toh95.md#^p375`):

1. **`ཁྱེད་ཅག་འགྲོ་ནའང་བཀྲ་ཤིས་གྱུར`** — "when you **go**, may there be `བཀྲ་ཤིས`" (auspiciousness, blessing, good fortune).
2. **`ལྡོག་པ་ན་ཡང་བཀྲ་ཤིས་ཤོག`** — "and when you **return**, may there be blessing."
3. **`གཉེན་ལ་ལྟ་བ་བཀྲ་ཤིས་གྱུར`** — "may your **seeing your kin** (`གཉེན`) be blessed."
4. **`གཉེན་གྱིས་བལྟ་བའང་བཀྲ་ཤིས་ཤོག`** — "and their **seeing you**, may that be blessed too."

The stanza covers a journey in four movements — leaving, returning, and both directions of the meeting at the end of it. Line 4 is what makes it more than a travel blessing: it turns the wish around so that the people waiting are blessed in the seeing too. Nothing is asked of the hearer.

- **Context worth knowing (and deliberately not on the card).** This is the Buddha blessing **Trapuṣa and Bhallika**, the two merchants who were his **first lay donors**, immediately after his awakening — they are about to set out on the road again. The card needs none of that: it is a blessing for anyone leaving the house.
- **Rendering choices flagged.** (a) `བཀྲ་ཤིས` ships as **"blessed"** and is repeated four times as the source repeats it — the repetition *is* the form and must not be varied for English style. "Auspicious" was rejected as archaic and faintly astrological; "may all go well" loses the four-part structure. (b) `གཉེན` is **kin / loved ones**, warm, not "relatives" (bureaucratic) — the same choice made on Day 85's card. The card ships **"your loved ones"** to reach a reader whose close people may not be family. (c) `ཁྱེད་ཅག` is **plural** in the source (two merchants); the card ships **singular "you"**, since one person reads it — the same shift flagged on Day 104. (d) The card keeps line 4's reversal explicit ("and their sight of you") rather than compressing lines 3–4 into one mutual clause, because the turn-around is the stanza's best moment.
