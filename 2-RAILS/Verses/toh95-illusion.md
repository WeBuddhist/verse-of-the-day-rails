## Disambiguated Meaning
Grounded on the verbatim Tibetan (`bo-toh95.md#^p426`), read in its chapter context (ch. 26, *Turning the Wheel of Dharma*):

1. **ཇི་ལྟར་སྒྱུ་མ་སྨིག་རྒྱུ་དང་། །རྨི་ལམ་བྲག་ཅ་ཆུ་ཟླ་དང་།** — five stock similes for the empty, insubstantial nature of appearances: **an illusion (སྒྱུ་མ), a mirage (སྨིག་རྒྱུ), a dream (རྨི་ལམ), an echo (བྲག་ཅ), the moon in water (ཆུ་ཟླ)**.
2. **དེ་དག་འདྲ་བའི་འཁོར་ལོ་ནི། །འཇིག་རྟེན་མགོན་པོས་རབ་བསྐོར་ཏོ།** — "the **wheel** that is like those, the **Guardian of the World** (i.e. the Buddha) has fully **set turning**."

The verse's teaching: the very Dharma-wheel the Buddha set turning is, in its own nature, dreamlike and empty. It uses the five classic emptiness-similes to point at the insubstantial nature of appearances (and even of the teaching itself). Buddha-spoken (in verse, in the frame of answering the assembly / Maitreya); Mahāyāna, worded ecumenically.

### ⚑ Two flagged rendering choices (both material — for curator + native review)
- **⚑ The subject is the WHEEL OF DHARMA, not "the world."** In this chapter every parallel verse reads `…འཁོར་ལོ [ཆོས་ཀྱི] …བསྐོར་ཏོ` ("the [Dharma-]wheel was turned"); `ཆོས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ` occurs 9× in the immediate context. So the literal subject is *the Dharma-wheel the Buddha turned*, described as illusion-like — **not** "the world/existence." A rendering like "this whole turning world is like an illusion…" reads beautifully and conveys the general emptiness point, but it **shifts the grammatical referent** (teaching → world) and would be a QA drift finding. The shipped rendering keeps the wheel-of-truth referent. *(If the curator prefers the "turning world" wording despite the shift, it is a one-line change — flagged for an explicit decision.)*
- **⚑ The epithet `འཇིག་རྟེན་མགོན་པོ` ("Guardian of the World" = the Buddha, third-person self-reference) is dropped** from the shipped renderings (curator's call, 2026-11-18: reads grandiose and self-referential). The shipped line is agent-less ("such is the wheel of truth now set turning"), which also sidesteps the "not *about* the Buddha" screen. The verbatim epithet stays in `## Source Text`.
