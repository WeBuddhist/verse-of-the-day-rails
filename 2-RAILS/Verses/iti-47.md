Verbatim, for reference while reading this rail:

> Jāgarantā suṇāthetaṁ, / ye suttā te pabujjhatha; / Suttā jāgaritaṁ seyyo, / natthi jāgarato bhayaṁ.

**Boundary ✓.** The quatrain is the whole verse portion of Iti 47. It opens at
`^iti47-3-1` immediately after the formula `Tatthetaṁ iti vuccati:`
(`^iti47-2-5`) and closes at `^iti47-3-4`. Nothing precedes or follows inside
the verse.

**Buddhavacana gate ✓.** `Etamatthaṁ bhagavā avoca` — "this is what the Blessed
One said" — stands at `^iti47-2-4`, directly before the verse
(1-SOURCES/Text/pi-itivuttaka.md#^iti47-2-4).

**First-person screen ✓.** The verbs are all imperatives and generalisations
addressed to the listener (`suṇātha` "listen," `pabujjhatha` "wake up") plus the
impersonal `natthi ... bhayaṁ` ("there is no fear"). The Buddha does not appear
in the verse in any grammatical person, and the word "Buddha" does not occur.

## Disambiguated Meaning

An address in two moves and then a reassurance. First to those already awake:
listen. Then to those asleep: wake up (`pabujjhatha`)
(1-SOURCES/Translations/en-itivuttaka-sujato.md#^iti47-3-2). Then the claim that
carries the card: being awake is better than sleeping (`suttā jāgaritaṁ seyyo`),
and — the clause that keeps it warm rather than hortatory — **for one who is
awake there is no fear** (`natthi jāgarato bhayaṁ`)
(1-SOURCES/Translations/en-itivuttaka-sujato.md#^iti47-3-4).

**The prose frame confirms "wakeful" is `appamāda`-adjacent, not literal.** The
verse sits after a prose passage (`^iti47-2-1`) describing the `bhikkhu` who
dwells `jāgara` — wakeful, mindful (`sata`), aware (`sampajāna`), composed. So
`jāgara` is spiritual wakefulness / heedfulness, the same family as `appamāda`.
**But the card deliberately keeps the literal surface**: a reader who takes
"wake up" at face value gets a true and usable meaning immediately, and the
deeper sense (come awake to your life, stop drifting) arrives on its own. This
is the opposite of a verse that needs its backstory — here the backstory only
deepens a meaning that already lands.

**⚑ Rendering choice flagged — the two-part address compresses to one.** The
Pali distinguishes "you who are awake, listen" from "you who sleep, wake up."
On a daily card that two-audience split is parse-load for no gain (the reader is
one person, not a crowd), so the shipped line collapses to the single move that
speaks to the reader who needs it: **wake up.** ⚑ This drops `suṇātha`
("listen") and the wakeful-audience clause. It is a real reduction, flagged for
a reviewer — the alternative is to keep both and spend characters on a
distinction the reader does not occupy. The claim and the reassurance
(`seyyo` + `natthi ... bhayaṁ`) are both kept whole.

**⚑ `bhaya` ships as "fear," plainly.** No termbase row exists for it and none is
needed; "fear" is exact and needs no gloss.

## Theme & Selection Notes

**Theme:** `wakefulness`. Related to the vault's `heedfulness` cards (Days 66,
71, 113) but distinct in felt-shape: those are about *diligence / not letting
the practice slip*; this one is about *coming awake* — the relief of it, and
that there is nothing to be afraid of in it. Tag kept as `wakefulness` rather
than `heedfulness` to mark that difference.

**Card type: `encouragement`.** It lowers a bar and removes a fear rather than
issuing an instruction — the imperative "wake up" is an invitation with a
reassurance attached (`natthi ... bhayaṁ`), not a demand. This keeps the type
adjacency legal against Day 130 (`teaching`) and Day 129 (`simile`).

**One-second test ✓ — this is the card that passes the screen the others in the
proposal failed on.** "Wake up. Being awake is better than sleeping, and the
wakeful have nothing to fear." Read once, at speed, it lands: rouse yourself,
it is better, and it is safe. No image to decode, no second clause to invert,
no context required.

**Not bleak ✓.** The only potentially heavy word is `bhaya`, and it appears
*negated* — "nothing to fear" — so the card's affective arc ends in
reassurance, not in the problem.
