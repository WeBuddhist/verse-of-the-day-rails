**⚑ This is the Buddha's ENTIRE utterance, not an extract from one.** The frame immediately before is `བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་ཀྱིས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཆེན་པོ་དྲ་བ་ཅན་གྱི་འོད་ལ་འདི་སྐད་ཅེས་བཀའ་སྩལ་ཏོ།` ("The Blessed One said this to the bodhisattva mahāsattva Jālinīprabha"). The narrative resumes immediately after with `དེ་ནས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཆེན་པོ་དྲ་བ་ཅན་གྱི་འོད་བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་ཀྱི་ཞབས་ལ་མགོ་བོས་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ནས…` (Jālinīprabha bows and sits to one side). **So the two optative sentences are the whole of what he says** — there is nothing before to truncate and nothing after to elide. This is the cleanest contiguity case in the batch, and it is worth noting because the previous candidate for this slot (Toh 152) failed on exactly this point.

**The vocative `དྲ་བ་ཅན་གྱི་འོད` (Jālinīprabha) is dropped**, as on Days 121, 124 and 127, so the blessing lands on the reader. `ཁྱོད` is explicit in **both** clauses, so no second person is being supplied.

## Disambiguated Meaning
Two sentences, grounded in the Tibetan source (`bo-toh101.md#^p30`), 84000's English consulted as a meaning reference only:

1. **`ཁྱོད་ཡུན་རིང་དུ་འཚོ་བར་གྱུར་ཅིག`** — "**may you** (`ཁྱོད` + optative `གྱུར་ཅིག`) **live** (`འཚོ་བ`) **for a long time** (`ཡུན་རིང་དུ`)."
2. **`ཁྱོད་བདེ་བ་དང་ནད་མེད་པར་གྱུར་ཅིག`** — "**may you be** **well / at ease** (`བདེ་བ`) **and free from illness** (`ནད་མེད་པ`)."

It is a benediction the reader **receives**. Nothing is asked of them and nothing is promised to them; the Buddha wishes them long life and health, in the plainest terms available. Its whole function on a card is that a reader who is unwell, exhausted, or frightened about their body is met by the tradition wishing them well rather than instructing them.

- **⚑ `གྱུར་ཅིག` is an optative and the card must keep it a wish, not a promise.** 84000 renders "you shall live long," which in English reads as a prediction or even a guarantee. **That is the one rendering error that would make this card harmful** — a promise of health, made to someone who is ill, that the tradition cannot keep. Every language ships "may you," not "you will."
- **⚑ One idea, or two?** The pair is two optative sentences, which is the axis Day 87 was rejected on and the axis Day 122 was flagged and overruled on. The reading that carries it: this is a **single benediction formula** in its two conventional halves (life, then health) — the same shape as Day 104's "may your night be well, may your day be well," which shipped. Recorded so a reviewer can rule rather than rediscover.
- **⚑ Wellbeing note, and it deserves a reviewer's eye.** The card speaks directly to the unwell, and the risk is that "may you be free from illness" lands badly on a reader with a chronic or terminal condition — as if health were the condition of the blessing. What protects it: it is a **wish**, not an instruction or a promise, and the first half wishes them life rather than recovery. Compare **Day 67** (*"though the body is sick, let the mind not be sick"*), which addresses the same reader from the opposite direction and is the natural companion card. **A reviewer should confirm this reads as tenderness rather than as a well-meant sting.**
- **`བདེ་བ`** is rendered "happy / well" rather than "blissful" or "at ease" — the register is ordinary, and the sentence pairs it with physical health, so the everyday sense is the right one.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **blessing-of-health-and-long-life** — a new tag. Card type: **blessing** (Day 122 was a teaching and Day 124 is a reframe, so no consecutive repeat, and blessing has not run since Day 104).

**Nearest shipped cards, and the honest position on dedupe.** This is **the third generic well-being benediction on the calendar**, after **Day 104** (Toh 292, *"may your night be well, may your day be well… may nothing bad come to you"*) and **Day 109** (Toh 95, *"may your going be blessed and your returning be blessed"*). The pool held it back for that reason and the objection is real: Day 104 in particular already carries "may you always be well." **What is genuinely new is the ground it stands on — longevity and the body.** Day 104 blesses the shape of your day, Day 109 blesses your coming and going; **neither wishes you life or health**, and nothing on the calendar addresses a reader who is ill except **Day 67**, which is about the mind amid sickness rather than a wish for the body. That is the gap this fills, and it is narrow. **Evan selected it with the third-benediction objection stated (2026-08-27).**

**⚑ The supply constraint this card sits inside.** `card-types.md` records that the entire corpus yields roughly **ten to twelve** Buddha-spoken blessings, and that "an all-affirmation calendar is the wellness-app failure mode." Days 104, 109 and now 123 spend three of them. **Part of why this card lands is that it is unexpected against a hundred teachings** — the type should stay rare by design, not by accident.

Selected against the screens on file:
- **Understandable on one read** ✓ — no image, no scene, no term needing a gloss; arguably the plainest card on the calendar.
- **No inference step** ✓ — `ཁྱོད` is in the source, twice.
- **Offers a way through** ✓ — the whole card is warmth received.
- **No first-person Buddha** ✓ (the screen added 2026-10) — grep-clean in every language.
- **No "Buddha" / "Dharma" / "Sangha" / "nirvana" in the card** ✓ — this is what killed Toh 312 and Toh 293 in the same genre.
- **Genre** ✓ — blessings are in scope by Evan's ruling of 2026-08-03; the reader receives, and there is no praise of the Buddha and no self-proclamation.
- Warm ✓ · not sober ✓ · ecumenical ✓ · fresh ✓ — **Toh 101 appears in neither `rejected.md` nor `candidate-pool/tibetan.md`**; it came out of the four-way Kangyur scan run for this batch.
- **⚑ Dharma-not-life-advice — stated plainly, because this is the screen most likely to be used against this card.** "May you live long and be healthy" is also a birthday wish. What distinguishes it: the genre ruling admits benedictions **as** dharma when they are Buddha-spoken and reader-received, which is exactly what Days 104 and 109 established. The card is not offering advice at all, so the "could this sit in a secular book of life advice" test does not bite in the usual way — the question is instead whether a bare well-wish earns a slot. **Evan's answer for this slot is yes.**
