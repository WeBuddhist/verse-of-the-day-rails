## Disambiguated Meaning
Grounded in the Pali source (`pi-dhammapada.md#^76`) and Sujato's English at the same anchor (`en-dhammapada-sujato.md#^76`):

1. **`Nidhīnaṁva pavattāraṁ`** — "as one who **points out buried treasure** (`nidhi` = a hidden hoard; `pavattāra` = one who declares or reveals it)." The simile comes **first** in the Pali, before what it is a simile for.
2. **`yaṁ passe vajjadassinaṁ`** — "so should one regard the one who **sees your faults** (`vajjadassin`)."
3. **`Niggayhavādiṁ medhāviṁ`** — "the **one who reproves** (`niggayhavādin`, who speaks correctively), the **discerning** one (`medhāvin`)."
4. **`tādisaṁ paṇḍitaṁ bhaje`** — "**keep company with** (`bhaje`) such a wise person."

The verse's move is a straight substitution of one feeling for another. The person who tells you what you got wrong normally registers as a threat; the verse says treat them the way you would treat someone who has just told you where money is buried. It does not ask the reader to enjoy criticism, only to recognise what they are being handed.

- **⚑ Why the closing couplet is dropped.** `Tādisaṁ bhajamānassa, seyyo hoti na pāpiyo` ("for one who keeps such company, things get better, not worse") is a **restatement of the benefit**, not new content, and including it would push the card past the character ceiling. Dropping it costs nothing doctrinally. It also keeps the card from arguing its case — the simile does the work, and a reader does not need to be told the outcome.
- **⚑ This is the batch's only simile, and it survives the self-applying screen for a specific reason.** Three vivid Kangyur cards were rejected because the reader had to work out what the image referred to. Here the quote **states both halves inside itself** — the treasure-guide *and* the fault-shower — so nothing has to be decoded. The rule this establishes: **a simile is safe when the quote names its own referent, and unsafe when the referent lives in the surrounding text.**
- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) English **reverses the source's order**, putting the plain statement before the simile ("keep company with one who… as you would keep a guide to treasure"). The Pali leads with the simile; a literal order in English reads as a riddle, which is the shape that got Toh 329's other verse rejected. Flagged as a real departure from the source's rhetoric, taken deliberately. (b) `vajjadassin` is **"one who points out your faults,"** not "critic" (too modern and hostile) and not "one who finds fault" (which implies fault-finding as a habit — the opposite of the verse's meaning). (c) `niggayhavādin` and `medhāvin` are **compressed into one phrase** ("one wise enough to point out your faults") rather than shipped as two separate epithets — a wording compression, not a content cut, and the flag is here so a reviewer can test it. (d) `nidhi` is **buried or hidden treasure**, a real hoard in the ground, not "a gift" or "riches"; the image depends on the treasure being *concealed* — which is what makes the fault-shower's information valuable. (e) `bhaje` is **keep company with / stay close to**, an ongoing association, not a one-off "listen to."
