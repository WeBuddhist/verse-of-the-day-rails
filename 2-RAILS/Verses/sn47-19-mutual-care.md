## Disambiguated Meaning
Grounded in the Pali (`^sn47-19-2-4`) and Sujato at the same anchor:

1. **`attānaṁ rakkhanto`** — "**guarding / looking after oneself**." `rakkhati` is to guard, protect, watch over. Not self-indulgence and not self-improvement: keeping watch over yourself.
2. **`paraṁ rakkhati`** — "**one guards another**." `para` is the other person, generically.
3. The sentence then **reverses the same clause**, so the two directions are stated as producing each other rather than as a sequence or a trade.

The teaching is that self-care and care of others are **not a zero-sum pair**. It does not rank them, does not say to do one first, and does not make either instrumental to the other. That symmetry is the entire content, which is why the rendering keeps the mirrored clause structure exactly.

- **⚑ THE PALI IS IMPERSONAL AND THE SECOND PERSON IS SUJATO'S. Disclose on any reuse.** `rakkhanto … rakkhati` is a present participle plus a third-person singular verb, with no pronoun of any person anywhere. Sujato supplies "you" twice. This is `log.md`'s standing Nikāya finding in its original form — **Sujato's translation choice is what makes this card pass the self-applying screen at all**, exactly as recorded for Day 103, AN 5.34 and MN 96. It is a weaker case than **Day 114** (Snp 2.4), where even Sujato was impersonal and the second person was ours; here we are following the authoritative translation, not going beyond it. A native reviewer should still be told.
- **⚑ Rendering choice: ship Sujato's wording, with one cut.** The card drops his "and" before the second clause (`others; **and** looking after others`) so the two halves sit in bare parallel, which is what the Pali does with no connective. Nothing else is changed. Where a translation is already plain, modern, correctly balanced and CC0, re-wording it for the sake of having our own phrasing would be churn — `translation-qa` rule 2, "don't over-correct."
- **⚑ The sutta's frame is satipaṭṭhāna, and it stays off the card.** Two sentences earlier the Buddha says *"Thinking 'I'll look after myself,' you should cultivate mindfulness meditation"* (`^sn47-19-2-2`/`2-3`), and the sentences after `^sn47-19-2-4` spell out that the mechanism is meditation practice, patience and harmlessness (`^sn47-19-3-1` onward). **None of that is on the card**, and it must not be added — the shipped sentence is complete and intelligible without it. But the rail records it because it is what answers the "dharma, not general life advice" question: in context this is a statement about *practice*, not about work-life balance.
- **⚑ The acrobat story is not needed and is not on the card.** The sutta opens with the acrobat Medakathālikā and her tutor balancing on a bamboo pole, and her line *"You look after yourself, and I shall look after myself"* (`^sn47-19-1-11`) is the setup the Buddha then corrects. The card ships the Buddha's own sentence, not hers, and it stands alone.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **mutual-care**, new tag.

**Dedupe — checked against the English line of every one of the 111 shipped cards, not just against `source_ref`.** Nearest is **Day 49** (Udānavarga / Dhp 160, *"You are your own refuge, who else could be?"*), which is **self-reliance as sufficiency** — nobody else can do it for you. This card is the opposite move: the two directions are not in competition at all. Also checked: **Day 69** (Ud 5.1 — since others hold themselves dear, harm no one; a *restraint* derived from self-regard) and **Day 70** (EĀ, "whoever tends the sick tends me" — care of others framed as devotion). **No shipped card says self-care and other-care produce each other.**

⚑ **Note the Day 49 caution transfers here in reverse and is worth a reviewer's eye.** `log.md` flags Day 49 as possibly falling foul of the "self-reliance that works by excluding others" screen (the DĀ 6 rejection). This card is the natural antidote to that concern and could reasonably be read as the vault's correction to it.

**Gate checks.** *Buddhavacana* — `^sn47-19-2-1` reads "That's the way," said the Buddha, and everything from there is his speech; the vocative `bhikkhave` sits inside the quoted sentence. Not the acrobat's words. *In scope* — Saṁyutta Nikāya. *Traceable* — `^sn47-19-2-4`. *License* — SuttaCentral/Sujato **CC0**. *Real quote, kept whole* — one complete sentence, vocative dropped. *Length* — en 93. *Fresh* — SN 47.19 has never been used, and the Sedaka Sutta is little quoted outside monastic contexts.

**Screens.** *Arrives already applied* ✓ (via Sujato's second person — see the flag). *Lands on one read* ✓ — no scene, no term, no frame; the acrobat story is not needed. *One idea in five words* ✓ — "caring for one is caring for both." *Warm* ✓ — it relieves a guilt rather than issuing a correction. *Dharma, not general life advice* — ⚑ **the screen to watch.** Stripped of its frame the sentence could sit in a wellness book. What holds it: the Buddha's own gloss two sentences earlier makes the mechanism *satipaṭṭhāna*, and the sutta's continuation makes it patience and harmlessness. Recorded so a reviewer can disagree knowingly. *No "Buddha" in the card* ✓. *Not sober* ✓. *Not a proclamation of attainment* ✓.

**⚑ Batch spacing — the reason this card is Day 117 and not Day 119.** Day 119 (SĀ 1222) turns on the same verb family: `rakkhā` / `自護`, guarding oneself. The two are genuinely adjacent ground — *what it actually means to look after yourself* — and were deliberately separated by Day 118 rather than run together. The angles differ (this card: self-care and other-care are one act; Day 119: the guard belongs inside, not outside), and both rails say so. Flagged rather than hidden.
