## Disambiguated Meaning
Four lines, grounded in the Pali (`pi-dhammapada.md#^382`) and Sujato (`en-dhammapada-sujato.md#^382`):

1. **`Yo have daharo bhikkhu`** — "whoever is a **young** (*daharo*) mendicant (*bhikkhu*)." *Daharo* is genuinely "young / junior," and carries the verse's point: newness is no obstacle.
2. **`yuñjati buddhasāsane`** — "**applies / yokes** himself to the Buddha's teaching" (*yuñjati*, from the same root as "yoke"). Active engagement, not mere belief.
3. **`Somaṁ lokaṁ pabhāseti`** — "he **lights up** this world" (*pabhāseti*, to illuminate, make shine).
4. **`abbhā muttova candimā`** — "**like the moon freed from clouds**" (*abbhā*, cloud; *mutta*, released).

The simile does the work and deserves care: the moon is not made brighter by escaping the clouds — it was always shining, and what changes is that nothing is now in the way. Read that way, the verse is not "practise hard and you will become impressive"; it is "apply yourself and what was always there becomes visible." That reading is what makes it an encouragement rather than a performance standard, and it is the reading the renderings should support.

- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) **`bhikkhu`** — the vault's practice on lay-facing cards is to render this **inclusively** (a practitioner, one who practises) rather than "monk," so the card speaks to our lay audience; precedent on Day 74 (`ud3-4-mountain`, where *bhikkhu* became "the wise one" with a reviewer flag). A reviewer may prefer the literal "monk" restored — flagged, not decided. (b) **`daharo` ("young") should be kept**, not dropped for length: it is the encouragement in the verse, not decoration. Dropping it would make the verse blander and slightly change what it says. (c) *yuñjati* is active application — "devoted to" is acceptable but slightly passive; "applies himself to" is closer. (d) The simile is `abbhā muttova candimā` — **the moon freed from clouds**, not "the moon shining brightly." Keep the *release* in it; that is where the meaning sits.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **radiance-of-practice** — a fresh tag. Adjacent to Day 50's `genuine-cultivation` and Day 71's `confidence`, but distinct: this is about what practice *does*, imaged rather than argued. Chosen for this batch to replace Udānavarga 26 ("there is the unborn, the unbecome, the unmade, the unconditioned"), which Evan rejected as too abstract for a daily card (2026-09) — this one is a single concrete picture anyone can hold, which is exactly the property that was missing.
