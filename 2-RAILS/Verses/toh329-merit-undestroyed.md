## Disambiguated Meaning
Four lines, grounded in the Tibetan source (`bo-toh329.md#^p518`), with 84000's English consulted as a meaning reference only:

1. **`རླུང་དམར་གྱིས་ཀྱང་མི་ཤིགས་ཤིང`** — "not **broken / destroyed** (`མི་ཤིགས`) even by the **wind** (`རླུང་དམར`, literally the red wind — the violent, destructive wind)."
2. **`མེས་ཀྱང་མི་འཚིག་བསོད་ནམས་ཡིན`** — "not **burned** (`མི་འཚིག`) even by fire — that is **merit** (`བསོད་ནམས`)."
3. **`ས་རྣམས་ཁེབས་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཡི`** — "by the water that **covers all the lands** (`ས་རྣམས་ཁེབས་པར་བྱེད་པ`)."
4. **`ཆུས་ཀྱང་མི་འདྲུལ་བསོད་ནམས་སོ`** — "not **rotted / decayed** (`མི་འདྲུལ`) even by that water — that is merit."

The verse names the three forces that destroy everything a person can accumulate — storm, fire, flood — and says merit is the exception. Its force is reassurance rather than instruction: it does not tell the reader to make merit, it tells them that what they have already done is not at risk. `བསོད་ནམས` is stated **twice**, once in each half, which is the source's own way of keeping the subject in view rather than saving it for a reveal.

- **⚑ Do NOT reach for the neighbouring stanza, and this is a real textual trap.** The adjacent "kings and thieves cannot plunder it" verse looks like an even better card in 84000's English. **84000's own note [n12] records that it emended the Degé there**, changing `ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས` ("ethical discipline") to "merit," following the Sanskrit and a Dunhuang witness. **The Degé we ground on does not say merit in that stanza.** It also straddles the `^p517` page break mid-word. The wind/fire/water stanza above reads `བསོད་ནམས` in the Degé itself, twice, unemended — which is why it is the one to use.
- **⚑ Subject-first ordering is deliberate, and it is what makes this card work.** The Tibetan withholds nothing (it states `བསོད་ནམས` in line 2), but a literal English line-order would read as a riddle — *"not broken by wind, not burned by fire… that is merit"* — and **Toh 329's other verse was rejected on exactly that ground** ("Nicer, but still doesn't have context," Evan, on the `^p516` couplet). So the card **names merit first**: *"Merit is not broken by wind…"* A riddle-order variant exists and is faithful, but it must not ship.
- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) `བསོད་ནམས` ships as **"merit"** — the `termbase.md` *puñña* row (福德 · `བསོད་ནམས` · पुण्य · पुण्य · буян). This is the one term the reader must already hold; it is ordinary vocabulary for a cultural Buddhist and is not glossed on the card. (b) `རླུང་དམར` is literally "red wind" — a destructive storm-wind, **not** a colour and **not** the medical/energetic `རླུང` of Tibetan medicine. Ships as plain "wind"; do not import the colour, which would read as exotic. (c) `མི་འདྲུལ` is **rot / decay**, the specific damage water does, not "wash away" — the three verbs are three different kinds of destruction and should stay distinct. (d) `ས་རྣམས་ཁེབས་པར་བྱེད་པ` is a **flood covering the whole earth**, kept at that scale; shrinking it to "rain" or "a river" loses the point that this is the largest destructive force named.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **merit-cannot-be-lost** — a new tag. Adjacent to `karma-and-its-fruit` (Days 57, 68, 79) but the claim is different: not *your actions bear fruit*, but *the good you have done cannot be destroyed*. Reassurance about durability rather than a statement of causation.

Selected against all the screens on file. On the two that are hardest:
- **No inference step** — this is the screen that killed Dhp 228 in the same proposal. This card passes, but by the narrowest margin of the five in this batch: it is a claim about **merit** rather than grammatically about *you*. It works because the reader's own merit is the obvious referent and no derivation is needed — but a reviewer should sanity-check that. **This is the card in the batch most worth re-reading cold.**
- **Public-facing** — nothing here would put off a newcomer; it is purely reassuring, and it makes no demand.
- Self-applying ✓ · one idea (*your good cannot be destroyed*) ✓ · warm ✓ · not sober ✓ · no deity frame ✓ · not a proclamation of attainment ✓.

Gate checks: **buddhavacana** — the Buddha answering a devatā's question at Jetavana; `བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་ཀྱིས་བཀའ་སྩལ་པ།` immediately precedes the verse. The devatā's question and a closing line of appreciation are the only non-Buddha speech in the sūtra and both fall outside the quote. **In scope** — Kangyur mdo. **Traceable** — `bo-toh329.md#^p518`. **License-cleared** — `vault-annex.md` corpus row 14 (PD Degé; 84000 English reference-only per §7). **Real quote, kept whole** — one complete verse, nothing stitched, no page straddle. **Intelligible out of context** ✓. **Length** — en 101 chars. **Fresh** — Toh 329 has never been used.
