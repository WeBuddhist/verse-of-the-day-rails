## Disambiguated Meaning
Four lines, grounded in the Tibetan source (`bo-udanavarga.md#^p437`) and the Pali parallel (`pi-sutta-nipata.md#^snp1-10-7-1`ff; Sujato at `en-sutta-nipata-sujato.md#^snp1-10-7-1`ff):

1. **`འདི་ན་སྐྱེས་བུའི་ནོར་མཆོག་དད་པ་སྟེ`** — "**here** (`འདི་ན`, in this world / this life), a person's finest **wealth** (`ནོར་མཆོག`) is faith (`དད་པ`)." Pali `Saddhīdha vittaṁ purisassa seṭṭhaṁ`; Sujato: "Faith here is a person's best wealth."
2. **`འདི་ན་ཆོས་ལ་ལེགས་སྤྱོད་བདེ་བ་འཐོབ`** — "here, **practising the teaching well** (`ཆོས་ལ་ལེགས་སྤྱོད`) obtains happiness (`བདེ་བ`)." Pali `Dhammo suciṇṇo sukhamāvahāti`; Sujato: "the teaching brings happiness when practiced well." Note the Pali `suciṇṇo` ("well practised") and the Tibetan `ལེགས་སྤྱོད` agree exactly: the happiness is conditional on the *quality* of the practice, not on merely having the teaching.
3. **`བདེན་པ་ཁོ་ན་རོ་བྲོ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་ཕུལ`** — "**truth alone** (`བདེན་པ་ཁོ་ན`) is the finest (`ཕུལ`) of tastes (`རོ་བྲོ`)." Pali `Saccaṁ have sādutaraṁ rasānaṁ`; Sujato: "Truth is the sweetest taste of all."
4. **`ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱིས་འཚོ་འཚོ་བའི་མཆོག་ཅེས་གསུངས`** — "living by **wisdom** (`ཤེས་རབ`) is said to be the finest living." Pali `Paññājīviṁ jīvitamāhu seṭṭhaṁ`; Sujato: "The one they say has the best life lives by wisdom."

The verse is a four-part answer to "what is worth most?" — and every answer replaces a material or sensory good with a quality of the person: wealth becomes faith, pleasure becomes well-practised teaching, taste becomes truth, a good living becomes a wise one. In the Pali frame it is the Buddha's reply to a direct question (the yakkha Āḷavaka asks what a person's best wealth is), which is why it reads as a list of answers rather than an exhortation.

- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) **`དད་པ` / `saddhā`.** Sujato ships "faith"; the vault's Day 71 card tagged this same doctrinal area `confidence`. `termbase.md` has **no row for this term** — one is being added with `faith, confidence` as the English and `信心 / དད་པ / श्रद्धा / श्रद्धा / итгэл`. Both English words are defensible: "faith" is the standard and matches Sujato, "confidence" avoids the theistic connotation English "faith" can carry for a secular-leaning reader. The card ships **"faith"**; a reviewer may prefer "confidence" and the termbase row permits either. (b) **`ལེགས` ("well") is lost in the shipped English** — "practising the teaching brings joy" drops the *well*, which both the Tibetan and the Pali carry, purely for length. This is the card's one real loss and is flagged for the reviewer; a longer rendering ("the teaching practised well brings joy") restores it at 126 chars, right at the ceiling. (c) **`ཁོ་ན` ("alone / only") in line 3 is also dropped** for length — the source says truth *alone* is the sweetest taste, an exclusive claim; the card's "truth is the sweetest taste" is inclusive-sounding. Second flagged loss. (d) `རོ་བྲོ` is literal **taste/flavour** and the sensory image should survive translation, not be abstracted to "truth is most satisfying." (e) `མཆོག` / `ཕུལ` / `seṭṭha` all mean *finest, supreme*; the English repeats "best/finest" twice by design, mirroring the source's own repetition, and should not be varied for style.
- **⚑ Note on the frame, so it is not imported.** The Pali parallel sits inside a Q&A with a yakkha (Āḷavaka) and is preceded by his question `Kiṁ sūdha vittaṁ purisassa seṭṭhaṁ`. The Tibetan Udānavarga verse stands free of any such frame, and the card ships it free. Do **not** add "the Buddha said" or a questioner into any rendering.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **confidence** — recurring, last run **Day 71** (2026-09-02, `udv-recollection`, the Udānavarga ch.15 recollection-of-the-Buddha verse). Sixteen days' distance, and a genuinely different angle: Day 71 is *what to bring to mind*; this is *what is worth most*. `theme-checklist.md` lists refuge/confidence as a gap with Udānavarga ch.10 and ch.15 as the two candidates — ch.15 was claimed on Day 71, **this claims ch.10**, closing the pair.

Selected against the batch's screens: **concrete** (wealth, taste, a way of living — three of the four images are physical), **no culture-specific deity**, and the **opening clause is affirming rather than bleak**. It is also unusually *simple*: four short declaratives, no subordination, which makes it one of the easier cards in the corpus to render into six languages without structural drift.

**⚑ Substitution note — this verse replaced a rejected pick.** Day 87 was first proposed as **Udānavarga 5 / Dhp 212** ("grief is born of what we hold dear… let go and neither grief nor fear remains"). Evan rejected it on the **anti-love misreading risk**: on a standalone card with no room to distinguish *piya* (clinging attachment) from ordinary affection, the surface reading is "do not love anyone." That verse is now in `rejected.md` with the reason. **The generalisable screen:** a verse whose *surface* reading tells the reader to withdraw affection fails for this audience even when the doctrine is exact and the verse resolves its own tension. This is a distinct failure mode from the existing accessibility and deity-frame gates, and worth checking explicitly before proposing.

**⚑ Consequence for the calendar: the sober-tone debt is NOT paid and carries forward.** Udānavarga 5 was this batch's sober card. With it removed, Days 86–90 are uplifting or steadying end to end, extending the run the log flagged at Day 85 to **eleven consecutive non-sober cards**. Recorded in `log.md`'s running balance; the Days 91–95 batch should pay it.
