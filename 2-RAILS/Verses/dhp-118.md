## Disambiguated Meaning
Four lines, grounded in the Pali (`pi-dhammapada.md#^118`) and Sujato (`en-dhammapada-sujato.md#^118`):

1. **`Puññañce puriso kayirā`** — "if a person does *puñña*" — merit, goodness, a wholesome act (termbase: merit, making merit · 福德 / 作福 · བསོད་ནམས · पुण्य).
2. **`kayirā naṁ punappunaṁ`** — "let them do it **again and again**." The verb repeats; the doubling `punappunaṁ` is the line's whole force.
3. **`Tamhi chandaṁ kayirātha`** — "let them make *chanda* toward it" — wish, inclination, a leaning-toward. Sujato: "set your heart on it." Not duty; wanting to.
4. **`sukho puññassa uccayo`** — "the *uccayo* (piling up, accumulation) of merit is *sukha* (happy, joyful)." Sujato: "for piling up goodness is joyful."

The verse's logic is worth naming because it is easy to lose: the joy is located in the **accumulating**, not in a future reward for it. That is what makes this an encouragement rather than a transaction — the practice pays as you go.

- **Rendering choices flagged (not fixed here).** (a) *puñña* must take each language's **standard** term (merit / goodness), not a vague "nice things" — but note the register here is warm and everyday, so a heavily technical word for merit would misfire. Sujato's "goodness" threads this well. (b) *chanda* is **desire in its wholesome sense** — inclination, gladness toward. Do not render it with the word used for *taṇhā*/craving, which would invert the verse. "Set your heart on it" is the model. (c) `sukho … uccayo` predicates joy of the *accumulation itself*; a rendering like "you will be happy later" relocates the joy and weakens it. (d) *puriso* ("a person") is generic — keep it impersonal or second-person, either is faithful, but don't narrow it to a monastic.

## Theme & Selection Notes
Theme: **joy-in-doing-good** — a fresh tag. Related to Day 28's `small-good-deeds` (T210) and Day 22's `wholesome-action`, but the angle is distinct and it is what earns the card: those say *do good*, this says *doing good is a pleasure and it compounds*. Selected specifically on the tone rule (`selection-criteria.md` §2/§3, and Evan's standing "lead uplifting" note): the verse contains no threat, no death, no diagnosis of a problem, nothing to be warned about — it is encouragement end to end, which is rare enough in the corpus to be worth using. Meets `discouraged` directly. Gate checks: buddhavacana (Dhammapada); complete four-line verse, quoted whole; intelligible with no backstory and no glossary term; not a proclamation of attainment. Freshness: Dhp 118 is not among the over-exposed greatest hits and is not in `log.md` or `previously-used.md`. Length: en 104 chars. License: Sujato CC0.
